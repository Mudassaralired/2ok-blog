DIY acrylic pour wall art turns a few bottles of paint into a marbled canvas that looks bought from a gallery. You thin acrylic paint with pouring medium, layer the colours in a cup, tip them onto a canvas and tilt — the paint does the painting, and no two pours ever come out the same. Each of the 15 projects below carries a real cost, a drying time and a difficulty rating, plus the paint ratios, sealing rules and hanging advice that single-project tutorials leave out.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every project here costs £6 to £30, and most take under one hour of hands-on work.
> - Mix one part paint to two parts pouring medium, and thin it until it flows like warm honey.
> - Add two to three drops of silicone oil per colour for cells, and torch lightly to pop bubbles.
> - Let every pour dry flat for 24 to 48 hours before moving or sealing it.
> - Seal with two coats of high-gloss varnish, or resin for a glass-like finish.

## Classic Dirty Pour and Marble Acrylic Pour Art

### 1. Classic dirty pour in terracotta and cream

Layer terracotta, cream, sage and gold in one cup, pour in slow ribbons across a 40 × 50 cm canvas and tilt gently until covered. Cost: £8 to £14. Time: 45 minutes plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: easy.

### 2. Liquid gold marble moment

Coat a 40 × 50 cm canvas in white, pour grey, black and beige in diagonal bands, and stretch them with a slow tilt. Cost: £10 to £16. Time: 1 hour plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: easy.

### 3. Blush cloud dream pour

Mix blush pink, peach and coral over a cream base on a 50 × 60 cm canvas, drizzle the colours freely, and tilt in every direction until they melt together. Cost: £9 to £15. Time: 45 minutes plus 24 hours drying. Difficulty: easy.

### 4. Retro psychedelic swirl

Pour lines of red, yellow, blue and white straight onto a 40 × 40 cm canvas, then drag a skewer through them in slow S-curves to twist the colours into ribbons. Cost: £7 to £12. Time: 45 minutes plus 24 hours drying. Difficulty: easy.

## Galaxy and Ocean Acrylic Pour Art

### 5. Cosmic galaxy ring pour

Pour rings of deep purple, teal and magenta from the centre of a round 40 cm canvas outward, then push the paint into a vortex with a hair dryer on low. Cost: £9 to £15. Time: 1 hour plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: moderate.

### 6. Ocean wave pour with foam cells

Mix three blues and white on a 40 × 50 cm canvas, pour in curved lines, and swirl a skewer through the paint in circles to suggest currents. Cost: £8 to £13. Time: 45 minutes plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: easy.

### 7. Sunset gradient pour

Blend orange, coral and deep pink across a 40 × 50 cm canvas in horizontal bands, then tilt slowly so the colours bleed into each other like a fading sky. Cost: £8 to £13. Time: 45 minutes plus 24 hours drying. Difficulty: easy.

### 8. Forest green and gold wood slice pour

Seal a sanded 30 cm wood slice with clear gesso, then pour forest green, gold and cream lightly across the top half and let the natural wood show through below. Cost: £10 to £16. Time: 1 hour plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: moderate.

## Gold Geode and Statement Acrylic Pour Art

### 9. Emerald flow triptych

Line up three 30 × 40 cm canvases edge to edge, then pour emerald green, white, black and gold across all three at once so the design flows from panel to panel. Cost: £16 to £25. Time: 1 hour plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: moderate. The [gallery wall layouts](/blog/gallery-wall-layouts) guide shows how to space a three-panel set evenly.

### 10. Neutral geode luxe round

Pour warm taupe, cream and white in curved, layered sections across a round 45 cm wood panel sealed with gesso, then edge the shapes with gold leaf. Cost: £12 to £20. Time: 1 hour plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: moderate.

### 11. Metallic feather swipe

Pour horizontal lines of grey, white and metallic copper onto a 45 × 60 cm canvas with a black base, then swipe once with a damp paper towel in a single smooth motion. Cost: £10 to £16. Time: 1 hour plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: moderate.

### 12. Blush gold liquid split diptych

Place two 25 × 25 cm canvases tightly side by side, base coat them white, then dirty-pour blush pink, metallic gold and beige across both at once. Cost: £9 to £15. Time: 1 hour plus 48 hours drying. Difficulty: easy.

## Small Budget and Gift Acrylic Pour Art

### 13. Mini canvas pour trio

Pour three 15 × 15 cm canvases in one shared palette — terracotta, cream and sage — using leftover paint from a bigger project. Cost: £6 to £9. Time: 30 minutes plus 24 hours drying. Difficulty: easy. The [DIY canvas wall art](/blog/diy-canvas-wall-art) guide has more small-canvas ideas in the same sizes.

### 14. Coaster and tile pour set

Pour onto four 10 cm ceramic tiles instead of canvas, using the same dirty-pour cup and a 1 to 2 paint-to-medium mix. Cost: £6 to £10. Time: 30 minutes plus 24 hours drying. Difficulty: easy. Seal with heat-resistant varnish and stick cork pads on the backs.

### 15. Christmas ornament pour baubles

Pour thinned paint over clear glass baubles, swirl it inside, and tip out the excess so a marbled coat dries on the inner wall. Cost: £7 to £12. Time: 30 minutes plus 24 hours drying. Difficulty: easy.

## Choosing Paints Medium and Canvas

Start with craft acrylics: about £1 to £2 per 120 ml bottle, they pour well when thinned and keep a first project cheap. Fluid acrylics cost about £4 to £6 per bottle, but need less medium and give glossier colour. Heavy-body acrylics need the most medium and stirring, so save them for later pours.

Pouring medium is what makes acrylic pour at all. Floetrol is the usual choice, about £8 to £12 per litre, and it keeps colours bright while the paint levels flat. PVA glue thinned with water is the £3 budget stand-in, but it can dull colours and crack thick pours, so use it for practice only. Silicone oil, about £5 a bottle, creates cells: two to three drops per colour is enough.

A stretched canvas is cheapest and lightest, about £5 for a 40 × 50 cm size, but it can sag under a heavy pour, so support it from below while it dries. A cradled wood panel costs £8 to £12, stays flat, and takes resin well. Canvas boards cost about £2 each but warp when wet, so keep them for minis.

## Pour Techniques Compared

Six techniques cover almost every acrylic pour. Start with the dirty pour or the flip cup, which forgive wobbly hands, and save the Dutch pour — the hardest — for a small practice canvas.

| Technique | How it works | Best for | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dirty pour | Layer colours in one cup, then pour | Marbled abstracts | Easy |
| Flip cup | Flip a layered cup onto the canvas, then lift | Blooms and bursts | Easy |
| Swipe | Drag a palette knife or card through wet paint | Feathers and flames | Moderate |
| Dutch pour | Blow thinned paint across the canvas with air | Wispy florals | Moderate |
| Ring pour | Pour in slow concentric rings from the centre | Galaxies and geodes | Easy |
| Open cup pour | Pour straight from the cup in lines | Stripes and waves | Easy |

## Paint to Medium Ratios

The ratio decides whether a pour flows or cracks. Aim for warm honey: lifted paint should fall in a thin ribbon that vanishes within a second or two. Too thick, and it will not move; too thin, and colours wash out and crack. Mix each colour separately and test one spoonful on scrap card.

| Paint type | Paint to medium | Water | Finish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy body acrylic | 1 to 2 | 1 teaspoon per 60 ml | Thick cells |
| Fluid acrylic | 1 to 1 | A few drops | Smooth flow |
| Craft acrylic | 1 to 2 | 1 to 2 teaspoons per 60 ml | Budget pours |
| Metallic acrylic | 1 to 2 | None, or a few drops | Gold veins |
| Ready-mixed pour paint | None needed | None | Fastest start |

## Sealing Your Pour

Never seal a pour before it is fully dry, or the varnish will cloud and trap moisture underneath. Wait at least 48 hours for a thin pour and 72 hours for a thick one, then press a fingertip to a corner: it should feel hard and cool, not tacky.

Two thin coats of high-gloss varnish, brushed on in opposite directions with an hour between coats, give most pours their shine for about £6 a bottle. Resin is the glass-like finish, about £15 to £20 for a small kit: it deepens every colour, but it must be mixed exactly 1 to 1, poured in a dust-free room, and left to cure for 24 to 72 hours.

## Sizing and Layout

Pour art is visually loud, so size it generously: a small piece looks lost on a big wall. A single canvas should cover roughly two-thirds of the furniture below it — above a 180 cm sofa, aim for about 120 cm of art, either one large canvas or a triptych. Hang the centre at about 145 cm, average eye level, and keep 15 cm clear above a sofa or headboard. The [DIY wall decor guide](/blog/diy-wall-decor-guide) shows how to lay out a repeating pattern, and the [DIY wall decor ideas](/blog/diy-wall-decor-ideas) pillar gathers the whole wall-decor cluster in one place.

In a small room, one medium canvas beats three small ones, because the pour's swirls need space to read.

## Hanging Hardware by Weight

Weigh the finished piece before you choose a fixing, because a resin-sealed pour weighs far more than a varnished canvas of the same size. A stretched canvas pour usually weighs under 1 kg, and a large adhesive strip or picture hook holds it. A wood-panel pour with a resin coat can reach 2 kg, so use one screw hook into a wall plug. Over 2 kg, use two screw hooks to split the load. The [textured plaster art](/blog/textured-plaster-art) guide shows how a heavy textured piece is anchored safely, using the same weight-first rule.

Never hang a wet or tacky pour: the paint can stick to the wall, and the canvas can sag off its stretcher bars overnight.

## Mistakes to Avoid

The biggest mistake is pouring paint that is too thick or too thin — test every colour on scrap card until it falls like warm honey. The second is using too many colours: five or more in one cup usually turns to mud, so keep it to four and let white or a metallic break them up. The third is tilting too much, which drags every colour off the edges; tilt just enough to cover the canvas, then stop.

Torch too close and the paint scorches and cells burn away — hold it 15 cm off and keep it moving. Moving a wet canvas sends paint sliding to one edge, and sealing too soon clouds the varnish.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY acrylic pour wall art cost?

Most cost £6 to £30: a mini canvas trio £6 to £9, a classic dirty pour £8 to £14, and an emerald triptych £16 to £25. A first project needs about £15 of supplies — canvas, four craft acrylics, Floetrol and a torch — and the leftovers cover several more pours.

### What is the best paint to medium ratio for acrylic pouring?

Use one part paint to two parts medium for craft and heavy-body acrylics, and one to one for fluid acrylics. Thin with a few drops of water until it flows like warm honey.

### How do I get cells in acrylic pour art?

Add two to three drops of silicone oil to each colour as you mix it, then pass a kitchen torch lightly over the wet surface, holding it 15 cm away and keeping it moving.

### How long does acrylic pour art take to dry?

Hands-on work takes 30 minutes to an hour, but paint needs 24 to 48 hours to dry flat, and thick pours up to 72 hours. Resin needs a further 24 to 72 hours to cure.

### How do I seal acrylic pour wall art?

Wipe off silicone residue, then brush on two thin coats of high-gloss varnish in opposite directions, an hour apart. For glass-like shine, mix resin exactly 1 to 1, pour thin, torch out bubbles, and cure 24 to 72 hours.

### Can beginners do acrylic pour wall art?

Yes — the paint does the painting, so no drawing skill is needed. A first dirty pour takes 45 minutes, costs £8 to £14, and even a messy attempt looks good once sealed and hung.
