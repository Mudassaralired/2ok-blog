## Why Pegboards Work as Wall Decor (Not Just Storage)

Most wall decor is fixed: once a picture is hung, it stays put. A pegboard is different — every hook, shelf and basket moves, so the wall changes with the season and your mood. That is why pegboards have moved from workshops into living rooms and kitchens.

They also solve the small-wall problem. A blank stretch beside a door or above a desk is too narrow for a [floating shelf](/blog/diy-floating-shelves/) and too awkward for a gallery wall, but a 60 cm pegboard fills it while holding keys, plants or books. In a small living room, one board replaces a whole shelf unit — see the [small living room storage](/blog/small-living-room-storage/) guide.

Treat the board as decor first, storage second: paint it, frame it, and hang beautiful things — prints, trailing plants, a vase of dried flowers — alongside the useful ones.

## Choosing Your Pegboard: Materials, Sizes and Costs

**Hardboard (Masonite)** is the classic: cheap, light, easy to cut and paint. Fixed into studs, a 6 mm panel holds 10 to 15 kg. The brown face needs paint. Cost: £8 to £15 for a 60 by 90 cm panel.

**MDF pegboard** is smoother, takes paint beautifully and has a cleaner edge, but it swells if wet, so avoid steamy bathrooms. Cost: £12 to £20.

**Metal pegboard** is the strongest — steel holds 20 kg or more — and suits kitchens and craft rooms. It needs a metal primer before painting. Cost: £25 to £45.

**Acrylic pegboard** is clear or frosted and lets the wall colour show through, but it scratches easily, so it suits display rather than heavy storage. Cost: £30 to £60.

Size the board to the wall: 60 by 90 cm suits a desk or entryway; 90 by 120 cm fills the wall above a sofa. Cover about one-third to one-half of the empty wall width.

## How to Install a Pegboard Step by Step

Hooks need about 20 mm of clear space behind the board to seat and lock, so it must stand off the wall on furring strips, spacers or a frame.

**Step 1: Find the studs.** Run a stud finder across the wall and mark the stud centres — usually 40 cm apart. If the board will hold more than 5 kg, at least two fixings must land in studs.

**Step 2: Cut the furring strips.** Cut two or three 20 mm by 40 mm timber strips to the board's width — top, bottom, and one in the middle for boards over 90 cm tall.

**Step 3: Fix the strips to the wall.** Drill pilot holes into the studs and drive 50 mm screws in, checking with a spirit level.

**Step 4: Mount the board.** Hold the board against the strips, level it, and screw through the peg holes with 25 mm screws and washers — four for a small board, six for a large one. Never screw it flat to the wall: the hooks will not fit.

**Step 5: Load it light first.** Hang shelves and baskets first, then light decor, then heavier items. Fixed into studs, a board holds 15 kg; on plasterboard plugs alone, about 8 kg.

**Renter-friendly, no-drill options.** If you cannot drill, use heavy-duty adhesive strips rated for 7 kg on a clean, dry wall — two per 30 cm of board edge, pressed for 30 seconds each. That holds a painted board up to about 5 kg. Or lean a framed board on a sideboard or desk. When moving out, pull strips straight down, never outwards.

## How to Paint and Finish a Pegboard Like a Designer

Sand lightly, wipe off the dust, and prime once — unprimed hardboard drinks paint and looks patchy. Then use two thin coats of eggshell or satin emulsion, letting the first dry fully. Spray paint gives the smoothest finish and reaches inside the holes; a roller works too, rolled lightly so paint does not clog them. Push a cocktail stick through any hole that fills up.

**Colour-blocked** is the easiest designer look: top half one colour, bottom half another, masked off with painter's tape — terracotta over cream, sage over white, navy over pale grey. **Ombré** blends three tones of one colour from dark at the bottom to pale at the top. **Wall-matched** paints the board the exact colour of the wall so it disappears and the objects do the talking.

Four strips of 20 mm pine, mitred and painted to match, hide the raw edge and add about £6 — framed, the board reads as furniture. Varnish a kitchen or bathroom board so steam never marks the paint.

## Styling Rules: Hooks, Shelves and the 20% Empty Rule

A pegboard looks designed when the pieces vary in size and depth. Mix flat things (prints, photos, a mirror), medium things (shelves, baskets) and one or two things that stick out (a trailing plant, a vase). If everything sits at the same depth, the board looks flat.

Group in odd numbers — three hooks together, one shelf with two objects — and repeat one material twice, such as brass hooks with a brass-framed print.

The 20% empty rule: leave at least a fifth of the holes bare. Empty space lets the eye rest and leaves room for a seasonal piece without rearranging everything.

## Pegboard Art Hacks

The holes are a grid, and a grid is a canvas. **Cross-stitch murals** use chunky yarn threaded through the holes to draw a simple shape — a sun, a leaf, a heart. Sketch it on graph paper first, one square per hole, then stitch with a blunt tapestry needle. A 30 by 30 cm motif takes about two hours and £5 of yarn.

**Photo displays** need only mini wooden pegs or bulldog clips: clip prints to the board in a loose grid and swap them whenever you like.

**Seasonal swaps** are the pegboard's party trick. Keep the shelves and hooks fixed and change only the small things: dried orange slices and pinecones in December, fresh herbs and pastel prints in spring, a sunflower in summer. The swap takes ten minutes. The [DIY wall decor ideas](/blog/diy-wall-decor-ideas/) pillar gathers the room-by-room looks that pair with a pegboard.

## Mistakes to Avoid

**Overloading one side.** A board loaded heavily on one side pulls away from the wall over time. Spread heavy items across the board, heaviest near the fixings.

**Skipping the gap.** Screwing the board flat to the wall is the most common mistake. Without the 20 mm stand-off, hooks cannot seat, shelves wobble, and the board cracks around the screws.

**Sagging spans.** A hardboard panel wider than 90 cm with no middle strip sags within months. Add a middle strip, or choose metal for wide boards.

**Overfilling.** A board packed to every hole looks cluttered and hides the paint and frame you worked on. If you cannot see board between the objects, take something off.

**Wrong fixings for the wall.** Plasterboard plugs hold about 8 kg; brick and stud fixings hold far more. Weigh the loaded board on bathroom scales before hanging anything heavy.

## FAQ

### What size pegboard should I buy for a living room?

A 90 by 120 cm board suits the wall above a sofa or sideboard; 60 by 90 cm suits a desk wall or entryway.

### How do I hang a pegboard without drilling holes?

Use heavy-duty adhesive strips rated for 7 kg on a clean, dry wall, keeping the loaded board under 5 kg. Or lean a framed board on a sideboard.

### How much weight can a pegboard hold?

A hardboard or MDF board fixed into studs holds 10 to 15 kg; on plugs alone, about 8 kg. Metal boards hold 20 kg or more.

### Can I paint a pegboard?

Yes — sand lightly, prime once, then apply two thin coats of eggshell or satin emulsion, or spray paint for the smoothest finish. Clear filled holes with a cocktail stick while the paint is wet.

### What is the best pegboard for a small living room?

A painted hardboard panel matched to the wall, framed in pale wood, with two small shelves and a trailing plant. It adds storage without bulk. See the [wall decor](/topics/wall-decor/) hub for more small-room ideas.
