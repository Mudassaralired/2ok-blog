A terracotta pot costs about £1, weighs almost nothing empty and brings the warm, baked-earth colour that half the decor internet is chasing. Hung on a wall — clipped, railed, roped or painted — a row of them becomes a living wall feature for less than one framed print. These 15 DIY terracotta pot wall decor ideas cover the mounting systems honestly, with the weights and watering worked out, because a pot wall fails on physics, not taste.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Wall pots work best at 9 to 15 cm — a filled 13 cm pot weighs about 1.4 kg.
> - Pot clips, rails and rings hold the weight; a board mount spreads it over two screws, best for renters.
> - Seal the inside of indoor pots or the clay wicks moisture into the wall.
> - Painted pots cost about £3 each all in: a £1 pot, acrylic and a matte sealer.
> - Trailing plants and succulents suit walls; thirsty, heavy plants do not.

## Five Ways to Mount a Pot on a Wall

Every pot wall in this guide hangs one of five ways. The method matters more than the arrangement: it decides the weight limit, the hole count and whether the display can move.

| Method | Cost | Holds | Renter-friendly? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Metal pot clips | £1–£2 per pot | 9–17 cm pots, to ~2 kg each | Only on a board |
| Wall rings and straps | £3–£5 per pot | 13–20 cm pots | No |
| Rail with S-hooks | £10–£18 per rail | Rows of 9–13 cm pots | No |
| Board or pallet mount | £5–£10 per board | Any size, load spread | Yes — two screws total |
| Macramé hangers | £3–£6 per pot | 11–15 cm pots | Yes — one hook each |

**Pot clips** are spring-steel clips that grip the pot's rim and screw to the wall, so the pot floats flat against it. They are the cleanest look and the strictest: pot size must match clip size, and the screw goes into a plug or stud.

**Rails** — a towel rail, curtain rod or length of black pipe — take S-hooks through the pot rim or clips along its length, and pots slide sideways to rearrange. A rail also fixes every pot at one height, so measure the drop: rims should clear the furniture or floor below by at least 20 cm once the plants trail. **Board mounts** fix clips to a stained board or pallet, then hang the whole board from two screws: the renter's pot wall, and the porch's too. **Macramé hangers** need one hook each and swing slightly, which trailing plants forgive. This guide sits inside the wider [DIY wall decor guide](/blog/diy-wall-decor-guide/); the [DIY wall planter ideas](/blog/diy-wall-planter-ideas/) guide covers the non-terracotta planters these swap with.

## Classic Planted Pot Walls

### 1. Macramé hanger trio

Three terracotta pots in rope macramé hangers, hung at staggered heights with trailing plants spilling out — the arrangement on this page's cover. Odd numbers and uneven drops are the whole trick; three identical hangers in a straight line look like a shop display. Cost: £12 to £18 for three hangers and pots. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. It is the natural partner to a [boho wall decor](/blog/boho-wall-decor-ideas/) scheme — same rope, same earth colours.

### 2. Pot clip herb wall

Clips on a board or straight onto a sunny kitchen or patio wall, one herb per pot: basil, thyme, rosemary, mint in its own pot before it takes over. Chalk the herb's name on each pot or stencil it (idea 8). Cost: £15 to £25 for six pots, clips and herbs. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. Keep the board within reach of the cooking — a herb wall you have to fetch a stool for is a herb wall that dies.

### 3. Rail and S-hook row

A 1 m rail above a sideboard or along a porch wall, with five 11 cm pots hung by their rims on S-hooks. The row rearranges in seconds — sun-lovers to the bright end, new pots in, tired pots out — which no fixed system allows. Cost: £20 to £30 all in. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. Use hooks with a closed loop or crimp the S-hook's top bend, so a knocked pot cannot lift off its hook.

### 4. Half-pot wall pockets

Half pots — made with a flat back to sit flush on a wall — pocketed with succulents or trailing sedum. Buy them ready (£3 to £5) unless you own an angle grinder and enjoy using it; cutting terracotta is dusty, loud work. Cost: £4 to £7 per pocket planted. Time: 20 minutes each. Difficulty: easy bought, intermediate cut. Two screws per pocket through the drainage hole and a top lip hold it; the flat back keeps soil off the wall.

### 5. Rope ladder tiers

Two lengths of rope joined by three wooden dowels make a hanging ladder; a pot clips or ties on at each level, plants cascading down the rungs. It fills a tall, narrow wall — a porch column, the space beside a door — that nothing else fits. Cost: £10 to £15. Time: one hour. Difficulty: intermediate, mostly knots. Hang it from one heavy-duty hook into a stud: three watered pots total 4 kg or more.

## Painted and Patterned Pots

### 6. Painted pots at £3 each

A £1 pot, a coat of acrylic and a coat of matte sealer: £3 a pot, and a shelf or rail of them in sage, cream and dusty white looks like a boutique set. The row on this page's shelf photo is the template — two colours per pot at most, dipped or taped lines, one colour family across the row. Cost: £3 per pot. Time: 30 minutes each plus drying. Difficulty: easy. Paint the outside only, and seal inside too if the pot will live indoors (see the watering section).

![Row of sage green and white painted terracotta pots with succulents on a wooden wall shelf](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-terracotta-pot-wall-decor/in-article-1)

### 7. Whitewashed Mediterranean pots

White paint thinned half-and-half with water, brushed on and wiped back with a rag while wet, leaves the terracotta glowing through — the Greek-island finish. It suits unglazed pots best and takes ten minutes a pot. Cost: about £2 per pot. Time: 15 minutes each. Difficulty: easy. Do a whole set in one session; whitewash mixed twice never matches, and a matched set is the point.

### 8. Stencilled number and herb pots

A stencil, white or black acrylic and a steady hand turn plain pots into a numbered row for the porch or a labelled herb set for the kitchen. House-number pots by the front door are the version visitors photograph. Cost: £2.50 per pot with a reusable stencil. Time: 20 minutes each. Difficulty: easy. Pencil the design first and stencil in two light coats — one heavy coat bleeds under the stencil and ruins the edge.

### 9. Ombre sunset row

Five pots on a rail or shelf, painted in one colour fading across the row — deep terracotta-red through coral to blush — so the set reads as one gradient. Mix the five shades in jam jars from one base colour plus white, darkest first. Cost: £12 to £15 for the set. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. The plants should be one repeated green — five different plants fight the gradient.

### 10. Saucer wall clock and saucer wreath

The saucers are decor too. A large 25 cm saucer takes a £4 clock movement through its centre and becomes a wall clock; a ring of small hand-painted saucers around it makes a wreath of mini plates. Cost: £10 to £16 for the set. Time: one hour. Difficulty: intermediate — drilling the saucer centre needs a ceramic bit, water cooling and patience. Hung with plate hangers, painted saucers also fill a narrow wall on their own, like a rustic echo of a plate wall.

## Unusual Terracotta Wall Ideas

### 11. Broken-pot terraced planter

A cracked pot is not rubbish — its shards become terraces. Set a large half pot against the wall, layer the broken pieces inside as steps, and plant a tiny staircase of succulents up them. Cost: £3 to £6, mostly plants. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. File or sand every exposed shard edge; broken terracotta is sharper than it looks.

### 12. Tiny pot wind chime

Five to seven miniature pots, 5 to 7 cm, threaded upside-down along a cord with wooden beads between them, hung beside a door or window. They knock softly rather than ring — a gentle wooden note, safe for neighbours. Cost: £6 to £9. Time: 40 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Thread through the drainage holes, bead above and below each pot, and keep the whole chime under 1 kg so a picture hook holds it.

### 13. Candle pot sconce row

Small pots on clips, each holding an LED candle, make a row of rustic wall lights for a patio or porch evening. Clay throws the light downward beautifully and cannot catch fire — but use LED candles anyway, because wax melts down the wall. Cost: £10 to £14 for four. Time: 20 minutes. Difficulty: easy. On a timer, they come on at dusk without anyone remembering; the [DIY wall sconce ideas](/blog/diy-wall-sconce-ideas/) guide covers the wired-looking versions of the same trick.

### 14. Air plant grid

Tiny 5 cm pots fixed to a grid board — glue or small clips — each holding an air plant: no soil, no weight, no drainage, just a weekly misting. A 3 × 3 grid covers 45 cm of wall and weighs under 1 kg total, the lightest idea in this guide. Cost: £15 to £20, mostly the plants. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Air plants come out of their pots for a monthly soak, so fix the pots, never the plants.

### 15. The seasonal swap rail

Fit one rail by the door or above the sideboard and change only the pots: bulbs and herbs in spring, trailing flowers in summer, pinecones and dried oranges in autumn, LED candles and fir at Christmas. The wall stays dressed all year for one rail's cost. Cost: £18 to £25 to start, then pennies a season. Time: one hour to fit. Difficulty: easy. It is the highest-value idea here — the decor changes, the holes never do.

## Watering, Drainage and Wall Protection

Terracotta breathes — that is why plants love it and walls hate it. An unsealed pot against a wall wicks moisture through the clay and leaves a damp patch behind it within a month. The fix is one coat of matte water-based sealer or terracotta sealant inside the pot before planting, plus a 1 cm air gap behind it — clips and rings provide this naturally; board mounts need spacer washers.

Weigh before you hang: a 9 cm pot planted and watered is about 0.7 kg, a 13 cm pot about 1.4 kg, a 17 cm pot 2.4 kg or more. Stay inside your fixing's rating with the watered weight, not the dry one — most pot-wall accidents happen in the hour after watering.

Water wall pots with a narrow-spout can, a little at a time, over a towel held beneath. Indoor pots without drainage get a gravel layer and restraint; pots with drainage get a saucer or come down once a month for a proper soak and drain before going back up. Soil stays in if you top each pot with gravel or coarse grit and never fill above the rim's shoulder.

## Best Plants for Wall Pots

Trailing plants earn their wall space: string of pearls, ivy, pothos and tradescantia all spill over the rim and hide the fixings. Succulents — echeveria, burro's tail, small cacti — suit sunny walls and forgive forgotten watering. Herbs suit kitchen walls near a window. Match the plant to the wall's light before anything else: succulents and herbs want four or more hours of sun, pothos and ivy tolerate the shadier wall, and a plant starved of light fails on a wall just as surely as anywhere else — only slower, and higher up. Avoid ferns and anything thirsty or top-heavy: they need misting the wall cannot take, and a dry fern drops leaves on the floor for months. For high or dark walls, good faux trailing plants beat dying real ones, and nobody at two metres can tell.

## FAQ

### How much weight can a pot clip hold?

A quality steel clip holds about 2 kg — one watered pot up to 15 cm. Match the clip size to the pot's rim, and screw into a plug or stud, never bare plasterboard.

### Will terracotta pots make my wall damp?

Unsealed, yes — clay wicks water through to the wall. Seal the pot's inside with matte sealer before planting and keep a small air gap behind it, and the problem never starts.

### Can renters hang a pot wall?

Yes — the board mount. Clips fix to one board, the board hangs from two screws, and the whole display comes down as one piece. Macramé hangers on single hooks work too.

### How do I stop soil falling out of wall pots?

Top the soil with gravel or coarse grit, fill only to the rim's shoulder, and water gently with a narrow spout. Hanging pots tilt slightly forward — the grit layer is what keeps the surface in place.

### Do painted terracotta pots survive outside?

Acrylic under two coats of matte sealer lasts two to three seasons. The real killer is frost in unsealed clay — water in the clay freezes, expands and cracks the pot — so bring painted pots under cover for winter.
