These 15 wall-only projects each carry a real cost, time and difficulty rating — plus the felt-type guide, cutting methods and hanging rules the short listicles skip.

## Felt Flower Wall Art

### 1. Layered felt flower bouquet panel

Glue layered felt petals into full, rose-like blooms in terracotta, blush and cream, then arrange nine on a painted plywood backing. Cost: $10 to $18. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. A 60 × 60 cm panel weighs under 1 kg, so two adhesive strips hold it.

### 2. Felt flower wreath

Wrap a foam wreath ring in felt strips, then glue on layered felt flowers and leaves. Cost: $8 to $14. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. A 40 cm wreath weighs about 500 g, so one adhesive hook holds it. The [DIY dried flower wall decor](/blog/diy-dried-flower-wall-decor) guide shows how a wreath anchors a hallway wall.

### 3. Giant single felt blooms

Cut oversized petals from stiff felt and layer them into one 45 cm flower, then mount it on a round backing board. Cost: $9 to $15. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. One bloom weighs about 400 g, so a single adhesive strip holds it — hang three in a row for a feature wall.

## Moon Phases and Celestial Pieces

### 4. Moon phase wall hanging

Cut eight felt moons — new, crescent, half and full — and stitch or glue them in a row on a wooden dowel. Cost: $6 to $11. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. The whole hanging weighs under 500 g, so one adhesive hook holds it.

### 5. Felt sun, moon and stars nursery piece

Cut a large felt sun, a crescent moon and small stars, then layer them on a cream and mustard felt backing panel. Cost: $8 to $12. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. A 50 × 70 cm panel weighs under 800 g, so adhesive strips hold it safely above a cot, with no hard parts. ## Tassels, Bunting and Garlands

### 6. Felt tassel wall hanging

Cut felt into strips, roll them into tassels, then knot a row in a color gradient onto a wooden dowel. Cost: $5 to $10. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. A 60 cm hanging weighs about 400 g, so one adhesive hook holds it. The [DIY yarn wall hanging](/blog/diy-yarn-wall-hanging) guide covers the same dowel-and-cord hanging method.

### 7. Felt tassel garland

String small felt tassels along a cord with wooden beads between them, and drape it across a mantel, doorway or headboard. Cost: $4 to $8. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. A 2 metre garland weighs under 300 g, so two small adhesive hooks hold it.

### 8. Felt pennant bunting banner

Cut felt triangles in alternating colors, stitch or glue them to ribbon, and hang the banner across a wall or shelf. Cost: $5 to $9. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. A 3 metre banner weighs about 350 g, so two adhesive hooks hold it, and it folds flat for storage.

## Geometric Felt Murals

### 9. Geometric felt tile mural

Cut felt into triangles, diamonds and half-circles, then arrange them into a bold geometric mural on the wall or a backing board. Cost: $12 to $22. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. A 1 square metre mural weighs about 600 g, so adhesive strips hold it — plan the pattern on the floor first. Cut felt hexagons in three or four colors and stick them to the wall in a honeycomb, leaving some cells empty. Cost: $11 to $20. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. Twenty hexagons weigh under 500 g, so adhesive strips hold them, and you can add more cells later.

## Choosing Your Felt

Felt comes in three main types, and the right one decides whether a project holds its shape or flops. Craft felt is the cheapest — thin acrylic sheets, about $1 for an A4 sheet — and it suits flat pieces like murals, bunting and garlands. Wool-blend felt costs about $4 to $6 per A4 sheet, but it is thicker and softer and holds a curl, so it suits layered flowers, leaves and anything three-dimensional. Stiff felt is treated to hold a rigid shape, about $3 to $4 per sheet, and it suits tiles, hexagons and letters that must stay flat.

Thin felt under 1 mm is best for layering and fine shapes, while 2 to 3 mm felt gives flowers and leaves body. A 40-sheet craft felt pack costs about $10 and covers most of the projects here.

| Felt type | Price per sheet | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Craft felt | About $1 | Flat murals, bunting, garlands | Flops in 3D shapes |
| Wool-blend felt | $4–$6 | Layered flowers, leaves, tassels | Costs more per sheet |
| Stiff felt | $3–$4 | Tiles, hexagons, letters | Hard to stitch by hand |

## Cutting, Stitching and Gluing Felt

Felt cuts cleanly with sharp fabric scissors — blunt blades crush the fibres and leave ragged edges, so keep one pair for felt only and never use them on paper. Cut from the back, and draw shapes with a fabric pencil or chalk so no marks show on the front. For repeated shapes like petals and hexagons, cut a card template first and trace around it.

Stitching gives the strongest joins and the neatest finish. A running stitch or blanket stitch in matching or contrasting thread holds layers together — use a sharp needle and embroidery thread, and keep stitches small and even. Hot glue is faster and suits flat joins, but use it sparingly: a pea-sized dot holds a petal, while a big blob soaks through thin felt and shows as a hard lump. Fabric glue dries slower but stays flexible, which suits pieces that will be rolled or folded. ## Sizing and Layout

Felt is soft and visually quiet, so size it generously: a piece that is too small looks lost. A single piece should cover roughly two-thirds of the furniture below it — above a 180 cm sofa, aim for about 120 cm of felt art. Hang the center at about 145 cm, average eye level, and keep at least 15 cm of clear wall above a sofa or headboard.

For a mural or honeycomb, cover a clear area or stop at a natural break — a doorway, a ceiling line or furniture. For a gallery of felt pieces, keep gaps of 5 to 8 cm and mix one large piece with two small ones. ## Hanging Hardware by Weight

Weigh the finished piece before you choose hardware, then pick a fixing rated for at least twice that weight. Almost every felt piece here weighs under 1 kg, and a large adhesive strip or hook holds it. From 1 to 2 kg — a layered flower panel or a botanical board — use two adhesive strips or one screw hook. Over 2 kg, use two screw hooks into wall plugs.

Two fixings split the load and keep the piece level — a single hook lets a board twist and sag. On plasterboard, use adhesive strips or toggle bolts rather than a single screw, which pulls out of bare plasterboard. Self-adhesive felt tiles need no hardware at all, but the wall must be clean, dry and smooth.

| Weight | Examples | Best fixing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 1 kg | Moon phases, tassels, rainbow, hoops | Large adhesive strip or hook |
| 1–2 kg | Flower panel, botanical board | 2 adhesive strips or 1 screw hook |
| Over 2 kg | Large framed felt piece | 2 screw hooks into wall plugs |

## Renter Friendly Felt Decor

Felt is one of the most renter-friendly materials, because almost every piece is light enough for adhesive fixings. Use removable adhesive strips on a clean, dry wall, wait an hour before loading, and remove them by pulling the tab slowly down the wall — never pull them straight off. Test one strip in a hidden spot first, and never stick felt to freshly painted walls: paint needs four weeks to cure, or the strips will pull it off.

A picture rail is even better where one exists — hooks slide along it and the piece hangs on cord. A tension rod across a doorway holds a felt garland with no fixings, and a large framed piece can simply lean on a sideboard. ## Mistakes to Avoid and Felt Care

The biggest mistake is cutting felt with blunt scissors: the edges go fuzzy and ragged, so keep one sharp pair for felt only. The second is using too much hot glue — a big blob soaks through thin felt and shows as a hard lump, so use pea-sized dots and let them set.

The third is hanging felt in a damp room. Felt absorbs moisture, goes limp and can grow mould, so keep it out of bathrooms and steamy kitchens. The fourth is overfilling the wall: felt is soft and quiet, but five felt pieces on one wall still look cluttered — pick one hero piece and let the rest support it. Care is simple: dust with a soft brush or a lint roller weekly, and spot-clean with a barely damp cloth. Never machine-wash a finished piece — the layers will separate.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY felt wall decor cost?

Most projects cost $4 to $22. A felt tassel garland costs $4 to $8, a moon phase hanging $6 to $11, and a geometric felt tile mural $12 to $22.

### What type of felt is best for wall decor?

Wool-blend felt holds its shape best for layered flowers and 3D pieces, craft felt suits flat murals and bunting, and stiff felt suits tiles, hexagons and letters. Start with a mixed craft felt pack if you are trying felt for the first time.

### How do I hang felt wall decor without damaging the wall?

Most felt pieces weigh under 1 kg, so removable adhesive strips or hooks hold them and peel off cleanly. For heavier framed pieces over 2 kg, use two screw hooks into wall plugs.

### Can I hang felt wall decor in a bathroom?

No — felt absorbs moisture, goes limp and can grow mould in steamy rooms. Keep felt in bedrooms, living rooms and hallways instead.

### How do I clean felt wall decor?

Dust it weekly with a soft brush or a lint roller, and spot-clean with a barely damp cloth. Never machine-wash a finished piece, because the layers will separate.
