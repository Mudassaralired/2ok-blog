A DIY Christmas centerpiece is the fastest way to make a plain dining table feel like Christmas dinner: one tray of greenery, a few candles and ornaments you already own, and the whole room changes. These 15 DIY Christmas centerpiece ideas are all homemade, each with a real cost, time and difficulty, so you can pick one that fits your table and budget.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Most DIY Christmas centerpieces cost £4 to £20 and take under two hours to build.
> - Keep the greenery low and let the candles carry the height, so everyone can still see across the table.
> - Size the centrepiece to the table: about two-thirds of the table length on a long table, one-third of the width on a round one.
> - Fresh cedar lasts about two weeks indoors, pine about ten days, so build greenery pieces the week of Christmas.
> - Never leave a real flame burning unattended, and keep every flame at least 10 cm from dry greenery.

## Classic Greenery and Candle Christmas Centerpieces

### 1. Evergreen tray with taper candles

A long wooden tray of cedar and fir branches, four taper candles in brass holders and gold baubles is the classic Christmas table look. Lay the branches in first, then push the holders in so the greenery hides their bases. Cost: £12 to £20. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. See the [Christmas table decor](/blog/christmas-table-decor) guide for matching place settings.

### 2. Wood-slice candle display

Three wood slices of different heights, each holding a chunky pillar candle, make a rustic centrepiece for a farmhouse table. Sand the slices, set a candle on each one, then tuck pine sprigs and pinecones around the bases. Cost: £8 to £14. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 3. Citrus and herb Christmas centerpiece

Dried orange slices, fresh rosemary and cinnamon sticks along a linen runner smell like Christmas before anyone sits down. Lay the slices in a row, add rosemary between them, and finish with three small white candles in glass votives. Cost: £5 to £9. Time: 1 hour plus drying the oranges. Difficulty: easy.

### 4. Mini tree in a vintage pot

A small potted fir or rosemary tree, dressed with miniature baubles and battery fairy lights, becomes a living centrepiece you can plant out in January. Choose a tree 40 to 50 cm tall so it does not block faces across the table. Cost: £10 to £18. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 5. Dried-orange garland runner

Run a garland of dried orange slices, cinnamon sticks and fir sprigs down the middle of the table, with tea lights tucked along it. The runner suits long tables because guests can see over it and still reach the serving dishes. Cost: £6 to £11. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. The [Christmas garland ideas](/blog/christmas-garland-ideas) guide covers drying oranges and wiring garlands.

## Ornaments, Fruit and Natural Texture Christmas Centerpieces

### 6. Ornament bowl with floating candles

Fill a wide glass bowl with water, float three tea lights on top, then add red and gold baubles and cranberries so they bob between the candles. It catches the light beautifully and costs almost nothing if you raid the ornament box. Cost: £6 to £12. Time: 20 minutes. Difficulty: easy. The [DIY Christmas ornaments](/blog/diy-christmas-ornaments) guide shows how to make matching baubles.

![Glass bowl centerpiece with red and gold ornaments, floating candles and cranberries on a round table](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-christmas-centerpiece-ideas/in-article-2)

### 7. Cranberry floating candles

A tall glass vase or hurricane, half filled with water and cranberries, with a floating candle on top, is the simplest centrepiece here and one of the prettiest. The berries sink and rise as the water moves, and the candlelight glows through them. Cost: £4 to £8. Time: 10 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 8. Pinecone and candle cluster

Gather pinecones, spray a few gold and leave the rest natural, then cluster them around three pillar candles of different heights on a round wooden board. It looks like a shop-bought display, and every part packs away for next year. Cost: £4 to £8. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Bake gathered pinecones at 120 degrees for 30 minutes first, and keep candles 10 cm from the nearest pinecone.

### 9. Gift-box tray centerpiece

Wrap three empty boxes in kraft paper and ribbon, stack them at different heights on a tray, and nestle greenery, pinecones and fairy lights around them. It looks like presents waiting to be opened. Cost: £7 to £12. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 10. Apple and cinnamon stick runner

A row of deep red apples, cinnamon sticks tied in bundles and fir sprigs makes a fragrant runner that costs less than a takeaway coffee. Set the apples in a line down the centre, tuck cinnamon bundles between them, and add two or three tea lights for evening glow. Cost: £5 to £9. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

## Lanterns, Trees and Statement Christmas Centerpieces

### 11. Lantern centerpiece

One large lantern with a pillar candle inside, ringed by greenery and a few baubles, is the easiest statement piece here — and the safest, because the glass keeps the flame away from everything else. Cost: £12 to £20. Time: 20 minutes. Difficulty: easy. A lantern 30 to 40 cm tall suits most tables. Battery candles work just as well and let you leave it lit all evening.

### 12. Bottle-brush tree cloche

A glass cloche or a large jam jar turned upside down over a bottle-brush tree, a pinch of fake snow and a tiny reindeer makes a snow-globe centrepiece that children love. Set it on a wooden board with greenery around the base. Cost: £6 to £11. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 13. Eucalyptus and white candle runner

Silver-green eucalyptus laid flat along the table, with white pillar candles in clear glass holders spaced along it, is the calmest look here and suits a modern dining room best. Eucalyptus dries gracefully, so this runner still looks good on Boxing Day. Cost: £8 to £14. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 14. Berry branch and candle centerpiece

Tall stems of red berries — real ilex or good artificial ones — in a low vase, ringed by three candles and a circle of pine, bring height without blocking anyone's view, because you can see through the stems. Cost: £9 to £15. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 15. Snowy village tray scene

A deep tray becomes a tiny winter village: three small bottle-brush trees, two miniature houses, a scatter of fake snow and fairy lights woven through it. It is the most charming centrepiece here and a lovely one to build with children. Cost: £10 to £18. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: moderate. ## Sizing Your Christmas Centerpiece by Table Shape

The most common centrepiece mistake is getting the size wrong for the table. On a long rectangular table, the centrepiece should run about two-thirds of the table length and no more than 30 cm wide, leaving 25 cm clear on each side. A 180 cm table takes about 120 cm; a 240 cm table takes about 160 cm, usually as two smaller pieces.

On a round table the rule changes. A round table of 120 cm across takes a centrepiece no wider than 40 cm, so guests still have room for plates and elbows. One compact piece — an ornament bowl, a lantern or a small tray — works better than a long runner.

Height matters as much as footprint. Keep the greenery and the base of the piece below about 30 cm so everyone can see across the table. Candles are the exception: tall tapers carry the height without blocking the view, because they are thin. Anything solid and tall belongs on a sideboard.

## Candle and Fire Safety

Real candles make a Christmas table glow, but they need respect. Keep every flame at least 10 cm from dry greenery, pinecones, paper and ribbon, and never let a candle burn down to within 5 cm of its holder. Trim wicks to 6 mm, and never leave a lit candle unattended.

Flameless candles are the safer choice with children or pets at the table, in a centrepiece packed tight with dry greenery, and anywhere the candles will burn for hours. Battery candles flicker convincingly and cost about £1 each in a multipack. If you use real candles, set each one in a proper holder on a stable, heatproof surface. Dried orange slices and cinnamon sticks are flammable, so keep them from open flames, and use LED fairy lights only.

## Greenery Freshness and Care

Fresh greenery makes a centrepiece look expensive, but every type has a different indoor life. Cedar lasts longest, holding colour and scent for about two weeks. Pine lasts around ten days before the needles drop. Eucalyptus lasts about two weeks and dries silvery. Rosemary lasts about a week and keeps its scent. Fir lasts about seven days, so build fir pieces last.

| Greenery | Lasts indoors | Best built | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cedar | About 14 days | 2 weeks before | Holds colour |
| Pine | About 10 days | 1 week before | Drops needles |
| Eucalyptus | About 14 days | 2 weeks before | Dries silvery |
| Rosemary | About 7 days | 3–4 days before | Keeps its scent |
| Fir | About 7 days | 3–4 days before | Build last |

Build cedar, pine and eucalyptus pieces up to two weeks before Christmas, and save fir and rosemary for the final few days. Mist the greenery every few days and keep it away from radiators, and it will stay fresh through Christmas Day. ## Mistakes to Avoid and Reusing Your Centerpiece Next Year

The biggest mistake is building too tall: a centrepiece that blocks faces ruins conversation. The second is overfilling the table — leave 25 cm clear in front of every place setting. The third is fresh greenery too early: a fir piece built in early December will be needles by Christmas Day.

The fourth is forgetting the table: hot holders, wet vases and damp greenery mark wood, so always set the piece on a tray or board. The fifth is buying everything new — most of these ideas use things you already own, so check the decoration box first.

Almost everything here reuses well. Trays, lanterns, cloches, candle holders and baubles pack away as they are. Pinecones, dried orange slices and cinnamon sticks keep in a labelled box for next year if they stay dry. Only the fresh greenery is single-use, so compost it after Christmas. ## FAQ

### How much does a DIY Christmas centerpiece cost?

Most ideas here cost £4 to £20. The cheapest are the cranberry floating candles and the pinecone cluster at £4 to £8, using things you mostly own already. The most expensive are the evergreen tray and the lantern centrepiece at £12 to £20, mainly because of the tray, lantern or candle holders. Greenery from a florist costs a few pounds more than from a garden centre.

### How far in advance can I make a Christmas centerpiece?

It depends on the greenery. Cedar, pine and eucalyptus pieces can be built two weeks before Christmas and still look fresh. Fir and rosemary pieces should be built three to four days before. Dry centrepieces — ornament bowls, gift-box trays, lanterns and bottle-brush cloches — can be made any time in December, because nothing in them wilts.

### What size should a Christmas centerpiece be?

About two-thirds of the table length on a long rectangular table, and no wider than 30 cm. On a round table, keep it under 40 cm across for a 120 cm table. Keep the greenery below 30 cm high so guests can see each other, and let tall thin candles carry the height.

### Are real candles safe in a Christmas centerpiece?

Yes, with care. Keep every flame at least 10 cm from dry greenery, pinecones, paper and ribbon, trim wicks to 6 mm, and never leave a lit candle unattended. Use a proper holder on a heatproof surface. If children or pets are at the table, or the greenery is very dry, use battery candles instead — they cost about £1 each.

### Can I use fresh greenery from my garden?

Yes, and it is the cheapest option. Cedar, pine, fir and rosemary all cut well from a garden, but shake each stem hard outdoors first to drop insects and loose needles, and rinse the stems. Avoid holly with sharp leaves near small children, and never use yew — its berries and leaves are poisonous. If you are unsure what a plant is, buy it from a florist.
