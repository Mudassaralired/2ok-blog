DIY Christmas pinecone decor is the cheapest way to make a home feel like December. A basket of pinecones costs nothing if you gather them from a park or garden, and a bag from a craft store runs about $5 to $8. The 15 projects below span the mantel, the walls, the tree, the table and the front door. Each lists a real cost, a time and a difficulty rating. Read the prep section first.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - All 15 projects cost $0 to $20; most take under two hours.
> - Bake gathered pinecones at 200°F for 20 to 30 minutes first.
> - Use LED candles only: never put a real flame near a pinecone.
> - Frosted and painted pinecones last 2 to 3 seasons if stored dry.
> - Pinecones suit every room, from the mantel to the front door.

## Pinecone Decor for the Mantel

### 1. Classic pinecone mantel garland

Twist pinecones onto jute twine with floral wire, 10 to 15 cm apart, tuck in faux greenery, and drape the garland along the mantel. Cost: $0 to $12. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Gathered cones make it free. The [Christmas mantel decor](/blog/christmas-mantel-decor) guide shows how to layer garlands with stockings and lights.

### 2. Frosted pinecone mantel row

Paint the tips of 10 to 12 medium pinecones white, dust them with fine glitter while the paint is wet, and stand them in a row along the mantel, tallest in the middle. Cost: $4 to $10. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. The frosted tips catch the light and read as snow, even in a warm room.

### 3. Pinecone and LED candle mantel display

Set three LED pillar candles of different heights on the mantel, then ring each one with small pinecones and a few sprigs of faux cedar, keeping the cones clear of the candle. Cost: $8 to $18. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. LED candles are the only safe choice: a real flame beside a dry pinecone is a fire risk.

## Pinecone Wall Decor

### 4. Pinecone wall hanging

Tie a 60 cm length of driftwood horizontally with jute twine, then hang pinecones from it on different lengths of twine so they dangle at staggered heights, . Cost: $3 to $12. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Use 7 to 9 cones in mixed sizes, largest lowest. It needs one picture hook.

### 5. Pinecone wall art panel

Glue pinecones in a tight grid onto a 50 by 70 cm plywood or foam board, filling gaps with small cones, then frame it with slim trim. Cost: $6 to $15. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Dry-fit the layout before gluing: once a cone is glued down, it does not move. It pairs with the [Christmas wall decor ideas](/blog/christmas-wall-decor-ideas) guide.

## Pinecone Decor for the Tree

### 6. Pinecone ornament set

Paint 12 pinecones white and gold or red and silver, screw a small eye hook into the base of each, thread a ribbon loop through it and hang them evenly through the tree. Cost: $2 to $8. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Ribbon loops hang more quietly than metal hooks. The [DIY Christmas ornaments](/blog/diy-christmas-ornaments) guide covers baubles, stars and ribbon ornaments.

### 7. Pinecone tree picks and clusters

Wire two or three small pinecones together with a sprig of faux greenery and a short stem of red berries, then push the stems deep into the tree branches at the outer tips. Cost: $0 to $6. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Make 8 to 10 picks for a 180 cm tree and space them evenly.

## Pinecone Decor for the Table

### 8. Pinecone table centerpiece

Arrange 7 to 9 pinecones of mixed sizes down the centre of a wooden tray or a folded linen runner, tuck in faux greenery and cinnamon sticks, and set two LED pillar candles among them, clear of the cones. Cost: $5 to $15. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. A tray makes the whole piece movable. Keep it low, under 25 cm, so guests can see across the table. The [DIY Christmas centerpiece ideas](/blog/diy-christmas-centerpiece-ideas) guide has taller and greener versions.

### 9. Pinecone place-card holders

Stand one small pinecone upright at each place setting and slide a folded name card between the scales near the top, or glue a thin wire holder to the base and clip the card to it. Cost: $2 to $5. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Choose cones with a flat base so they stand without wobbling. Gold-tipped cones suit a formal dinner; plain cones suit a rustic one.

## Pinecone Decor for the Door and Entryway

### 10. Pinecone Christmas wreath

Glue pinecones onto a 35 cm grapevine or foam wreath form, largest first, then add red berries and a plaid ribbon bow. Cost: $8 to $20. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Use 20 to 25 cones for a full wreath, and test the shape upright as you go. A wire hanger carries the weight better than a nail. The [DIY Christmas wreath ideas](/blog/diy-christmas-wreath-ideas) guide covers more door styles.

### 11. Pinecone door swag

Gather a bundle of faux greenery, 5 pinecones and a few berry stems, bind the stems with floral wire, and hang the swag vertically on the door with a plaid ribbon loop at the top. Cost: $5 to $12. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. A swag is lighter and faster than a wreath, and it suits a narrow door or porch post. Keep the cones at the bottom so the weight hangs straight.

## Pinecone Decor Around the Room

### 12. Pinecone bowl filler

Fill a wide wooden or ceramic bowl with 15 to 20 plain pinecones, then tuck in dried orange slices, cinnamon sticks and small sprigs of faux greenery so the bowl reads as a scented display, not a pile of cones. Cost: $0 to $4. Time: 15 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Put the bowl on a coffee table, a sideboard or an entryway console.

### 13. Pinecone fairy-light jars

Place 3 to 5 small pinecones inside a wide glass jar or hurricane vase, coil a battery fairy-light string around them, and set the jar on a shelf, a windowsill or a bedside table. Cost: $6 to $14. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Use warm-white lights only: cool white makes the cones look grey. Never use a real tea light in a jar with pinecones.

### 14. Pinecone stair garland

Twist pinecones onto a faux greenery garland with floral wire, spacing them every 20 cm, then drape the garland along the banister and tie it at each newel post with ribbon. Cost: $5 to $15. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. A 3-metre garland takes about 15 cones. Tie the garland at three points so it cannot slip.

### 15. Mini pinecone Christmas trees

Glue small pinecones upright onto wooden discs or tiny terracotta pots, then paint the tips white or gold and add a star cut from card on top of the tallest one. Cost: $3 to $10. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Make a set of three in different heights, 10, 15 and 20 cm, and group them on a shelf, a mantel corner or a windowsill. Three mini trees fill a shelf without taking floor space.

## How to Prepare Pinecones for Christmas Decor

Shake each cone hard outside, then soak in warm water with a cup of white vinegar for 20 to 30 minutes. The cones will close up in the water, which is normal. Rinse them, shake off the water, and lay them on a towel to dry for a few hours. Then bake them at 200°F (93°C) on a foil-lined tray for 20 to 30 minutes, checking every 10 minutes. The heat kills insects, melts sap and opens the cones back up. Never bake above 200°F, and never leave the oven unattended: pinecones are resinous and can scorch. Let them cool fully before crafting.

## Scented vs Plain Pinecones

Scented pinecones smell like Christmas the moment you open the bag. Plain ones smell of nothing, but they take paint cleanly and last for years.

| Pinecone type | Scent | Best for | Cost | Lasts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scented, cinnamon oil | Strong, 2 to 3 weeks | Bowls, entryways | $3 to $6 | One season |
| Scented, store-bought | Strong, 4 to 6 weeks | Quick displays | $5 to $8 | One season |
| Plain, natural | None | Painting, wreaths | $0 to $5 | 2 to 3 years |
| Plain, frosted | None | Mantels, trees | $4 to $10 | 2 to 3 years |

Make your own by sealing baked cones in a bag with 10 to 15 drops of cinnamon or pine oil for a week. Keep scented cones away from pets and small children, and never scent cones that will sit near food. If anyone has asthma, use plain cones.

## Where to Put Pinecone Decor Room by Room

In the living room, the mantel garland and tree picks do the heavy lifting, with a bowl filler on the coffee table and the wall art panel above the sofa. In the bedroom, keep it small: a fairy-light jar on the bedside table and three mini trees on a shelf, and avoid scented cones where someone sleeps. In the bathroom, skip real cones near steam and water; a sealed jar on a high shelf is the only safe option. In a child's room, glue large cones firmly in place, never small loose ones within reach of a child under three. Keep the palette calm if the rest of the house is simple: the [minimalist Christmas decor](/blog/minimalist-christmas-decor) guide shows how a few plain cones and white candles can carry a whole room.

## Storing and Reusing Pinecone Decor

Pinecone decor is worth keeping, since most pieces last two to three seasons. Pack cones loosely in a cardboard box with tissue paper between the layers, and store the box somewhere cool and dry, never in a damp garage or a hot attic. Heat warps painted cones and fades glitter, while damp makes cones close up and smell musty.

| Piece | Storage | Reuse life |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mantel garland | Coil loosely in a box | 2 to 3 seasons |
| Frosted and painted cones | Tissue between layers | 2 to 3 seasons |
| Wreath | Hang on a hook in a bag | 2 to 3 seasons |
| Ornament set | Egg box or divided tray | 3 seasons or more |
| Bowl filler cones | Paper bag, dry shelf | 3 seasons or more |
| Fairy-light jars | Leave assembled, remove batteries | 2 seasons |

Before reusing, re-glue loose cones and touch up chipped paint. Scented cones do not keep: bin them after one season.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the bake is the biggest mistake: unbaked cones can bring insects indoors and leak sap. Using a real candle near pinecones is the most dangerous one: dry cones catch fast, so LED candles are the only safe choice. Over-gluing is the quiet killer: a blob of hot glue shows through paint, so use a thin line. Scenting cones before baking traps moisture under the oil, so always bake first. Storing damp cones makes them close up and go musty, so dry them fully first. 

## FAQ

### How do you clean pinecones for Christmas decor?

Shake them outside, soak in warm water and vinegar for 20 to 30 minutes, rinse and dry, then bake at 200°F (93°C) for 20 to 30 minutes. The bake kills insects, melts sap and opens the cones.

### Are pinecones safe near candles?

Only with LED candles. A real flame beside a dry pinecone is a fire risk. Use LED candles, keep cones 5 cm away, and never put a real tea light in a jar with pinecones.

### How long do pinecone Christmas decorations last?

Plain and painted decor lasts 2 to 3 seasons if stored dry and cool. Scented cones last one season. Store pieces loosely with tissue paper, and re-glue or touch up paint before reusing.

### Can I use pinecones I collected outside?

Yes, once cleaned and baked. Shake off dirt, soak in warm water and vinegar, then bake at 200°F for 20 to 30 minutes. Avoid cracked or mouldy cones, and never collect from sprayed or roadside trees.

### How do you make pinecones smell like Christmas?

Seal baked cones in a bag with 10 to 15 drops of cinnamon or pine oil for a week. The scent lasts 2 to 3 weeks. Keep them away from pets and small children, and skip scented cones if anyone has asthma.
