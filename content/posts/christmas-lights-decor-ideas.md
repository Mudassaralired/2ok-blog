Christmas lights decor ideas are the fastest way to make a home feel festive, inside and out. One strand of warm white fairy lights can turn a plain mantel into the warmest seat in the room, and a lit roofline can make the whole street slow down after dark. Here are 15 ideas with real costs, plus two DIY builds and the maths.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every idea here costs $6 to $90, and most take under an hour to hang.
> - Buy 100 lights per foot of tree height and 100 lights per 25 feet of roofline.
> - LED lights cost about 80% less to run than incandescent and stay cool to the touch.
> - Plug outdoor lights into a GFCI outlet and use a timer so they switch off by themselves.
> - Store coiled lights in a labelled box so they work next year.

## Indoor Christmas Lights Decor Ideas

### 1. Fairy light Christmas tree

Wrap warm white fairy lights around the tree from the trunk outward, working from the bottom up, so the glow sits inside the branches instead of draping on the tips. A 6-foot tree needs about 600 lights, and two 300-light strands are easier to handle than one long one. Cost: $18 to $40. Time: 45 minutes. The [Christmas tree decorating ideas](/blog/christmas-tree-decorating-ideas) guide shows how to layer ornaments over a lit tree.

### 2. Warm white mantel garland lights

Weave a strand of warm white lights through a full evergreen garland before you hang it, so the glow sits inside the branches and no bare wire shows. Cost: $15 to $35. Time: 30 minutes. A standard mantel takes 100 to 200 lights. The [Christmas mantel decor](/blog/christmas-mantel-decor) guide layers the same garland with stockings and candles.

### 3. Window curtain fairy lights

Hang a curtain of fairy lights from a tension rod or small clear hooks and let the strands fall straight, so the light reads as a soft sheet rather than a tangle. It turns a plain window into a glowing backdrop. Cost: $12 to $25. Time: 20 minutes. The [Christmas window decor ideas](/blog/christmas-window-decor-ideas) guide pairs the same curtain with paper stars.

### 4. Staircase and banister lights

Wrap a lit garland around the banister, tucking the light strand into the greenery so only the glow shows, and fasten it with green zip ties every 30 cm. Battery lights with a built-in timer are the easiest choice here, since stairs rarely sit near an outlet. Cost: $15 to $30. Time: 30 minutes.

### 5. Shelf and bookcase fairy lights

Run a short battery strand along the back edge of a shelf, behind books and small decorations, so the light washes the wall instead of shining in your eyes. Cost: $8 to $18. Time: 15 minutes. The [DIY fairy light wall decor](/blog/diy-fairy-light-wall-decor) guide uses the same trick on bedroom walls.

## Outdoor Christmas Lights Decor Ideas

### 6. Roofline and gutter lights

Outline the roofline with C9 bulbs on all-in-one clips that grip the gutter or shingle edge, spacing the bulbs 12 inches apart, one per foot. Measure the roofline first and buy one extra strand. Cost: $40 to $90, clips included. Time: 2 to 3 hours. The [Christmas outdoor decor ideas](/blog/christmas-outdoor-decor-ideas) guide plans the whole front of the house around the roofline.

### 7. Porch string lights

String cafe-style bulbs across the porch ceiling or along the beam, hanging them in gentle swags about 30 cm deep, and add a dimmer so the porch glows instead of glaring. Cost: $25 to $60. Time: 1 hour. The [Christmas porch decor](/blog/christmas-porch-decor) guide styles the same porch with garland and lanterns.

![Hands hanging warm white Christmas string lights along a wooden porch railing at dusk, with glowing glass jar lanterns filled with fairy lights on the steps](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/christmas-lights-decor-ideas/in-article-2)

### 8. Walkway and path lights

Line the walkway with stake lights or small lanterns filled with fairy lights, spacing them about 1 metre apart on both sides so the path reads clearly after dark. Solar stakes cost nothing to run but fade on cloudy days; plug-in stakes stay bright all evening. Cost: $20 to $45. Time: 30 minutes.

### 9. Lit wreath on the front door

Wrap a 50-light battery strand through a fresh or faux wreath, tucking the battery pack behind the greenery, and hang it from a wreath hook over the door. It is the most-saved front-door look on Pinterest, and it takes ten minutes. Cost: $25 to $55. Time: 10 minutes. The [Christmas front door decor](/blog/christmas-front-door-decor) guide builds the whole entry around the same wreath.

### 10. Wrapped trees and shrubs

Wrap the trunk and main branches of a small tree with a strand of mini lights, working upward in a spiral and spacing each turn about 15 cm apart. For shrubs, drape a net light over the top and tuck the edges under the branches. Cost: $15 to $35 per tree. Time: 30 minutes per tree.

## DIY Builds and Small-Space Glow

### 11. Glowing jar lanterns

Drop a short battery fairy light strand into a clean glass jar, add a pine sprig and a cinnamon stick, and set three jars of different heights on the porch steps or the mantel. The glass softens the light and each jar glows like a little lantern. Cost: $6 to $15 for three. Time: 15 minutes. Use a timer so the jars switch on at dusk.

### 12. Lit garland build

Build a lit garland from a plain faux garland, one 100-light warm white strand and green zip ties. Lay the garland flat, weave the light strand through the branches in an S-shape, and fasten it with a zip tie every 30 cm so the lights stay buried in the greenery. Cost: $20 to $40. Time: 45 minutes. Hang it with two sturdy hooks. The [Christmas garland ideas](/blog/christmas-garland-ideas) guide uses the same garland across the mantel, stairs and doorway.

### 13. Balcony and renter-friendly lights

Hang fairy lights with clear Command hooks and suction-cup clips, which come off cleanly in January and leave no holes for a landlord to find. Drape the lights along the railing, around a potted plant, or across the balcony ceiling in a zigzag. Cost: $10 to $25. Time: 20 minutes.

### 14. Lighted Christmas village window display

Set a row of small ceramic houses along the windowsill, run a fairy light strand behind them, and add a pinch of fake snow at the base. From the street the houses read as a glowing village, and from inside they frame the window like a night light. Cost: $15 to $30. Time: 20 minutes. Keep the lights behind the houses, not in front, and use a timer.

### 15. Table centrepiece with fairy lights

Lay a runner down the centre of the table, set three glass vases or jars of different heights along it, coil a short fairy light strand inside each one, and tuck pine sprigs and pinecones between them. Cost: $8 to $20. Time: 15 minutes. The [Christmas table decor](/blog/christmas-table-decor) guide styles the same table with matching place settings.

## How Many Lights Do You Need

The most common Christmas lights mistake is buying too few, so measure before you shop. The rule for trees is 100 lights per foot of height: a 6-foot tree takes 600 lights, a 7-foot tree takes 700, and an 8-foot tree takes 800 to 1,000 for a full look. For a mantel, 100 to 200 lights woven through the garland is enough, because the greenery spreads the glow. Along a roofline, C9 bulbs sit 12 inches apart and mini lights about 3 inches apart.

| Spot | Lights to buy | Rule of thumb |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6-foot tree | 600 | 100 lights per foot of height |
| 7-foot tree | 700 | 100 lights per foot of height |
| 8-foot tree | 800–1,000 | 100–125 lights per foot for a full look |
| Standard mantel | 100–200 | Weave through garland, not along the front |
| Roofline, C9 bulbs | 1 per foot | Spaced 12 inches apart |
| Roofline, mini lights | 100 per 25 ft | Spaced about 3 inches apart |
| 60 cm wreath | 50–100 | One short battery strand |


## What Christmas Lights Cost to Run

LED lights cost about 80% less to run than the old incandescent kind, and they stay cool to the touch, which makes them safer around garland and curtains. The maths is simple: watts × hours ÷ 1,000 × your electricity rate. A 100-light LED strand draws about 8 watts, while a 100-light incandescent strand draws about 40 watts. Run either one for 6 hours a day over 30 days and the LED strand costs about $0.24 a month at $0.17 per kWh, while the incandescent strand costs about $1.22.

| Display | LED watts | LED cost per month | Incandescent cost per month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 lights | 8 W | $0.24 | $1.22 |
| 600-light tree | 48 W | $1.47 | $7.34 |
| 2,000-light display | 160 W | $4.90 | $24.48 |

A whole-house LED display of 2,000 lights costs under $5 a month; the same display in incandescent costs nearly $25.

## Outdoor Safety, Timers and Smart Controls

Plug every outdoor strand into a GFCI outlet, the kind with the test and reset buttons, which cuts the power in a fraction of a second if water gets in. If your outdoor outlets are not GFCI, fit a GFCI adapter for about $15 before you hang a single light. Use outdoor-rated extension cords only, keep every connection off the ground and out of puddles, and never run a cord under a rug or through a doorway that pinches it.

On a ladder, keep three points of contact at all times, never lean sideways to reach one more clip, and move the ladder instead. Hang lights in daylight, never at dusk when you cannot see the rungs. Never hang lights in rain, high wind or icy conditions.

A timer is the single best buy in Christmas lighting: a simple outdoor timer costs $10 to $15 and switches the display on at dusk and off at midnight.

## Takedown and Storage

Take the lights down on a dry day in early January, while you still remember how they were hung. Test each strand as you take it down and throw away any strand with a cracked bulb, a frayed wire or a dead section. Coil each strand around your elbow into a loose loop, secure it with a twist tie, and label the coil with masking tape: tree, mantel, roofline. Store the coils in a clear plastic box with a lid, labels facing up, indoors rather than in a damp garage.

## Mistakes to Avoid

The biggest mistake is buying too few lights: a sparse tree looks unfinished, so measure first and use the light-count table. The second is mixing warm white and cool white in one display, which makes the room look like two different houses. The third is using indoor extension cords outside, which crack in the cold and let water in. The fourth is stapling lights to the roofline, which pierces the wire. The fifth is hanging lights from a ladder in the dark or in the rain.

## FAQ

### How many Christmas lights do I need for a 6-foot tree?

About 600 lights, using the rule of 100 lights per foot of tree height. Two 300-light strands are easier to handle than one long one

### How much does it cost to run Christmas lights for a month?

A 600-light LED tree costs about $1.47 a month at $0.17 per kWh for 6 hours a night, while the same tree in incandescent costs about $7.34.

### Are LED Christmas lights really cheaper than incandescent?

Yes. An LED strand draws about a fifth of the power of an incandescent strand, so it costs about 80% less to run.

### Can I hang Christmas lights outside without an outdoor outlet?

Yes, with battery lights or solar lights. Battery fairy lights with a timer cover a wreath, a balcony railing or a jar lantern, and solar stakes light a walkway.

### How do I store Christmas lights so they work next year?

Coil each strand loosely around your elbow, secure it with a twist tie, label it with masking tape, and store the coils in a clear lidded box indoors.
