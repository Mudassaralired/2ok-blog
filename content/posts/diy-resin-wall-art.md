DIY resin wall art turns a plain board into a glossy panel that catches the light. These 15 wall-only projects each carry a real cost, time and difficulty rating, plus the resin, safety and hanging rules short lists never give you. Every project needs 72 hours curing after the active work.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every project costs £9 to £38, and most take under three hours of active work.
> - Epoxy is best for wall art; UV resin suits small details, polyester is for confident makers only.
> - Wear nitrile gloves and goggles, ventilate the room, and never pour resin near food.
> - Pour in thin layers, pop bubbles with a heat gun, and give pieces a full 72 hours to cure.
> - Resin art is heavy, so hang pieces over 2 kg on French cleats or wall plugs, never strips.

## Ocean and Geode Resin Art

### 1. Ocean wave panel

Pour blue, turquoise and white resin across a panel, drag a heat gun through the white for foam-like lacing. Cost: £14 to £24. Time: 2 hours active. Difficulty: easy. A 60 × 90 cm panel weighs 2.5 kg, so use two screw hooks.

### 2. Geode panel with crystals

Ring a round board's centre with crushed quartz, pour dark resin at the edge and pale resin inside, then add gold leaf veins. Cost: £18 to £32. Time: 3 hours active. Difficulty: moderate. A 50 cm round panel weighs 2 kg, so use one D-ring hanger.

### 3. Beach scene panel

Spread sand along the bottom third, set shells and pebbles into it, then pour clear resin over the top. Cost: £12 to £20. Time: 2 hours active. Difficulty: easy. A 40 × 60 cm panel weighs 1.5 kg, so use one screw hook. The [DIY concrete wall decor](/blog/diy-concrete-wall-decor) guide shows textured bases under smooth finishes.

### 4. Mermaid shimmer panel

Mix pearlescent mica in teal, lilac and silver, pour in soft overlapping waves, and dust fine glitter over the wet surface. Cost: £15 to £26. Time: 2 hours active. Difficulty: easy. A 50 × 70 cm panel weighs 2 kg, so use two screw hooks.

## Botanical and Nature Resin Art

### 5. Pressed flower resin panel

Arrange pressed flowers on a painted board, seal them with a thin brush coat, then pour a clear layer over the top. Cost: £11 to £19. Time: 2 hours active. Difficulty: easy. A 40 × 50 cm panel weighs 1.2 kg, so use one screw hook. The [DIY dried flower wall decor](/blog/diy-dried-flower-wall-decor) guide lists flowers that press flat without browning.

### 6. Dried fern panel

Lay a dried fern and eucalyptus sprigs on a cream board, pin them flat with dots of clear resin, then flood the panel with a clear coat. Cost: £9 to £16. Time: 1 to 2 hours active. Difficulty: easy. A 30 × 40 cm panel weighs under 1 kg, so a large adhesive hook holds it.

### 7. Wood slice resin panel

Take a sanded wood slice with bark on the edge, fill its cracks with gold-tinted resin, then coat the face so the grain shines through like glass. Cost: £13 to £22. Time: 2 hours active. Difficulty: moderate. A 40 cm slice weighs 1.5 kg, so use one screw hook.

### 8. Seashell mosaic panel

Glue pebbles and small shells into a loose wave pattern on a board, then pour clear resin over them in two thin layers. Cost: £10 to £18. Time: 2 hours active. Difficulty: easy. A 40 × 60 cm panel weighs 2 kg, so use one D-ring hanger.

## Colour, Marble and Abstract Resin Art

### 9. Marble swirl panel

Pour white resin, then trail thin ribbons of grey and gold through it with a stick, tilting slowly until the veins look like real marble. Cost: £14 to £23. Time: 2 hours active. Difficulty: easy. ### 10. Alcohol ink abstract panel

### 12. Galaxy resin panel

## Functional and Statement Resin Pieces

### 13. Resin wall clock

Pour a marble or ocean resin face onto a round board, drill a centre hole once cured, and fit a quartz clock movement with gold hands. Cost: £19 to £32. Time: 2 to 3 hours active. Difficulty: moderate. A 40 cm clock weighs 1.5 kg, so use one screw hook, and keep it out of steamy rooms so the movement stays dry.

### 14. Resin-coated mirror frame

Paint a plain wooden mirror frame, brush two thin resin coats over it, and sprinkle gold leaf flakes into the second coat while wet. Cost: £17 to £30. Time: 2 hours active. Difficulty: moderate. A 50 × 70 cm framed mirror weighs 3 kg, so hang it on a French cleat.

### 15. Layered resin triptych

Pour three matching panels in one colour story, varying the pattern on each, then hang them in a row with 5 cm gaps. Cost: £24 to £38. Time: 3 to 4 hours active. Difficulty: moderate. Each 30 × 60 cm panel weighs 1.2 kg, so use one screw hook per panel. The [gallery wall layouts](/blog/gallery-wall-layouts) guide shows how to space a three-panel row.

## Choosing Your Resin

Epoxy resin is the right choice for almost every project here. It cures at room temperature, pours clear, and gives you 30 to 45 minutes of working time — enough to mix colours, pour and pop bubbles without rushing. Most brands mix at a 1:1 or 2:1 ratio by volume, so follow your own bottle: a wrong mix stays sticky forever. Buy UV-stabilised epoxy to slow sun yellowing.

UV resin cures in minutes under a UV lamp, but only in thin layers and at far more cost per millilitre — use it for small details. Polyester resin is cheaper and cures fast, but it smells strongly, needs a catalyst, shrinks and yellows sooner, so it suits confident makers. A starter kit costs about £25 to £45.

## Resin Safety First

Wear nitrile gloves every time you touch uncured resin, and safety goggles when you pour or use a torch. Work with a window open and a fan pulling air across your table, and wear a respirator with organic vapour cartridges if you pour often. Never pour resin near food, drinks or a cooking area, and keep children and pets out until the piece has cured. If resin touches your skin, wash it off with warm water and soap — never use acetone or thinners on skin.

## Mixing, Pouring and Removing Bubbles

Measure resin and hardener exactly, by volume, in separate marked cups. Pour the hardener into the resin, then stir slowly for 3 to 5 minutes, scraping the sides and bottom. Stirring too fast whips air in, so keep it slow, then rest the cup 5 minutes so the first bubbles rise. Pour in thin layers of 3 to 6 mm, and let each layer go tacky before adding the next, or the colours will sink and blur together.

Seal porous surfaces first: wood, paper and dried flowers trap air, so brush on a thin resin coat and let it set. Pop surface bubbles with a heat gun or torch held 15 cm above the resin, sweeping in quick passes within the first 30 minutes.

## Curing Times

Resin feels dry after 8 to 12 hours, but it is not ready. Leave every piece untouched for at least 24 hours before handling, and give it a full 72 hours before hanging, drilling or adding hardware. Cold rooms slow curing badly — keep the room between 20 and 25°C. Cover the piece loosely with a cardboard box so dust cannot settle, but leave a gap for air. If a piece stays tacky after 72 hours, the mix ratio was wrong: scrape the soft layer off, sand lightly, and pour a fresh thin coat over it.

## Sealing and Finishing

A cured resin panel is already sealed — the resin is its own waterproof top coat — so finishing is mostly about the edges and the shine. Sand any drips off the sides with 400-grit paper, then work up through 800 and 1200 grit for a smooth edge, and wipe the dust away. For extra gloss, pour one final thin clear coat over the face and cure it for another 72 hours. For a matt edge against a glossy face, sand the sides only. A UV-protective spray slows yellowing and takes five minutes to apply.

## Sizing and Layout

Resin art is visually heavy — dark, glossy and dense — so size it generously or it looks lost. A single panel should cover about two-thirds of the furniture below it: above a 180 cm sofa, aim for about 120 cm of art. Hang the centre at about 145 cm, average eye level, and keep 15 cm clear above a sofa. For a triptych, plan the layout on the floor first, then transfer it from the centre panel, keeping 5 cm gaps. One resin piece per wall is usually enough. ## Hanging Heavy Pieces by Wall Type

Weigh the piece first, then pick a fixing rated for twice that weight. Resin is heavy: a 60 × 90 cm panel with a 3 mm layer weighs about 2.5 kg before the board, and a framed mirror can reach 3 kg. Adhesive strips are only safe under 1 kg. On brick or block walls, use one or two screw hooks into masonry wall plugs. On plasterboard, use toggle bolts or a French cleat screwed into a stud. A French cleat is the safest choice over 3 kg.

| Weight | Examples | Best fixing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 1 kg | Fern, flower panels | Large adhesive hook |
| 1–2 kg | Clock, geode, marble panels | 1 screw hook into a wall plug |
| Over 2 kg | Ocean, triptych, mirror | French cleat or 2 screw hooks |

## Renter-Friendly Methods

Resin art and rented walls can get along, as long as you pick the light pieces. The fern panel and the small pressed flower panel weigh under 1 kg, so removable adhesive hooks hold them and peel off cleanly. Test one hook in a hidden spot first, stick it to a clean, dry wall, and wait an hour before loading it. For heavier pieces, lean them on a sideboard or mantel, or rest a wide panel on a picture ledge. Never drill a rented wall without written permission. The [renter friendly wall decor](/blog/renter-friendly-wall-decor) guide covers more no-damage hanging methods.

## Mistakes to Avoid

The biggest mistake is the wrong mix ratio — measure by volume, not by eye, and stir for the full 3 to 5 minutes, or the piece stays tacky. The second is pouring too thick: a deep pour overheats, cracks and can yellow in the middle, so keep layers to 3 to 6 mm. The third is skipping the seal coat, which lets bubbles rise for hours. The fourth is curing in a cold room. The fifth is hanging a heavy panel on adhesive strips. The sixth is torching too close: hold the flame 15 cm away and keep it moving.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY resin wall art cost?

Most projects cost £9 to £38: a dried fern panel £9 to £16, a marble swirl panel £14 to £23, and a layered triptych £24 to £38.

### Which resin is best for wall art?

Epoxy resin is best: it cures at room temperature, pours clear and gives 30 to 45 minutes of working time. UV resin suits small details only.

### How long does resin wall art take to cure?

Resin feels dry after 8 to 12 hours, can be handled after 24 hours, and needs a full 72 hours before hanging or drilling. Keep the room at 20 to 25°C.

### Is resin wall art safe to make at home?

Yes: wear nitrile gloves and safety goggles, ventilate the room, keep resin away from food and children, and wash skin with warm water and soap if it touches you.

### How do I stop bubbles in resin wall art?

Seal porous surfaces with a thin resin coat first, stir slowly for 3 to 5 minutes, and pop surface bubbles with a heat gun or torch held 15 cm above the resin within 30 minutes.

### How do I hang heavy resin wall art?

Weigh the piece first and use a fixing rated for twice that weight. Under 1 kg, adhesive hooks are fine; 1 to 2 kg needs a screw hook into a wall plug; over 2 kg, use a French cleat.
