A woven wall hanging is the fastest way to add warmth and texture to a blank wall. Shop-bought pieces cost £45 to £120; most DIY versions cost £8 to £30, and a first project takes about three hours. This guide covers fifteen buildable ideas, with real costs, times, skill levels, a ten-minute loom build and renter-friendly hanging rules.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - A first woven wall hanging costs £8 to £15 and takes about 3 hours; a large driftwood piece costs £20 to £30.
> - A cardboard loom costs nothing and takes 10 minutes to build — start there, not with a bought frame loom.
> - Size the hanging to one-third to one-half of the empty wall width, and hang the centre at 145 to 150 cm.
> - Wool yarn gives the softest texture; cotton yarn holds crisp geometric patterns better.
> - Pieces under 1 kg hang safely on one adhesive hook, so renters need no drilling at all.

## What Is a Woven Wall Hanging (and How It Differs From Macramé)

A woven wall hanging is yarn, wool or cotton cord woven over and under vertical warp threads on a loom, then hung from a dowel, branch or driftwood mount. The loom holds the warp taut while you pass the weft yarn across it, row by row, making a flat, textured panel with a fringe.

Macramé is different: knotting cords into square knots and half knots, with no loom and no warp. Weaving is interlacing yarn over and under threads on a loom. A woven piece is denser and softer, and holds colour blocks and patterns more cleanly; a knotted piece has open, lacy gaps. If you love the knotted look, see our [macrame wall hanging](/blog/macrame-wall-hanging/) guide instead. Cream wool fits Scandinavian and farmhouse rooms; terracotta, mustard and sage fits boho rooms. A wonky row can be pulled out and rewoven in seconds.

## Supplies You'll Need: Yarn, Looms, Dowels and Driftwood

Buy the yarn first, because it decides the look. Chunky wool yarn (£3 to £5 a ball) gives soft, fluffy texture and weaves fast — the best choice for a first project. Cotton yarn (£2 to £4 a ball) is smoother, holds crisp stripes and shapes, and suits the patterned ideas below. Roving, about £6 a hank, makes thick, cloud-like accent rows.

You need a loom, but you do not need to buy one yet. A cardboard loom costs nothing and suits every beginner project here; a wooden frame loom (£12 to £18) is worth it only for a second piece. An embroidery hoop (£3 to £5) doubles as a round loom. A wooden dowel (£2) gives a clean top edge, while a driftwood branch (free) gives a relaxed boho look — wash and dry it first. Keep a tapestry needle (about £2), a fork and sharp scissors on the table.

## How to Build a Simple DIY Loom in 10 Minutes

Cut a rectangle of sturdy cardboard, 30 cm wide by 40 cm tall. Cut notches, 1 cm apart and 1 cm deep, along the top and bottom edges — they hold the warp threads evenly spaced, the secret of a neat weave.

Tie the warp thread to the first top notch, run it down to the matching bottom notch, loop it round the back, and come up to the next top notch. Repeat across the width until every notch is filled, keeping the thread taut but not so tight it bends the cardboard. Tie off at the last notch. Your loom is ready. Weave from the bottom up, leave 10 cm of warp free at the top and bottom, and pack each row down with a fork.

## 5 Beginner Weaving Techniques to Learn First

Learn these five before you start. Practise each one in a small sample — ten rows is enough — before a full piece.

**Plain weave** is the foundation: pass the weft over one warp thread, under the next, and alternate on the next row. Keep the weft slightly arched as you lay it in — pulling it straight pulls the piece into an hourglass shape. **Soumak** wraps the weft around each warp thread in turn, making a raised diagonal braid like a rope; it is best for strong horizontal bands.

**Rya knots** are the shaggy texture in most boho hangings. Fold a short piece of yarn in half, loop it around two warp threads, pull the ends through, repeat in a row and trim even. **Twining** twists two weft yarns around each warp thread, holding the spacing firm — use it at the top and bottom edges to stop unravelling. **Yarn painting** means weaving short weft pieces only where you want a colour or shape, such as a hill or a sun.

## 15 DIY Woven Wall Hanging Ideas

The ideas run from easiest to hardest. Start with one of the first six if you have never woven before.

### 1. Classic cream fringe hanging

Plain weave in cream chunky wool on a 30 cm cardboard loom, with a rya-knot band and a long cut fringe. Cost: about £10. Time: 3 hours. Skill: beginner.

### 2. Three-stripe boho hanging

Three wide stripes — terracotta, mustard and sage — in plain weave, separated by thin soumak bands in cream. Cost: £12. Time: 3 hours. Skill: beginner.

### 3. Driftwood mini hanging

A small 25 cm weave on a cardboard loom, hung from a foraged driftwood branch with jute cord. Cost: about £8. Time: 2 hours. Skill: beginner.

### 4. Chunky roving statement piece

Two or three thick roving rows in cream and blush, woven loosely over a plain-weave base so the wool puffs out. Cost: £15. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Skill: beginner.

### 5. Rainbow kids' room hanging

Six narrow rainbow stripes on a 30 cm loom, with a short fringe and a painted dowel. Cost: £12. Time: 3 hours. Skill: beginner.

### 6. Neutral textured sampler

One band each of plain weave, soumak, rya knots and twining, in cream, beige and grey. Cost: £10. Time: 3 hours. Skill: beginner.

### 7. Round hoop wall hanging

Weave a circle on a 30 cm embroidery hoop: warp threads radiate from the centre like spokes, and the weft spirals round them. Cost: £10. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Skill: intermediate.

### 8. Geometric diamond wall hanging

Cream base with a large terracotta diamond in yarn painting, outlined in soumak. Cost: £14. Time: 4 hours. Skill: intermediate.

### 9. Half-moon sunset hanging

A semicircle of mustard, rust and blush yarn painting that reads as a setting sun over a cream sky. Cost: £14. Time: 4 hours. Skill: intermediate. It pairs with a [tapestry wall hanging](/blog/tapestry-wall-hanging/) in the same colours.

### 10. Woven wall hanging with tassels

Plain-weave panel with chunky tassels along the bottom edge and two on the dowel ends. Cost: £12. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Skill: intermediate.

### 11. Two-tone Scandinavian hanging

Black and white stripes and triangles on a 40 cm loom, hung from a pale beech dowel. Cost: £12. Time: 4 hours. Skill: intermediate.

### 12. Large driftwood statement piece

A 60 cm wide weave in cream, rust and sage, hung from a 75 cm driftwood branch. Cost: £20 to £25. Time: 5 hours. Skill: intermediate.

### 13. Layered fringe wall hanging

Three overlapping panels — wide, medium and small — hung from one dowel at different lengths. Cost: £20. Time: 5 hours. Skill: confident.

### 14. Woven wall hanging with wooden beads

Plain-weave panel with wooden beads threaded onto the warp fringe and hanging cords. Cost: £15. Time: 4 hours. Skill: confident.

### 15. Mountain landscape wall hanging

A yarn-painted scene — cream sky, sage hills, rust sun — on a 45 cm loom, with a soumak horizon and rya-knot grass. Cost: £18. Time: 5 to 6 hours. Skill: confident.

## Choosing Colors and Sizes for Your Room

Pull two or three colours from the room itself — the sofa cushions, a rug, a curtain — and make one the dominant yarn, about 60 percent of the piece. Cream or natural wool is the safest dominant colour, because it matches everything and lets one accent, terracotta or sage, do the work. In a dark room, use lighter yarns so the piece does not disappear; in a bright white room, add a deep rust or charcoal band. For a full boho look, see our [boho wall decor ideas](/blog/boho-wall-decor-ideas/) guide, which uses the same palette.

Size follows the same rule as most wall art: the hanging should span one-third to one-half of the empty wall width. A 30 cm piece suits a 90 cm stretch beside a door; a 60 cm piece suits a 150 cm wall over a sofa. Hang the centre at eye level, 145 to 150 cm from the floor, as in our [DIY wall decor ideas](/blog/diy-wall-decor-ideas/) hub, and leave 15 to 20 cm between the fringe and the furniture below.

## How to Hang a Woven Wall Hanging (Renter-Friendly, No Damage)

Weigh the finished piece on kitchen scales first — weight decides the fixing, not size. Most woven hangings weigh under 1 kg; only large driftwood pieces reach 1 to 2 kg.

- **Under 1 kg** (hoop hangings, minis, small fringe pieces): one adhesive hook rated for 1 kg, or a single picture nail.
- **1 to 2 kg** (large driftwood pieces, layered hangings): two adhesive hooks 20 cm apart, or one picture hook rated for 2 kg with a wall plug.
- **Over 2 kg**: two screws into wall plugs or a stud, 30 cm apart, with the cord looped over both so the piece cannot twist.

For a dowel mount, tie a cord to each end and bring the ends together in a loop at the centre — the loop goes over the hook. Renters can hang every piece here without drilling, as long as it weighs under 2 kg. Press adhesive strips onto a clean, dry wall for 30 seconds each, wait an hour before hanging, and pull the strip straight down when moving out. ## Care Tips: Keeping Yarn Art Dust-Free and Looking New

Shake the piece gently outdoors once a month, then brush it lightly with a soft clothes brush. A hairdryer on cool and low, held 30 cm away, lifts dust out of rya knots and roving rows. Keep woven hangings out of direct sunlight, which fades the yarn, and out of kitchens and bathrooms, where steam and grease dull it. If a fringe tangles, comb it with a wide-tooth comb and trim the ends straight; never pull a loose thread — snip it flush. Moths are the one real risk for pure wool: store spare yarn sealed, and air the hanging outdoors twice a year.

## FAQ

### Is a woven wall hanging the same as macramé?

No. Weaving passes yarn over and under warp threads on a loom; macramé ties cords into knots with no loom. Woven pieces are denser and hold colour blocks better, while macramé is lacier and more open.

### How long does a beginner woven wall hanging take?

About 2 to 3 hours for a small 30 cm piece, including the cardboard loom. A large 60 cm driftwood piece takes 4 to 5 hours.

### How much does it cost to make a woven wall hanging?

£8 to £15 for a first small piece, including yarn, warp thread and a dowel. A large statement piece costs £20 to £30.

### What yarn is best for a woven wall hanging?

Chunky wool yarn for a first project — soft, forgiving and fast to weave. Cotton yarn is better for crisp geometric patterns, and roving adds thick, cloud-like texture in accent rows.

### How do I hang a woven wall hanging without damaging the wall?

Use adhesive hooks rated for the piece's weight — most woven hangings are under 1 kg, so one 1 kg hook is enough. Press the strip onto a clean, dry wall, wait an hour, then hang the piece.
