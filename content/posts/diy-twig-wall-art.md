DIY twig wall art turns garden or park branches into a wall feature that costs almost nothing. Every project below is a wall piece only, with a real cost, time and difficulty rating. This is garden twig craft, not beach driftwood: the wood is fresh-cut, so it needs drying and a bug check first — see our [DIY driftwood wall decor](/blog/diy-driftwood-wall-decor) guide for salt-worn beach wood.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - All 15 projects cost $0 to $30; most cost under $8.
> - Pick straight, dry twigs 1 to 3 cm thick: willow and hazel bend best.
> - Lashing with jute twine holds better than glue on round, uneven twigs.
> - Most pieces weigh under 2 kg and hang on one or two picture hooks.
> - Use battery LED fairy lights only: never put a real flame near dry twigs.

## Classic Twig Wall Art

### 1. Twig ladder wall hanging

Lash five or six horizontal twigs between two long uprights into a ladder about 60 by 90 cm, hung flat on the wall. Cost: $0 to $4. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 2. Twig star wall art

Lay five straight twigs, each about 40 cm long, into a five-pointed star and lash every crossing with jute twine. Cost: $0 to $3. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Keep the points slightly uneven.

### 3. Twig heart wall art

Bend two flexible willow twigs into a heart outline about 40 cm wide, lash the join at the bottom point, and add short cross twigs inside. Cost: $0 to $3. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. It pairs well with the [DIY dried flower wall decor](/blog/diy-dried-flower-wall-decor) guide.

### 4. Twig circle wall wreath

Bend long, flexible twigs into a 45 cm circle, bind the overlaps with twine, and weave shorter twigs around the ring until it looks full. Cost: $0 to $5. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

## Statement Twig Pieces

### 5. Large branch wall hanging

Mount one dramatic branch, 90 to 120 cm long, horizontally on the wall with two brackets, then hang strands of twine, beads and dried flowers from it at different lengths. Cost: $2 to $8. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Pick a branch with interesting forks.

### 6. Twig sunburst wall art

Cut 20 to 30 straight twigs to lengths from 30 to 60 cm and fan them out from a central wooden disc like rays of the sun. Cost: $3 to $9. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

![Hands lashing thin twigs together with jute twine on a wooden worktable outdoors, with pruning shears and twine spools nearby](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-twig-wall-art/in-article-1)

### 7. Woven twig lattice panel

Weave thin, flexible twigs over and under a frame of thicker twigs to make a lattice panel about 60 by 80 cm. Cost: $4 to $10. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Soak the weaving twigs overnight.

### 8. Twig tree silhouette

Build a tree shape from one thick trunk twig, three or four branch twigs and dozens of thin offshoots, fixed to a painted board or straight onto the wall with small nails. Cost: $2 to $8. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Hang small photos from the offshoots. For spacing, see the [above sofa wall decor](/blog/above-sofa-wall-decor) guide.

## Small-Space and Bedroom Twig Art

### 9. Twig photo clip wall

Fix a straight branch 80 cm long horizontally on the wall, then clip photos to it with small wooden pegs on short twine loops. Cost: $1 to $5. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Use two screws into wall plugs.

### 10. Twig jewellery wall hanger

Mount a branch with plenty of small forks horizontally on a bedroom wall and hang necklaces, bracelets and earrings from the forks. Cost: $0 to $4. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Pick forks pointing slightly upward.

### 11. Mini twig frame trio

Make three small square frames, 20 cm across, from short twigs lashed at the corners, and hang them in a row with a small print or pressed leaf inside each. Cost: $2 to $6. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Keep the frames the same size, 5 cm apart. See the [DIY wall decor ideas](/blog/diy-wall-decor-ideas) pillar.

## Seasonal and Lit Twig Art

### 12. Twig fairy-light star

Build the star from project 2, then wrap a 2-metre battery LED fairy light string around the twigs, tucking the battery pack behind the top point. Cost: $4 to $9. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 13. Twig Christmas tree wall art

Arrange twigs in horizontal rows, longest at the bottom and shortest at the top, on a wall or a board to form a flat Christmas tree about 90 cm tall, with a small star on top. Cost: $2 to $7. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. It matches the [DIY rope wall decor](/blog/diy-rope-wall-decor) guide.

### 14. Autumn twig leaf wall hanging

Lash a horizontal branch to the wall and hang short twine strands from it, each carrying a dried autumn leaf, a pine cone or a small twig bundle. Cost: $1 to $5. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 15. Twig snowflake wall art

Cross six straight twigs through a central point to make a six-armed snowflake about 50 cm across, lash the centre tightly, and add short side twigs near the arm tips. Cost: $0 to $4. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

## Which Twigs to Pick and How to Prep Them

The twig decides how the piece looks and lasts. Willow and hazel bend without snapping, dry straight, and keep their bark. Birch is pale but stiff: use it for straight pieces like stars. Oak and maple are strong and rigid, good for ladders and frames. Pine is sappy and sticky: it glues badly and drops resin, so skip it.

| Twig | Best for | Flexibility | Drying time | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Willow | Hearts, wreaths | Very high | 1 to 2 weeks | Soak first |
| Hazel | Ladders, stars | High | 1 to 2 weeks | Straight growth |
| Birch | Stars, snowflakes | Low | 1 week | Pale bark |
| Oak | Ladders, shelves | Very low | 2 to 3 weeks | Heavy |
| Maple | Frames, lattice | Low | 2 weeks | Smooth bark |
| Apple or cherry | Feature branches | Medium | 2 weeks | Ask a pruner |

Collect twigs after a storm or from pruning piles, never by cutting live branches off a neighbour's tree without asking. Choose twigs 1 to 3 cm thick, and check every one for bugs: look for tiny holes, fine sawdust and soft bark. Scrub with a stiff brush, wash in warm soapy water, and dry flat for one to two weeks, turning every few days. Strip loose bark, or seal it with clear matt varnish, about $6 a can. Garden twigs need this drying and bug check: driftwood arrives already weathered and salt-cleaned.

## Lashing vs Glue: Which Joint Holds

Twigs are round, uneven and often slightly waxy, so glue has almost no flat contact to grip: the bond peels away. Lashing works because it wraps the joint and squeezes the twigs together. Use jute twine: wrap it tightly around the crossing at least six times, tie it off with a square knot, and add a dab of glue on the knot only. For heavy pieces, use both: lash the joint, then add a dot of wood glue where the twigs touch. Glue alone is fine for light decoration on a twig that is already lashed. Never trust glue alone on a structural joint: if you can slide the joint with your fingers, wrap it again.

## Sizing Your Twig Wall Art

Size the piece to the wall, not to the twigs you happen to have. Above a sofa, the piece should span about two thirds of the sofa width: a 180 cm sofa takes a 120 cm branch hanging, and a star or wreath 60 to 80 cm across. In a hallway, keep the piece under 30 cm deep from the wall, and choose a flat piece like the frame trio. Lay the finished piece on the floor first and photograph it from the doorway. Hang the piece so its centre sits at eye level, about 145 cm from the floor, or 15 to 20 cm above furniture.

## Hanging Twig Wall Art by Weight

Twig art is heavier than it looks: a large branch hanging can weigh 2 to 3 kg, while a small star weighs under 300 g. Weigh the finished piece before choosing hardware, and always hang from two points on pieces over 1 kg. Use a picture wire across the back, not a single loop of twine, which stretches and lets the piece tilt.

| Finished weight | Hardware | Fixings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 500 g | Picture nail or adhesive hook | 1 |
| 500 g to 1.5 kg | Two picture hooks, wire back | 2 |
| 1.5 kg to 3 kg | Two 3 kg hooks, wire back | 2 |
| Over 3 kg | Two screws into wall plugs or a stud | 2 |

Renters should favour picture hooks over nails. Never hang a piece over 1 kg from a single adhesive hook, or any twig piece from a bare nail in drywall. Check the fixings every six months.

## Fairy Lights and Twig Safety

Dry twigs are kindling, so light safety is not optional on a lit twig piece. Use battery-powered LED fairy lights only: LEDs stay cool, and a battery pack cannot overload a socket. Never use mains fairy lights on twigs, and never put a candle or tea light near a twig piece. Keep the lights on a timer so they switch off after four to six hours, and switch them off when you leave the house. Check the battery pack and wire monthly for heat or damage, and replace any frayed string at once: a short circuit around dry wood is the one real fire risk. Keep lit twig pieces away from curtains, bedding and radiators.

## What DIY Twig Wall Art Costs

Twig wall art is one of the cheapest wall decor crafts because the main material is free. The money goes on twine, hooks and lights: jute twine about $3 a ball, picture hooks about $4 a pack, and a 2-metre battery LED string about $5. A glue gun costs about $8 once, and varnish about $6 a can. Size and light drive the cost: a small star costs $0 to $3, while a large lit branch hanging costs $12 to $30.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the drying step is the biggest mistake: a green twig warps as it dries, loosens its lashings and drops bark. Trusting glue alone on a structural joint is the second: it looks solid on day one and falls apart in a month. Using pine twigs is the third: the resin stains the wall and the glue never sets. Hanging a heavy piece from one hook is the fourth: the piece tilts, then falls. Mixing too many twig species in one piece is the fifth: willow, birch and oak have different colours and bark, so use one species per piece. Forgetting the bug check is the sixth: wood-boring beetles come indoors on unwashed twigs. And using real candles near a twig piece matters most: battery LEDs give the same glow with none of the risk.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY twig wall art cost?

Most projects cost $0 to $10: a star or heart $0 to $3, a ladder $1 to $5, and a large branch hanging $7 to $18.

### What twigs are best for wall art?

Willow and hazel are best: they bend without snapping. Birch suits straight pieces; oak and maple suit rigid ones. Avoid pine: its resin stains walls and stops glue setting.

### How do you hang twig wall art?

Under 500 g takes one picture nail or adhesive hook; 500 g to 1.5 kg takes two picture hooks with a wire back; over 1.5 kg takes two screws into wall plugs or a stud.

### Do twigs need to be treated before going on a wall?

Yes: scrub them, wash them in warm soapy water, check for bugs, and dry them flat for one to two weeks. Strip loose bark or seal it with varnish.

### Are fairy lights safe on twig wall art?

Battery LED fairy lights are safe because LEDs stay cool, but use a timer and check the wire monthly. Never use mains lights or candles near dry twigs.

### Can I make twig wall art without any tools?

Yes, for the easy projects: a star, heart or snowflake needs only twigs and twine, and you can snap twigs to length by hand.
