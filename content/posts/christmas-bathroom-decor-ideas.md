Christmas bathroom decor is the easiest festive win in the house: one small room, one mirror and one counter can make every December morning feel like a holiday. These 15 ideas each carry a real cost and time, plus three DIY builds.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every idea costs £3 to £42; most take under an hour.
> - The mirror, counter and shower curtain change the room most.
> - Choose plastic, ceramic and sealed wood — paper goes limp in steam.
> - Use flameless candles, and keep electrics on an RCD-protected socket.
> - A powder room needs one hero moment.

## Mirror and Walls

### 1. Pine garland across the mirror

Drape a 1.8 metre faux pine garland across the top of the mirror, ends falling 20 cm down each side. Cost: £8 to £15. Time: 20 minutes. The mirror doubles the greenery.

### 2. Mini wreath on the mirror

Hang a 25 to 30 cm mini wreath from a suction hook on the mirror, or tie it to the frame with ribbon. Cost: £5 to £12, or £4 to £8 from DIY Build 1. Time: 10 minutes.

![Hands tying a deep red velvet bow onto a small fresh pine wreath on a white bathroom counter, with a potted mini Christmas tree, gold baubles and a spool of red velvet ribbon beside it](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/christmas-bathroom-decor-ideas/in-article-1)

### 3. Festive window clings

Stick reusable static-cling snowflakes, stars or a Christmas tree scene onto the mirror glass, then peel them off in January. Cost: £3 to £6. Time: 15 minutes. Clings carry no adhesive, so steam never loosens them.

### 4. Framed Christmas prints

Hang two or three small framed prints — holly, a snowy village, a simple "Merry Christmas" — beside the mirror, sealed behind glass. Cost: £6 to £14. Time: 20 minutes. Without glass, steam curls paper within days. The [bathroom wall decor ideas](/blog/bathroom-wall-decor-ideas) guide covers frame heights and spacing.

## Sink and Counter

### 5. Mini tree on the counter

Stand a 30 to 45 cm potted mini tree on the counter in the corner farthest from the tap, in a ceramic pot on a cork mat. Cost: £8 to £18. Time: 10 minutes. A living spruce needs water every few days; a faux one needs nothing.

### 6. Festive hand towels

Fold two or three festive hand towels in red, forest green or a snowflake print and stack them beside the sink, with one plain towel on top for use. Cost: £6 to £12 for two. Time: 5 minutes. Folded towels look deliberate where a draped towel looks like laundry.

### 7. Pinecone and eucalyptus display

Fill a small ceramic bowl with pinecones, dried orange slices and eucalyptus sprigs, and set it between the tap and the soap dispenser. Cost: £4 to £9. Time: 15 minutes. Pinecones and dried citrus outlast fresh flowers in humidity.

### 8. Soap dispenser and tray

Swap the plain soap dispenser for a ceramic one in red, green or cream, and set it with a matching soap dish on a small tray. Cost: £7 to £16, or £5 to £10 from DIY Build 2. Time: 10 minutes. A tray groups the dispenser, toothbrush holder and candle, and catches every drip.

## Shower and Tub

### 9. Festive shower curtain swap

Swap the plain shower curtain for a red-and-cream snowflake, tartan or winter-forest print, and add a matching bath mat. Cost: £10 to £22 together. Time: 15 minutes. The curtain is the largest surface in the room.

### 10. Garland on the curtain rod

Wind a slim 1.5 metre pine garland with battery fairy lights along the shower curtain rod, fixing it with cable ties at each end. Cost: £9 to £16. Time: 20 minutes. Battery lights keep mains power out of the wet zone.

![A small Christmas bathroom with deep navy blue walls, a red and cream snowflake shower curtain, a matching red bath mat and a pine garland with warm fairy lights along the curtain rod](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/christmas-bathroom-decor-ideas/in-article-2)

### 11. Bath tray

Lay a wooden bath tray across the tub with a folded towel, a pine sprig in a bud vase and a flameless candle. Cost: £12 to £25. Time: 10 minutes. Never put a real candle on a bath tray — one knock and the wax is in the water.

### 12. Eucalyptus on the shower head

Tie a small bundle of eucalyptus and pine sprigs to the shower head with twine, hanging just clear of the water stream. Cost: £3 to £6. Time: 10 minutes. The steam releases the scent every morning. Replace it every two weeks.

## Floor and Door

### 13. Festive bath mat and rug

Lay a thick red, green or snowflake-print bath mat in front of the bath, and a smaller matching rug by the door. Cost: £8 to £18 for the pair. Time: 5 minutes. Choose mats with a rubber backing so they never slide.

### 14. Wreath on the door

Hang a 30 to 40 cm wreath on the outside of the bathroom door with an over-door hook, keeping the inside clear. Cost: £6 to £14. Time: 10 minutes. An over-door hook leaves no holes and no adhesive on paint.

### 15. Cistern dressing

Dress the cistern with a folded festive towel, a pine sprig in a jar and a tiny tree — the full version is DIY Build 3 below — and stack spare rolls in a basket wrapped with ribbon. Cost: £4 to £10. Time: 15 minutes.

## DIY Build 1: Mini Pine Wreath for the Mirror

You need a 25 cm wire ring (£2 to £3), two bunches of faux pine picks (£3 to £4), 25 mm red velvet ribbon (£2) and florist wire. Total: £7 to £9. Time: 45 minutes.

Cut the pine picks into 15 cm lengths and lay them in overlapping rows around the ring, all pointing the same way. Bind each pick to the ring with florist wire, pulling tight so it cannot spin — loose picks make a wreath look thin. Add a second layer to fill gaps, and tuck in three pinecones. Fold 60 cm of velvet ribbon into a bow, wire it to the top of the wreath with a 20 cm hanging loop, and hang it from a suction hook on the mirror.

## DIY Build 2: Festive Soap Dispenser and Tray Set

You need a ceramic soap dispenser (£4 to £6), a wooden tray about 25 × 15 cm (£4 to £6), red and gold acrylic paint, a brush, clear matt varnish and faux pine. Total: £10 to £14. Time: 1 hour plus overnight drying.

Wash and dry both pieces, then paint a 3 cm red band around the dispenser base and a matching stripe along the tray edge. Add a thin gold line above the red band and let both dry for two hours. Seal the tray with two thin coats of clear matt varnish, drying an hour between coats, so splashes bead off. Set the dispenser on the tray with a pine sprig beside it. Varnish matters most: an unsealed tray warps within a fortnight.

## DIY Build 3: Mini Tree on the Cistern

You need a 30 cm potted mini spruce (£5 to £8), a ceramic pot 2 cm wider than the nursery pot (£3 to £5), 20 battery fairy lights (£3 to £4), six mini baubles (£2 to £3) and a cork mat. Total: £13 to £20. Time: 30 minutes.

Set the cork mat on the cistern lid so the pot never scratches the ceramic. Stand the tree in its pot on the mat, at the back corner, at least 15 cm from the flush button. Wind the fairy lights upward in a loose spiral, tucking the battery pack behind the pot where it stays dry. Hang the six mini baubles evenly and switch the lights on — warm white suits a bathroom best. Water with two tablespoons every three days.

## Humidity-Proofing Materials Table

A bathroom runs at 70 to 90 percent humidity during a hot shower. Paper curls, card warps, untreated wood swells, and cheap metal rusts.

| Material | Bathroom-safe? | Best use | Avoid for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Faux pine and greenery | Yes | Garlands, wreaths, trees | — |
| Ceramic and glass | Yes | Dispensers, vases, candle holders | — |
| Sealed wood and trays | Yes, if sealed | Bath trays, cistern displays | Unsealed wood, which swells |
| Plastic and resin ornaments | Yes | Tree baubles, window clings | — |
| Paper and card | No | Nothing in the bathroom | Prints, unless behind glass |
| Fabric and towels | Yes, if washable | Towels, mats, curtains | Anything that cannot be washed |

Ventilate after every shower: run the extractor fan for 20 minutes or open the window for 10.

## Electrical and Candle Safety

Bathrooms and electricity need respect: no mains-powered item within reach of the bath or shower, and no mains cable crossing a wet surface. Use battery fairy lights — 20 warm-white LEDs cost £3 to £5 and run all season. Mains lights need an RCD-protected socket at least 3 metres from the bath. The US Consumer Product Safety Commission warns against mixing water and mains electricity.

Candles start thousands of home fires each year, according to the National Fire Protection Association, and a small bathroom is a bad place for a naked flame: towels, curtains and toilet rolls are all within arm's reach. Use flameless LED candles instead — a set of three costs £6 to £10. If you do burn a real candle, keep it at least 30 cm (12 inches) from anything flammable and never leave the room while it burns. ## Zone Plans for Every Bathroom

A powder room, a family bathroom and a guest suite need different plans — pick yours and stop there.

A powder room — the smallest room, used by guests — needs one hero moment: the mirror garland (idea 1), folded towels (idea 6) and cistern dressing (idea 15). Cost: about £18 to £37. Time: under an hour.

A family bathroom — used daily, often by children — needs wipe-clean, kid-proof choices: the shower curtain swap (idea 9), eucalyptus bundle (idea 12), festive bath mat (idea 13) and soap dispenser set (idea 8). Cost: about £28 to £62. Time: about an hour. Skip real candles.

A guest suite can take the full treatment. Add the mini tree (idea 5), bath tray (idea 11), door wreath (idea 14) and framed prints (idea 4) to the powder-room plan. Cost: about £48 to £95. Time: about two hours. Keep the palette to two colours — red and green, or cream and gold.

## Renter Friendly Methods

Almost everything here is renter-friendly. Suction hooks hold up to 1 kg on clean glass and glazed tile — wipe the surface dry first, press flat, and test with a gentle pull. Clear adhesive hooks hold garlands on mirror frames, and peel off cleanly. Over-door hooks carry the door wreath with no fixings at all. Never stick adhesive to freshly painted walls — paint needs four weeks to cure — and never use nails in bathroom tiles: a cracked tile costs far more than any decoration. The [renter friendly wall decor](/blog/renter-friendly-wall-decor) guide covers the same no-damage methods for the rest of the house.

## Takedown and Storage

Take the decor down in the first week of January, before the pine sheds and the eucalyptus turns brown. Work in reverse: lights and batteries out first, then garlands and wreaths, then textiles into the wash. Remove the batteries from every fairy-light string before storage — a leaking battery ruins it over the summer. Wash and dry the towels, mats and shower curtain before packing — damp fabric grows mould by March. Pack the wreath and garland flat in a labelled box, baubles in an egg box, and fairy lights coiled around card. Label the box "Bathroom Christmas" and store it somewhere dry — never a bathroom cupboard. The [Christmas tree alternatives](/blog/christmas-tree-alternatives) guide covers storing small trees for next December.

## FAQ

### How much does Christmas bathroom decor cost?

Most bathrooms cost £15 to £60: powder room £18 to £37, family bathroom £28 to £62, guest suite £48 to £95.

### Can I put a real Christmas tree in a bathroom?

A small potted tree of 30 to 45 cm works on a counter or cistern lid, clear of the tap splash zone and the flush button. Water it lightly every three days, and use battery fairy lights.

### Are fairy lights safe in a bathroom?

Battery fairy lights are safe anywhere in a bathroom. Mains-powered lights should stay at least 3 metres from the bath or shower, on an RCD-protected socket, with no cable crossing a wet surface.

### Can I burn candles in the bathroom?

It is safer not to: towels, curtains and toilet rolls sit within arm's reach, so use flameless LED candles. If you do burn one, keep it at least 30 cm from anything flammable and never leave the room.

### What is the fastest way to decorate a bathroom?

Swap the shower curtain and bath mat, drape a garland across the mirror, and fold festive towels beside the sink. Those three moves take about 40 minutes and cost £24 to £49.
