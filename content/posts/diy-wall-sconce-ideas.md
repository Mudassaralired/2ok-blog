A wall sconce is the fastest way to finish a room: warm light at eye level, no floor space, no table. Most people never fit one because of the wiring — yet most DIY sconces never touch a wire. Battery pucks, rechargeable bulbs and plug-in cords cover nearly every look below.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - No-wiring sconces use a battery puck or rechargeable bulb inside an ordinary sconce — £10 to £20 each.
> - Hang sconces with the shade centre 150 to 165 cm from the floor, pairs 150 to 180 cm apart. 
> - Choose 2700K for bedrooms and living rooms, 3000K for bathrooms, and at least 400 lumens for reading.
> - Plug-in sconces need a cord cover (£5) and a nearby socket.
> - Only hardwired sconces need an electrician.

## The Three Sconce Types: Battery, Plug-In and Hardwired

Every sconce is one of three types, and the type decides the cost, the light and whether you need an electrician. 

**Battery sconces** run on a puck light or a rechargeable bulb. A puck is a flat LED disc, 8 to 10 cm across, that sticks inside a shade; three cost about £8. A rechargeable bulb fits a normal E27 socket, charges by USB and costs about £10. They are the cheapest and only type a renter can fit anywhere — but most pucks give only 100 to 200 lumens, ambience, not reading light. 

**Plug-in sconces** have a cord, plug and switch. A 40 to 60 watt equivalent bulb gives 400 to 800 lumens, and they never need charging. Their weakness is the cord: it must reach a socket and be hidden with a £5 paintable cord cover.

**Hardwired sconces** connect to the mains through a junction box. They are the brightest and neatest, with no cord and no batteries, but the only type here that needs a qualified electrician — and the only type a renter cannot fit. Expect £60 to £120 per sconce; without a junction box in place, the electrician must chase a cable in.

## Light Quality: Lumens, Colour Temperature and Batteries

Brightness is measured in lumens. Under 200 is a glow — enough for a hallway at night, not for reading. Bedside reading needs at least 400 lumens on the page, so a rechargeable bulb or plug-in, not a puck. At a bathroom mirror, two 400-lumen sconces give even light.

Colour temperature, in Kelvin, decides the mood. **2700K warm white** is candle-like and relaxing — right for bedrooms and living rooms. **3000K** shows skin tones truly — right for bathrooms. **4000K cool white** looks clinical and belongs in a workshop. Check the packet: the same fitting is sold in all three.

A puck on three AAAs lasts 30 to 50 hours on its brightest setting — about a month of evenings — and a remote timer stretches that. A rechargeable bulb lasts 4 to 8 hours per charge and recharges by USB in about three hours; keep a spare charged for bedside use. Rechargeable pucks, about £12 each, are the better buy over a year: AAAs cost about £3 a month, a rechargeable puck nothing after that.

## No-Wiring Sconces: The Magic Light Trick

The "magic light trick" is simple: take any ordinary hardwired sconce and put a battery light inside it. The sconce is only a shade and a fitting.

### 1. Thrifted brass sconce with a puck light

A charity-shop brass sconce (£3 to £5) with a puck light stuck inside the shade gives a warm glow for under £10. Stick the puck inside the shade, not on the wall, so the light washes down the wall. Cost: £8 to £12. Time: 15 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 2. Rechargeable bulb in a glass-globe sconce

A black metal sconce with a glass globe (£15 to £20) and a rechargeable E27 bulb (£10) looks exactly like a wired fitting and gives 400 lumens — bright enough to read by. Its remote switches it on and dims it. Cost: £25 to £30. Time: 20 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 3. Wooden sconce with a shelf and USB port

A small wooden sconce with a built-in shelf (£20 to £25) holds a phone and a book, with a puck light underneath lighting both. Thread a USB cable behind the shelf to a socket and it becomes a charging station. Cost: £25 to £35. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. 

### 4. Swing-arm sconce, battery version

A swing-arm sconce (£20) pulls towards you for reading and pushes back when done. Fit a rechargeable bulb and it works exactly as the wired version, with no cable to fight. Cost: £30 to £35. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. It pairs well with the [bedroom wall decor ideas](/blog/bedroom-wall-decor-ideas/) guide for the bed wall.

### 5. Fabric-shade sconce for soft light

A drum shade (£8) on a simple wall bracket (£10), with a warm 2700K rechargeable bulb inside, gives the softest light in this guide — no glare, no hard shadows. Linen diffuses best; dark shades swallow half the light. Cost: £18 to £25. Time: 20 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 6. Candle-style sconce with an LED taper

A wall-mounted candle holder (£5 to £10) with a battery LED taper candle (£3) gives flickering candlelight with no flame and no wax. Use a timer candle so it switches itself off. Cost: £8 to £15. Time: 10 minutes. Difficulty: easy — the safest sconce here for a child's room.

## Plug-In Sconces

Plug-in sconces trade a visible cord for real brightness and no battery chores. The cord is a solved problem: a paintable cover (£5) sticks to the wall, hides the cable and paints over to match.

### 7. Plug-in sconce with a cord cover

Any hardwired sconce becomes plug-in with a £6 conversion kit — a cord with a plug, a switch and a fitting. Route the cord straight down to the socket inside a cord cover and it looks wired in. Cost: £20 to £40 depending on the sconce. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 8. Plug-in sconce with a long cord and a dimmer

A plug-in dimmer (£8) on the cord turns a bright 800-lumen sconce down to a night-light glow — the best living-room sconce in this guide: bright for reading, dim for films. Cost: £30 to £45. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 9. Industrial pipe sconce

Black iron pipe fittings (£12) and a cage shade (£8) make an industrial sconce for a home office or loft-style living room, cord hidden inside the pipe. Cost: £25 to £35. Time: one hour. Difficulty: intermediate — tighten the threads firmly so it never sags. The [home office wall decor ideas](/blog/home-office-wall-decor-ideas/) guide shows the same look.

### 10. Rattan and cane sconce

A rattan shade (£12) over a plug-in fitting gives patterned, dappled light that suits boho rooms. Rattan diffuses gently, so use a 400-lumen bulb — a puck is too dim behind cane. Cost: £25 to £35. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Pair it with a [basket wall decor](/blog/basket-wall-decor/) arrangement for a matching texture story.

## Candle and Rustic Sconces

Candle sconces give the oldest kind of wall light: a flame, real or LED, in a holder on the wall. They are cheap, need no electricity, and suit rooms where a wired light would look wrong.

### 11. Punched-tin sconce

A tin can, punched with a nail in a pattern and sprayed matte black, throws patterned light across the wall. Hang it with a small nail and a tea light or LED candle inside. Cost: £3 to £5. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Never use a real flame above soft furnishings.

### 12. Dollar Tree candle sconce

Two Dollar Tree candle holders, a small mirror tile and a length of jute make a wall sconce for under £5. Glue the holders back to back, hang the mirror behind them, and it doubles the candlelight. Cost: £4 to £6. Time: 20 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 13. Whiskey-barrel stave sconce

A barrel stave (£5 from a reclamation yard), cut to 40 cm and fitted with two candle cups, makes a rustic sconce for a hallway or dining room. Cost: £10 to £15. Time: one hour. Difficulty: intermediate — sand it smooth, seal it, and mount the cups level.

### 14. Mason-jar sconce

A mason jar (£2) in a wire or leather holder (£5) on the wall, with a tea light or fairy-light bundle inside, gives soft light for a bathroom or porch. Cost: £7 to £10. Time: 15 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Use LED tea lights in a bathroom, where steam and flame do not mix.

### 15. Arched mirror sconce pair

Two small arched mirrors (£10 each) with a slim sconce mounted on each frame turn a mirror into a light. The mirror bounces the light back, so one 300-lumen bulb reads like two. Cost: £30 to £40 for the pair. Time: 40 minutes. Difficulty: easy. The [bathroom wall decor ideas](/blog/bathroom-wall-decor-ideas/) guide covers mirror sizing for the same wall.

## Room-by-Room Placement Guide

**Bedroom.** Pairs flanking the headboard, shade centre 150 to 165 cm from the floor, 150 to 180 cm apart. Choose 2700K, 400 lumens or more, and a dimmer or remote.

**Bathroom.** Either side of the mirror, not above it — side light removes the shadows an overhead bar casts under your chin. Centre at eye level, about 160 cm, 15 to 20 cm from the mirror's edge. Choose 3000K, and keep fittings 60 cm from a bath or shower unless rated for wet zones.

**Living room.** Sconces 180 to 240 cm apart along a long wall, or one either side of a fireplace or large art, centre 150 to 165 cm. Choose 2700K and a dimmer.

**Hallway.** One sconce every 240 to 300 cm, at 165 cm high. A puck-light sconce is enough — hallways need wayfinding light, not reading light — and a timer saves batteries. 

**Home office.** One sconce beside the desk, aimed at the work surface, cuts screen glare better than a ceiling light. A swing-arm is best: pull it in for paperwork, push it back for calls. Choose 3000K, and see the [home office wall decor ideas](/blog/home-office-wall-decor-ideas/) guide for the desk wall.

**Dining room.** Sconces either side of a sideboard or large mirror, at 150 to 165 cm, give dinner light without a pendant. Candle sconces suit this room best.

## Hiding Cords and Mounting Without Damage

Plan the cord route before you buy: the socket must be within the cord's length, and the cord must run straight down, never diagonally. A paintable cord cover (£5, ten minutes) sticks to the wall, hides the cable and paints over to match. Never run one under a rug.

Mounting depends on weight. Under 1 kg, a battery sconce hangs on one adhesive hook or a single nail — no drilling, and renters can use Command strips rated for the weight. At 1 to 2 kg, a plug-in sconce needs one screw into a wall plug or two adhesive strips. Over 2 kg, such as a pipe or heavy mirror sconce, needs a screw into a stud or a plasterboard anchor. Weigh the sconce first, and test an adhesive hook for an hour.

For hardwired sconces: if the wall already has a junction box, an electrician can swap the fitting in under an hour. If not, the job means chasing a cable, making good and repainting — budget £60 to £120 per sconce and a half-day of dust.  Never open a junction box yourself unless qualified: switch the breaker off, test it, and call a professional. ## FAQ

### Do I need an electrician to install a wall sconce?

No, for battery and plug-in sconces — both are DIY jobs with no wiring. Only hardwired sconces need a qualified electrician.

### How high should a wall sconce be mounted?

Centre the shade 150 to 165 cm from the floor, about 160 cm beside a bathroom mirror. Space pairs 150 to 180 cm apart.

### What is the best light for a bedside wall sconce?

A 2700K warm white bulb at 400 lumens or more, with a dimmer or remote. A puck at 100 to 200 lumens is too dim for reading — use a rechargeable bulb or plug-in.

### How long do battery wall sconces last?

A puck on AAA batteries lasts 30 to 50 hours, about a month of evenings. A rechargeable bulb lasts 4 to 8 hours per charge, and a remote timer stretches both.

### How do I hide the cord on a plug-in wall sconce?

Run it straight down to the socket inside a paintable cord cover (£5). Never run a cord diagonally or under a rug.
