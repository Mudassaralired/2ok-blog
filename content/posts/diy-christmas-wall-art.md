Christmas wall art is the fastest way to make a room festive. A few framed prints, one painted canvas or a glowing sign can carry a wall.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every project here costs $5 to $48, most under three hours.
> - Framed print sets are the cheapest start: about $12 for four prints, thrifted frames.
> - Paint on canvas board, not stretched canvas, for your first piece.
> - Light-up art needs battery fairy lights only; never mains lights inside a frame.
> - Renters can hang every piece here with adhesive strips — check the hanging table.
> - Store prints flat between tissue paper for next December.

## Framed Print Projects

### 1. Four-piece snowy forest print set

Print four snowy pine forest images in matching sizes and frame them in identical thin frames for a calm row above a sofa. Cost: $8 to $14. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Use free public-domain winter photos on matte paper — it suits the [minimalist Christmas decor](/blog/minimalist-christmas-decor) look.

### 2. Red reindeer silhouette triptych

Print one large reindeer silhouette split across three vertical frames, so the animal reads as a single image broken into panels. Cost: $10 to $18. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Cut the image into three equal strips and hang them 3 cm apart.

### 3. Vintage-style Christmas postcard gallery

Frame six to eight vintage-style Christmas postcards in a loose grid for a nostalgic wall that looks collected over years. Cost: $9 to $16. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Mix portrait and landscape cards; it works in a [small space](/blog/christmas-decor-ideas-for-small-spaces) where one large piece would overwhelm the room.

### 4. Gold star minimalist print set

Print three gold stars — large, medium, small — on warm white paper and frame them in pale wood frames for a quiet, Scandinavian-style set. Cost: $6 to $12. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Draw the stars in a free design tool, filled with soft metallic gold.

## Painted Canvas Projects

### 5. Beginner snowy pine canvas

Paint one snowy pine tree on a 40 by 50 cm canvas board: a dark blue-grey sky, one green pine in the centre, and white dots flicked on for falling snow. Cost: $7 to $12. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Sketch the tree in pencil first, paint the sky before the tree, and add snow last.

![Hands painting a snowy pine tree on a canvas with white, green, red and gold paints on a wooden table](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-christmas-wall-art/in-article-1)

### 6. Watercolour village scene on canvas

Paint a small snowy village — cottages with glowing yellow windows, pine trees, and a pale sky — on a 50 by 60 cm canvas board in watercolour or thinned acrylic. Cost: $9 to $15. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Keep the houses simple rectangles and hang it by the [Christmas mantel decor](/blog/christmas-mantel-decor).

### 7. Abstract gold-and-green canvas

Paint bold abstract shapes — green arcs, gold leaf rectangles, and a wash of cream — on a 60 by 80 cm canvas for a modern piece that reads as Christmas without a tree or star. Cost: $12 to $22. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Use a large flat brush and only three colours: deep green, metallic gold and warm cream.

### 8. Textured plaster snow canvas

Spread textured plaster across a 50 by 70 cm canvas board, comb it into snowdrift ridges with a fork, let it dry, then paint it white with pale blue in the hollows. Cost: $11 to $18. Time: 3 hours plus drying overnight. Difficulty: moderate. Add three tiny pine silhouettes in the distance and leave the rest bare.

## Paper-Cut and Wood-Sign Art

### 9. Paper-cut nativity silhouette in a frame

Cut a nativity silhouette — stable, star and three figures — from black card with a craft knife, mount it on cream paper, and frame it behind glass. Cost: $5 to $9. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Print the outline, tape it over the black card, and cut through both layers on a cutting mat.

### 10. Layered paper snowflake shadow box

Layer five or six paper snowflakes of decreasing size inside a deep box frame, each spaced 1 cm apart on foam pads, so the flakes float in layers behind the glass. Cost: $8 to $14. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Cut the snowflakes from white and pale blue card, folding each one in quarters first.

### 11. Rustic wooden plank tree sign

Paint a simple pine tree and star on three horizontal reclaimed wood planks joined at the back, then drape a battery fairy-light string around the edges. Cost: $10 to $18. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. Sand the planks lightly, paint the tree in one flat green, and leave the wood grain showing.

![A rustic wooden plank sign painted with a pine tree and star, draped with warm fairy lights, hanging on a sage-green hallway wall above a wooden bench](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-christmas-wall-art/in-article-2)

### 12. Painted wood-slice ornament art panel

Glue painted wood slices — each a tiny ornament with a painted star, stripe or snowflake — in a tight grid on a 40 by 50 cm painted board, framed in a deep shadow-box frame. Cost: $12 to $20. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Paint the slices first and let them dry fully before gluing, or the paint smudges.

## Light-Up and Gallery Wall Art

### 13. Backlit canvas with fairy lights

Paint a simple night scene on a stretched canvas, poke tiny holes where the stars and windows should glow, and push battery fairy lights through from behind. Cost: $18 to $30. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: hard. Paint first, let the canvas dry, then pierce each hole with a needle from the front.

### 14. Light-up starburst print panel

Mount a large printed starburst on a foam board, punch small holes at the ends of the rays, and feed battery fairy lights through so the starburst glows from behind the print. Cost: $15 to $25. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Print the starburst on matte paper and hang it above a [Christmas centerpiece](/blog/diy-christmas-centerpiece-ideas) table.

### 15. Full Christmas print gallery wall

Combine six to nine framed prints — snowy forests, reindeer, stars and village scenes — into a full gallery wall above the sofa, keeping the same warm paper tone throughout. Cost: $25 to $48. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Lay the arrangement on the floor first, photograph it, and only then hang the pieces.

## Framing and Print Sizes

The frame makes a cheap print look expensive; the wrong size frame ruins a good one. Match the frame to the print with a small mount gap: a 30 by 40 cm print in a 40 by 50 cm frame with a mount looks gallery-ready.

| Print size | Frame size | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 by 25 cm | 30 by 40 cm with mount | Small prints, bedrooms |
| 30 by 40 cm | 40 by 50 cm with mount | Gallery wall pieces |
| 40 by 50 cm | 50 by 60 cm with mount | Feature prints above a sofa |
| 50 by 70 cm | 60 by 80 cm with mount | Large statement prints |
| A4 (21 by 30 cm) | 30 by 40 cm with mount | Postcards, small sets |

Thrifted frames are the cheapest route: charity shops sell solid wood frames for $1 to $3, and one coat of spray paint makes mismatched frames match. Print on matte paper, not gloss: gloss reflects lamplight and hides the image at night.

## Hanging Without Damage

Most pieces here are light enough for damage-free hanging. Framed prints under 1 kg hold on a pair of medium adhesive strips; canvas boards up to 2 kg need large strips; the wood-slice panel and the full gallery wall need proper hooks. Weigh each piece first — guessing is how frames fall at 2 a.m.

| Piece weight | Hardware | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 1 kg | Medium adhesive strips, 2 per frame | Small framed prints |
| 1 to 2 kg | Large adhesive strips, 4 per frame | Canvas boards, shadow boxes |
| 2 to 3 kg | Picture hook in a stud or wall plug | Wood signs, large canvases |
| Over 3 kg | Two hooks with a wire back | Gallery walls, heavy panels |

Adhesive strips work on painted walls, tiles and glass, but not on textured wallpaper or fresh paint — wait four weeks after painting. Press each strip firmly for 30 seconds and wait an hour before hanging the frame. Renters can use the [renter-friendly Christmas wall decor](/blog/christmas-wall-decor-ideas) guide as a companion.

## Christmas Colour Palettes

A palette is what makes separate pieces look like one collection. Pick one before you start and use it across every project this year. Classic red and green suits traditional rooms; Nordic white and wood suits pale rooms; deep green and gold suits moody ones.

| Palette | Colours | Best room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classic Christmas | Red, deep green, cream, gold | Traditional living room |
| Nordic white | White, pale wood, soft grey, silver | Small or pale rooms |
| Deep green and gold | Forest green, gold, charcoal, cream | Moody living room |
| Frosted blue | Ice blue, white, silver, pale pine | Bedroom, hallway |
| Rustic farmhouse | Barn red, cream, natural wood, sage | Kitchen, entryway |

Keep one colour dominant and the rest as accents: a gallery wall that is 70 percent cream and white with red and gold accents feels calm, while equal amounts of red, green and gold feel loud. Choose one metal — gold reads warm, silver reads cold — and stick to it.

## January Storage and Takedown

Take Christmas art down in the first week of January, before the dust settles and the tape hardens. Remove adhesive strips by pulling straight down along the wall, never outward — pulling outward takes paint with it. Wipe glass with a dry cloth, and brush canvases with a soft dry brush; never wipe a painting with a damp cloth.

Store framed prints flat, face up, with tissue paper between each frame so the glass does not scratch the print below. Stand canvases upright, face to face in pairs with tissue between them, in a dry cupboard — never in a damp garage, where the canvas sags and the paint cracks. Coil fairy lights around a piece of card, tape the battery pack to its own piece, and label each piece so next year the wall goes up in an afternoon.

## What Christmas Wall Art Costs

Wall art is one of the cheapest Christmas crafts: the paper, paint and frames cost little and the work is yours. The four-piece print set is the best value here: about $12 for a full sofa-wall display. Light-up pieces cost the most, because fairy lights and batteries are the priciest single item.

If you only make one piece this year, make the four-piece print set: it costs the least and takes an hour.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Printing on glossy paper is the most common mistake: gloss reflects lamplight and hides the image at night, so always choose matte. Hanging a gallery wall without laying it on the floor first wastes holes and leaves a crooked arrangement — photograph the floor layout first. Using mains-powered lights inside a canvas or frame is a fire risk; battery lights only. Start on canvas board, not stretched canvas, for a first project.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY Christmas wall art cost?

Most projects here cost $5 to $20, and the four-piece snowy forest print set costs $8 to $14. The most expensive is the full gallery wall at $25 to $48, because it uses six to nine frames.

### Can I make Christmas wall art if I cannot paint?

Yes — start with the framed print projects, which need no painting at all: print the images, frame them, and hang them. The paper snowflake shadow box and the gold star print set also need no painting skill.

### What size should Christmas wall art be above a sofa?

Aim for art that spans two-thirds of the sofa's width. For a 180 cm sofa, that means about 120 cm of art — one large 50 by 70 cm print or a gallery wall of four to six smaller frames. Hang the centre at eye level, about 145 to 155 cm from the floor.

### How do I hang Christmas wall art without damaging the walls?

Use adhesive picture strips rated for the weight: medium strips under 1 kg, large strips up to 2 kg. Press each strip for 30 seconds and wait an hour before hanging. Heavier pieces need a proper picture hook in a stud or wall plug.

### How do I store DIY Christmas wall art after the holidays?

Store framed prints flat with tissue paper between them, and stand canvases upright in pairs in a dry cupboard. Coil fairy lights around a piece of card, tape the battery pack to its own piece, and label everything.
