## Classic Paper Mache Wall Art

### 1. Paper mache sunburst wall art

Fan 20 to 30 tapered rays from a central disc, about 90 cm across, painted terracotta and cream. Cost: $5 to $10. Time: 4 to 5 hours over two days. Difficulty: moderate.

### 2. Paper mache botanical vine wall panel

Press leaves, stems and small flowers onto a board about 60 by 80 cm, then paint the vines sage green on a cream background. Cost: $6 to $12. Time: 5 to 6 hours over two days. Difficulty: moderate.

### 3. Paper mache moon phases wall art

Make five crescent and full-moon discs, 15 to 20 cm across, from strips over balloon moulds, hung in a row. Cost: $4 to $8. Time: 3 to 4 hours over two days. Difficulty: easy.

### 4. Paper mache geometric relief tiles

Press pulp into square moulds, 20 cm across, to make raised triangles and arcs, then mount nine tiles in a grid. Cost: $6 to $11. Time: 4 hours over two days. Difficulty: moderate.

## Flour Paste vs Glue Paste vs Paper Pulp

Flour paste is the classic recipe: one part plain flour to two parts water, whisked smooth and simmered for two to three minutes until it thickens like cream. It costs almost nothing and is safe for children, but it feeds mould if sealed while damp. Glue paste is one part white PVA glue to one part water. It costs about $5 a bottle and dries harder, clearer and more water-resistant, so it suits damp rooms. Paper pulp is different again: soak torn newspaper in hot water overnight, blend it into a mush, squeeze out the water, and mix in glue until it feels like clay. Pulp sculpts freely without a mould, but it dries slowest and shrinks.

| Method | Cost | Best for | Drying | Mould risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flour paste | Under $1 | Strips, kids | Slow | High if damp |
| Glue paste | $2 to $4 | Strips, damp rooms | Medium | Low |
| Paper pulp | $2 to $4 | Sculpting, relief | Slowest | Medium |

Use newspaper inside and plain white paper for the final layer, since print shows through thin paint. Tear the strips, never cut them: torn edges feather out, while cut edges leave a visible step. Keep strips 2 to 3 cm wide, dip each one, pull off the extra paste, and smooth it flat. Four layers is the minimum; six layers makes it sturdy enough to sand.

## Drying Times by Thickness

Drying is the slowest part of paper mache. A piece is dry only when it feels light, sounds hollow when tapped, and shows no cool or damp patch inside. In a warm room, thin strip work dries in about 24 hours, while thicker pulp needs far longer, and a cold or humid room can double every time below. Never use an oven or a hairdryer on high heat: the outside hardens while the inside stays wet, and the piece cracks later. A fan on low is the fastest safe method; turn the piece over halfway through.

| Thickness | Layers | Drying time | Dry when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thin, 2 to 3 mm | 3 to 4 layers | 24 hours | Hollow sound |
| Medium, 4 to 6 mm | 5 to 6 layers | 48 hours | No cool spots |
| Thick pulp, 8 to 12 mm | Pulp, 1 to 2 cm | 3 to 5 days | Hard throughout |
| Large sculpture | Armature, 6 layers | 4 to 7 days | No flex |

In winter, add a day to every time above. If the piece still feels heavy, give it another 24 hours.

## Sealing and Painting Paper Mache

Sealing turns a fragile paper shell into a lasting piece. Once dry, brush on two coats of diluted white PVA glue, one part glue to one part water, letting the first coat dry before the second. This seals the fibres and stops uneven paint soaking. For bathroom or kitchen pieces, use a clear matt varnish instead: two coats, about $8 a can, gives a water-resistant skin.

Paint with acrylic paint: it sticks well, dries fast, and comes in every colour. Prime first with white acrylic for light colours over newspaper, or the print will show through. Two thin coats beat one thick coat, which can crack as it dries. Finish with a clear matt or satin varnish. Avoid watercolour and poster paint: they stay soft, smudge, and fade fast.

## Sizing Your Paper Mache Wall Art

Size the piece to the wall, not to the mould you happen to have. Above a sofa, the piece should span about two thirds of the sofa width: a 180 cm sofa takes a piece 110 to 120 cm across, as our [above sofa wall decor](/blog/above-sofa-wall-decor) guide shows. Hang the piece so its centre sits at eye level, about 145 cm from the floor, or 15 to 20 cm above furniture.

## Hanging Paper Mache by Weight

Paper mache is light, but a large pulp sculpture can still weigh 2 to 3 kg. Weigh the finished piece first, and hang from two points on pieces over 1 kg. Fix a picture wire across the back, not a single loop of string, which stretches and lets the piece tilt. Under 500 g, a single adhesive hook is enough.

| Weight | Hardware | Fixings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 500 g | Adhesive hook or nail | 1 |
| 500 g to 1.5 kg | Two picture hooks, wire | 2 |
| 1.5 kg to 3 kg | Two 3 kg hooks, wire | 2 |
| Over 3 kg | Two screws into wall plugs | 2 |

Renters should favour picture hooks over nails. Never hang a piece over 1 kg from a single adhesive hook, or any piece from a bare nail in drywall.

## Mould Prevention and Kid Safety

Mould is the one real risk with paper mache. It grows when a piece is sealed or painted while still damp inside, trapping moisture that feeds on the flour paste. The fix is simple: dry the piece fully, using the times in the table above, then seal it with diluted PVA or varnish. For bathroom pieces, use glue paste instead of flour paste and finish with two coats of clear varnish. If you spot dark spots or a musty smell, take the piece down, dry it in the sun, and reseal it; if the mould is deep inside, throw the piece away.

Paper mache is one of the safest crafts for children, with a few rules. Use flour paste only for kids under eight: it is non-toxic and washes off skin and clothes. Never let children use craft knives, hot glue guns or spray varnish: cut the strips and armatures for them, and do the sealing and varnishing yourself outdoors or by an open window. A child-made piece should still be sealed before it goes on the wall, or it will not survive a damp season.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Sealing a damp piece is the biggest mistake: it traps moisture and grows mould. Skipping the final unprinted layer is the second: print shows through pale paint. Using strips that are too wide is the third: they wrinkle on curves, so keep them 2 to 3 cm wide. Hanging a heavy piece from one hook is the fourth: it tilts, then falls. And using a hot oven to speed drying matters most: the outside hardens while the inside stays wet, and the piece cracks later. ## FAQ

### How much does DIY paper mache wall art cost?

Most projects cost $3 to $15: a mini moon $3 to $6, a sunburst $5 to $12, gold-leaf $10 to $18.

### How long does paper mache take to dry?

Thin strip work dries in 24 hours, medium pieces in 48 hours, and thick pulp in 3 to 5 days. Large sculptures take 4 to 7 days.

### What is the best paste for paper mache wall art?

Flour paste is cheapest and best for beginners. Glue paste dries harder and clearer and resists mould, so it suits damp rooms. Paper pulp is best for sculpting relief shapes without a mould.

### How do you hang paper mache wall art?

Under 500 g takes one adhesive hook or nail; 500 g to 1.5 kg takes two picture hooks; over 1.5 kg takes two screws into wall plugs. Always weigh the piece first.

### Can kids make paper mache wall art?

Yes: flour paste is non-toxic and safe for children. Adults should handle craft knives, hot glue guns and spray varnish, and seal every child-made piece first.
