An advent calendar is the one Christmas decoration you touch every single day, and a handmade one beats a shop-bought chocolate calendar: it fits the person it is for, refills every year, and becomes a keepsake. Here are 24 buildable calendars, with costs, times, difficulty, hanging safety and filler plans.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Decide the container, location and palette first.
> - Cheapest: numbered paper bags on a string, about £8, one afternoon.
> - Best keepsake: a sewn fabric calendar, about £25, two evenings, refilled every December.
> - A filled wall calendar weighs 2–4 kg — two fixings, never one adhesive hook.
> - Make it in November: first week for sewn, last weekend for paper.

## Three Decisions Before You Start

**Container.** Pockets suit flat fillers; bags suit chunky ones; drawers suit anything but take longest; houses suit display. Choose the container by the filler.

**Location.** A wall calendar needs two fixings and 60 to 90 cm of clear wall; a mantel calendar needs none; a tabletop one needs 40 cm of clear surface away from the edge. Measure first, buy second.

**Palette.** Three colours, maximum: red, green and cream; white, gold and kraft; or dusty pink, sage and cream. One palette reads as designed; a different colour per pocket reads as a jumble.

## Fabric and Felt Calendars: The Heirloom Route

Make one once, refill it every December, and it lasts a decade.

### 1. Sewn fabric pocket calendar

A cotton panel (60 × 90 cm) with 24 pockets in four rows of six, each 12 × 14 cm, sewn on and numbered with iron-on vinyl. Cost: about £25. Time: two evenings. Difficulty: intermediate — identical pockets get faster as you go.

### 2. Felt Christmas tree with Velcro ornaments

A large felt tree (90 cm tall) with 24 small felt ornaments on Velcro dots — one per day, so the tree fills up through December. Cost: about £18. Time: one evening. Difficulty: easy. Best for toddlers: soft, large ornaments.

### 3. Felt truck calendar

A felt truck with 24 pockets in its trailer, appliqued onto a backing panel, suits a child's room. Cost: about £20. Time: one evening. Difficulty: easy to intermediate. Keep pockets 10 cm deep, so small hands reach inside.

### 4. Quilted wall-hanging calendar

A quilted panel with 24 patchwork pockets, bound with bias tape — the heirloom version. Cost: £28 to £35. Time: a weekend. Difficulty: advanced — quilting needs practice; a second-year project. The [DIY fabric wall art](/blog/diy-fabric-wall-art/) guide covers the same patchwork basics.

### 5. Teddy-bear pocket calendar

A felt teddy that moves from pocket to pocket — one pocket per day — turns the countdown into a game. Cost: about £15. Time: two hours. Difficulty: easy. Sew a loop on its paws to hang it on a small button.

## Paper Bag and Envelope Calendars: The One-Afternoon Route

The fastest, most forgiving route: a mistake costs one bag, not one weekend.

### 6. Numbered paper bags on a string

Twenty-four small kraft bags (£3 a pack), each numbered with a sticker or a stamp, pegged to twine with mini pegs (£2), hung from a branch or a rail. Cost: about £8. Time: one afternoon. Difficulty: easy — the best first calendar here.

### 7. Envelope calendar on a hanger

Twenty-four small envelopes (£3) in alternating red and cream, clipped to a wooden coat hanger with mini pegs and hung on a hook by the door. Each envelope holds a flat treat or an activity card. Cost: about £6. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 8. Paper-cone calendar

Twenty-four paper cones, rolled from patterned card and hung in rows from a branch or a dowel, each holding a small sweet. Cost: about £10. Time: two hours. Difficulty: easy. Glue rows to the dowel first, or cones slide together when full.

### 9. Matchbox drawer calendar

Twenty-four small matchboxes (£4 a pack of 24), stacked and glued into a 6 × 4 grid, each drawer painted and numbered. Add a backing board, or boxes slide apart. Cost: about £8. Time: two hours plus paint drying. Difficulty: easy.

### 10. Kraft bag basket calendar

Twenty-four numbered kraft bags standing upright in a basket or a crate on the mantel, each tied with twine. No hanging, no fixings; the basket doubles as storage. Cost: about £8. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy — the simplest calendar here, and the safest with toddlers.

## Wooden Drawer and Box Calendars

The sturdiest, most giftable calendars.

### 11. Wooden drawer organiser calendar

A blank 24-drawer wooden organiser (£18), painted in your palette and numbered with stickers or a paint pen. Each drawer is 6 × 6 × 6 cm — big enough for a small toy. Cost: about £22. Time: two to three hours plus paint drying. Difficulty: easy.

### 12. Mini metal bucket calendar

Twenty-four small metal buckets (£8 for 24) hung from a board with cup hooks, each numbered with a luggage tag. The buckets hold chunky fillers — a bath bomb, socks, an ornament. Cost: about £15. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. Two screws into studs: 24 filled buckets weigh 3 kg.

### 13. Wooden crate calendar

A small wooden crate (£8) with 24 plywood-divider compartments, each holding a treat. It sits on the mantel — no fixings needed. Cost: about £12. Time: two hours. Difficulty: intermediate — cut dividers square. The [DIY wood slice wall art](/blog/diy-wood-slice-wall-art/) guide covers the same discipline.

### 14. Pottery Barn-inspired wood calendar

A long wooden board with 24 small muslin bags (£6) hung from brass hooks in a row, numbered with kraft tags. The board hangs on two fixings; bags lift off one at a time. Cost: about £20. Time: two hours. Difficulty: easy.

## House and Village Calendars

Display pieces first: each house is a tiny scene, and the filler hides inside.

### 15. Gingerbread-house advent

Twenty-four small gingerbread houses, baked from one batch of dough and decorated with royal icing, each holding a sweet inside. They last the month if kept dry. Cost: about £12. Time: one afternoon. Difficulty: intermediate — stiff icing must glue the walls.

### 16. Paper house village

Twenty-four paper houses, cut from a free template on 250 gsm card and folded into shape, arranged as a village. Each house has a numbered door that opens to reveal a treat. Cost: about £8. Time: three to four hours. Difficulty: easy to intermediate. Use 250 gsm card or heavier.

### 17. Cardboard house advent

A row of small cardboard houses, painted white and gold, with numbered doors cut into the fronts, made from cereal boxes and a craft knife. Cost: about £5. Time: three hours. Difficulty: easy. The boxes are free; paint is the only purchase.

## Hanging and Repurposed Calendars

The most creative calendars start as something else — a shoe organiser, a ladder, a set of stockings — and cost almost nothing.

### 18. Over-door shoe-organiser calendar

A clear over-door shoe organiser (£8) with 24 pockets becomes a calendar in ten minutes: number the pockets, fill them, hang it on a door. Cost: about £10. Time: 15 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Keep it on a door nobody slams.

### 19. Mini-stocking calendar

Twenty-four mini stockings (£8 for a pack), hung from twine with mini pegs along a mantel. Each stocking holds a small treat. Cost: about £10. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Two cup hooks, not adhesive: 24 filled stockings weigh 2 kg. The [Christmas stocking display ideas](/blog/christmas-stocking-display-ideas/) guide covers full-size ones too.

### 20. Blanket-ladder calendar

A leaning blanket ladder (£20, or free from a fence offcut) with 24 numbered bags pegged to its rungs, standing in a corner. No fixings, no holes; it folds away in January. Cost: £8 to £25. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 21. Dollar Tree calendar

Twenty-four Dollar Tree finds — mini buckets, tins, gift boxes and bags — arranged on a tray or a board, numbered with stickers. Cost: about £10. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. The [dollar tree Christmas decor DIY](/blog/dollar-tree-christmas-diy/) guide covers the same method.

### 22. Tea advent calendar

Twenty-four tea bags (£4), each in a numbered kraft envelope, pegged to a string: one tea a day, from spiced orange to chamomile. Cost: about £6. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy — best for an adult who skips sugar.

### 23. Activity advent calendar

Twenty-four envelopes, each holding an activity card: bake biscuits, watch a Christmas film, make paper snowflakes. Cost: about £4. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. For a house with too much chocolate — the [Christmas entryway decor ideas](/blog/christmas-entryway-decor-ideas/) guide sets the scene.

### 24. Photo advent calendar

Twenty-four small photos — one per day — pegged to a string with mini pegs, each backed with a note. Cost: about £5. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. Print them at 10 × 15 cm and keep the string out of direct sun.

## 24-Day Filler Plans: Kids, Adults, Couples and No-Sugar

Four plans, 24 days each — mix and match, never repeat a filler.

**Kids (ages 4 to 8).** Stickers, dinosaur, ball, crayons, chocolate coin, hair clip, puzzle, puppet, bath bomb, cutter, notebook, glow star, car, bracelet kit, pencil, bubble wand, tattoo, hot chocolate, snow globe, storybook, antlers, gingerbread kit, torch, letter from Santa (24th).

**Adults.** Tea, truffle, lip balm, tea light, bath salts, hand cream, coffee pod, face mask, ornament, biscuit, nail polish, playlist, jam, scented sachet, puzzle, mulled-wine spices, socks, photo, mini fizz, bauble, recipe, bath bomb, note, gift voucher (24th).

**Couples.** Alternate days, and make half the fillers shared: a date-night card, a film-night ticket, a recipe to cook together, a photo memory, a massage voucher, a hot-chocolate kit for two. The rest are small personal treats, one each.

**No-sugar, activities only.** Every envelope holds an activity: day 1, snowflakes; day 5, gingerbread; day 10, a Christmas film; day 15, a neighbour's card; day 20, a blanket fort; day 24, a Christmas story. Write all 24 cards in one sitting.

**Size rule.** Measure the filler first: a 12 × 14 cm pocket holds a tea bag, not a toy car; a 6 cm drawer holds a bath bomb, not a book. Build around the biggest.

## Hanging Safety and Storage: Making It Last Ten Years

A filled wall calendar weighs 2 to 4 kg — the fillers weigh more than the calendar. Hang it on two fixings, 30 cm apart, into studs or plugs rated for 5 kg. Never use one adhesive hook: it holds the empty calendar and fails at 3 kg on a cold wall. Test the fixings for an hour first.

Keep cords out of reach: hang bag calendars at adult eye level, the bottom bag 90 cm from the floor, and keep pegs and strings away from the crib. Keep real candles away from paper and fabric; use LED tea lights.

After Christmas, empty every pocket, brush out the crumbs, and fold fabric calendars flat in a labelled box with a lavender sachet — never a damp loft, where mould eats cotton. Put the filler list in the lid.

## FAQ

### When should I start making a DIY advent calendar?

Sewn or quilted: the first week of November. Paper or bags: the last weekend. Never start after the 28th.

### How much does a DIY advent calendar cost?

From £6 for an envelope calendar to £30 for a quilted heirloom; a good first one costs about £15 all in.

### How do I hang a heavy advent calendar safely?

Two fixings 30 cm apart into studs or plugs rated for 5 kg — a filled calendar weighs 2 to 4 kg, so one adhesive hook is never enough.

### What can I put in an advent calendar instead of chocolate?

Tea bags, small toys, stickers, activity cards, bath bombs, lip balm, socks, photos and notes. The no-sugar plan covers 24 activity-only days.

### Can I reuse a DIY advent calendar next year?

Yes — fabric, felt and wood calendars are made to refill. Empty every pocket, store flat in a labelled box, and keep a filler list in the lid.
