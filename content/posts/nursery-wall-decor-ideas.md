A nursery wall should soothe a newborn, delight a toddler, and survive both. The best DIY nursery decor is soft, safe to touch, cheap to make and easy to change as the baby grows. Here are 21 ideas by style and age, with real costs, a safety checklist, and renter-friendly options.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Start above the crib: one focal piece — a name sign or a rainbow — anchors the room for under £25.
> - Peel-and-stick decals are the best renter option: removable, repositionable, gone without a mark.
> - Never hang anything heavy, glass or looped above or within reach of the crib — the checklist below is not optional.
> - Use zero-VOC paint only, and finish painted projects at least two weeks before the baby arrives.
> - Plan two stages: a calm newborn wall, then a toddler wall with lower, touchable pieces.

## Nursery Safety First: The Checklist Before You Hang Anything

Read this first: in a nursery, a falling object or a loose cord is a real danger.

**Nothing heavy over the crib.** Never hang a picture, mirror, shelf, basket or glass above or within 60 cm of the crib. Lightweight fabric, paper or felt only; heavier pieces go opposite.

**No cords, strings or loops within reach.** Blind cords, garland strings, bunting ties and mobile cords are strangulation hazards. Keep every cord out of reach, tie bunting with short knots, and keep mobile cords under 18 cm.

**No small parts a baby can pull off.** Buttons, beads, pom-poms and felt pieces must be glued or stitched firmly, never pinned. If it comes off in your hand, a baby can pull it off — anything under 4 cm across is a choking hazard.

**Anchor everything heavy.** Strap every shelf, bookcase or wall storage to a stud, even if it looks stable. A climbing toddler can pull an unanchored shelf down.

**Use zero-VOC paint and finishes only.** Ordinary paint releases fumes for days. Use zero-VOC paint, finish painted projects two weeks before the due date, and ventilate for 48 hours. Varnish and stain: water-based, zero-VOC, fully cured first.

**Mind the hanging height.** Art above the crib sits at adult eye level — centre about 150 cm from the floor. Toddler art sits at 90 to 110 cm, where a standing child can see and touch it safely. Never hang anything a toddler can pull down.

## Boho Nursery Walls: Rainbows, Macrame and Soft Neutrals

Boho dominates nursery Pinterest: soft neutrals, natural textures and rainbows soothe a newborn. Keep the palette to blush, terracotta, sage, cream and beige — bright primaries overstimulate a baby.

### 1. Crochet rainbow wall hanging

Chunky yarn in blush, terracotta and cream, crocheted into three or four arches, makes the most-saved nursery DIY. A free pattern and £8 of yarn make a 40 cm rainbow in an evening; it weighs almost nothing, so it hangs safely above the crib on one nail. Cost £8–£12, 2–3 hours, easy. Pair it with a [macrame wall hanging](/blog/macrame-wall-hanging/).

### 2. Macrame rainbow mobile

A small macrame rainbow on a wooden ring, hung from the ceiling or a wall hook, doubles as a mobile. Keep the cord short and out of reach, and use cotton cord, not wool, so it never sheds fibres near the baby. Cost £10–£15, 2 hours, easy to intermediate.

### 3. Pom-pom rainbow garland

Yarn pom-poms in a rainbow gradient, strung on a cord along one wall, add colour without paint. Make them with a cardboard template and knot each firmly so a toddler cannot pull it off. Cost £5–£8, 1 hour, easy.

### 4. Felt cloud and raindrop mobile

Cut clouds and raindrops from felt in cream, grey and dusty blue, stitch or glue them onto ribbons, and hang them from a wooden hoop. Felt is soft, light and silent if it falls. Cost £6–£10, 1 hour, easy.

### 5. Woven wall hanging above the crib

A small woven hanging in cream and terracotta, made on a £10 lap loom or a cardboard loom, adds texture without weight. Keep it under 50 cm wide. Cost £10–£15, 3 hours, intermediate. The [DIY fabric wall art](/blog/diy-fabric-wall-art/) guide covers the same weaving basics.

## Name Signs and Personalised Walls

A name above the crib is the most personal piece in any nursery, and the one parents keep longest. Make it once, well, and it moves to the big-kid room.

### 6. Wooden name sign

A laser-cut or hand-cut wooden name in a script font, painted cream or sage, mounted above the crib with two small nails. A 60 cm name in 12 mm plywood costs about £15 from a maker, or £8 cut yourself. Cost £8–£20, 1 hour plus drying, easy. Hang it at 150 cm, never over the baby's head if it weighs over 500 grams.

### 7. Acrylic name sign with fairy lights

A clear acrylic name (£12) with battery fairy lights (£4) looped behind it glows softly at night and doubles as a night light. Use warm white LEDs only, and keep the battery pack out of reach. Cost £16–£20, 30 minutes, easy.

### 8. Embroidered birth-details hoop

Stitch the baby's name, birth date and weight in backstitch on linen in a 25 cm embroidery hoop — a keepsake costing about £8 and taking an evening. The [DIY embroidery hoop wall art](/blog/diy-embroidery-hoop-wall-art/) guide teaches the five stitches you need.

### 9. Name banner and bunting

Triangle-flag bunting spelling the baby's name, made from cotton fabric scraps, strung opposite the crib. Each flag is 12 cm wide, the string is fixed with short knots, and the banner costs £5. Time 1 hour, easy.

## Decals and Painted Walls: The Big-Impact, Low-Cost Route

For the biggest change for the least money, change the wall itself. Decals and paint transform a nursery in an afternoon.

### 10. Peel-and-stick boho rainbow decals

Watercolour rainbow, daisy or polka-dot decals (£10–£15) turn a plain wall into a feature wall in 20 minutes. Peel, stick, and smooth bubbles with a credit card. They remove cleanly and leave no mark. Cost £10–£15, 20 minutes, easy.

### 11. Moon-and-stars celestial decals

Gold or cream moon-and-stars decals above the crib suit a calm, sleepy palette and catch the evening light. A set costs £8–£12. Apply them in a loose scatter, not a grid. Cost £8–£12, 20 minutes, easy.

### 12. Painted scallop border

A scalloped border along the top of the wall, in one soft colour, is the cheapest high-impact nursery DIY: a tin of zero-VOC paint (£15), a cardboard scallop template, and a small roller. One wall takes an afternoon. Cost £15–£20, 3 hours, easy-intermediate.

### 13. Half-wall paint dip

Paint the lower half in soft sage or dusty pink, leave the top half cream, with a crisp line at 90 cm. It grounds the room, hides scuffs at toddler height, and costs one tin of paint. Cost £15–£20, 2 hours, easy. Use zero-VOC paint, finished two weeks before the due date.

### 14. Hot-air-balloon and cloud decal scene

A large hot-air-balloon decal (£12) with a few cloud decals makes a storybook corner above a reading chair. Cost £12–£18, 30 minutes, easy.

## Woodland and Animal Walls

Woodland animals suit a nursery that grows into a toddler's room: a fox, bear or deer reads as sweet for a baby and as adventure for a toddler.

### 15. Woodland animal print gallery

Three framed prints — a fox, a deer and a bear — in simple wooden frames, hung in a row opposite the crib. Print the art yourself for £5 or buy prints for £15. Cost £10–£25, 30 minutes, easy. Centre them at 150 cm, with acrylic glazing, not glass.

### 16. Felt animal heads

Felt fox, bear and bunny heads, stuffed lightly and mounted on wooden plaques, give a three-dimensional woodland wall for about £15. Cost £12–£18, 2 hours, intermediate. Sew every piece firmly.

### 17. Growth chart ruler

A wooden growth chart (£10 for a blank board, or free from a fence offcut), painted with height markings, becomes decor a child uses for years. Mount it at toddler height, starting 60 cm from the floor. Cost £8–£15, 2 hours, easy.

## Grow-With-Baby Walls: Newborn to Toddler

Plan the wall in two stages and avoid repainting in a year: stage one calm and high, stage two, from twelve months, low and touchable.

**Newborn stage (0 to 12 months).** One focal piece above the crib — a rainbow, a name sign, a decal scene — and nothing else within 60 cm of it. The baby sees contrast and gentle shapes.

**Toddler stage (12 months and up).** Move the focal piece higher if the child can reach it, and add a low gallery at 90 to 110 cm: felt animals, a book sling, a chalkboard or a magnetic board with large magnets. Everything there must be light and soft.

**The transition plan.** When the baby starts pulling up, around eight to ten months, walk the room at toddler height and look at every wall from 90 cm. Anything you can grab, pull or chew needs moving or removing. Re-hang the name sign higher and take down the bunting and mobile.

### 18. Fabric book sling

A fabric sling (£10 of cotton and two dowels) mounted at toddler height holds six picture books with their covers facing out — decor and a reading habit in one. Cost £10–£15, 1 hour, easy. Anchor it into a stud.

### 19. Felt story board

A felt board (£8) with felt animals and shapes that stick by friction gives a toddler a quiet activity on the wall. Make the pieces large — nothing under 5 cm — and store spares in a wall basket. Cost £8–£12, 1 hour, easy.

### 20. Chalkboard or magnetic board, toddler height

A small chalkboard (£10) or magnetic board (£12) at 90 cm gives a toddler a drawing wall that does not involve your walls. Use chunky chalk and large magnets only, and wipe it daily. Cost £10–£15, 20 minutes, easy.

### 21. Wall storage baskets as decor

Two or three woven baskets (£8 each) mounted at adult height hold nappies, muslins and toys, and look like decor while doing a job. Mount each on a hook into a stud — a full basket weighs 2 kg, and a toddler will pull at the lowest one. Cost £16–£25, 30 minutes, easy. ## Renter-Friendly Nursery Decor

Renters can build almost every idea here without drilling a hole. Peel-and-stick decals remove cleanly. Command strips hold anything under 1 kg — the crochet rainbow, felt mobile, embroidery hoop and name banner — and two strips hold 1.5 kg. Washi tape frames a print without a nail, and leaning a print on a dresser or [picture ledge](/blog/picture-ledge-wall-decor/) needs no fixing.

Painted ideas need a landlord's yes, so ask first and offer to repaint before you leave. If the answer is no, decals in the same colours give 80 percent of the look. The [renter-friendly wall decor](/blog/renter-friendly-wall-decor/) guide covers the fixings.

## FAQ

### What is safe to hang above a crib?

Only lightweight, soft pieces: fabric, felt, paper or a crochet hanging under 500 grams. Never hang anything heavy, glass or looped above or within 60 cm.

### What paint is safe for a nursery?

Zero-VOC paint only, water-based, fully cured. Finish at least two weeks before the due date and ventilate for 48 hours.

### How do I decorate a nursery in a rented home?

Use peel-and-stick decals, Command strips under 1 kg, washi tape frames, and leaning art on a dresser or picture ledge. Ask your landlord first.

### When should I change nursery decor for a toddler?

From about twelve months, or as soon as the baby pulls up to standing. Move focal pieces higher, remove cords and small parts, and add low pieces at 90–110 cm.

### What colours work best for a nursery wall?

Soft neutrals and pastels: blush, terracotta, sage, cream and beige. Bright primaries overstimulate a baby — save them for toys.
