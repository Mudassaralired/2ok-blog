## Classic Bamboo Wall Art

### 1. Bamboo pole sunburst mirror

Cut 24 thin bamboo sticks to lengths from 30 to 55 cm and glue them in a fan around a round mirror about 30 cm across, so the sticks radiate like sun rays. Cost: $8 to $15. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 2. Bamboo stick star wall art

Lay five straight bamboo sticks, each about 45 cm long, into a five-pointed star and lash every crossing with jute twine. Cost: $2 to $5. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 3. Bamboo ladder wall hanging

Lash five short bamboo sticks between two long poles to make a ladder about 50 by 80 cm, then hang it flat on the wall. Cost: $4 to $9. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 4. Bamboo circle wall wreath

Bend thin, flexible bamboo strips into a 45 cm circle, bind the overlaps with twine, and weave shorter strips around the ring until it looks full. Cost: $3 to $7. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

## Statement Bamboo Pieces

### 5. Large bamboo pole wall hanging

Mount one thick bamboo pole, 100 to 120 cm long, horizontally on the wall with two brackets, then hang strands of twine, wooden beads and dried flowers from it at different lengths. Cost: $6 to $14. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 6. Bamboo slat wall panel

Fix 12 to 16 vertical bamboo poles side by side on two hidden horizontal battens to make a slat panel about 90 by 120 cm. Cost: $12 to $22. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 7. Woven bamboo strip wall hanging

Weave thin bamboo strips over and under a frame of thicker sticks to make a hanging about 60 by 80 cm, then thread wooden beads onto twine tassels along the bottom edge. Cost: $5 to $11. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Soak the strips in warm water for 30 minutes first. It pairs well with the [DIY woven wall hanging ideas](/blog/diy-woven-wall-hanging-ideas) guide.

### 8. Bamboo hexagon wall tiles

Cut split bamboo strips into short lengths and glue them into hexagon outlines, then arrange six or seven hexagons in a honeycomb cluster on the wall. Cost: $6 to $12. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

## Small-Space and Bedroom Bamboo Art

### 9. Bamboo photo clip wall

Fix a straight bamboo pole 80 cm long horizontally on the wall, then clip photos to it with small wooden pegs on short twine loops. Cost: $2 to $6. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Use two screws into wall plugs.

### 10. Bamboo jewellery wall hanger

Mount a short bamboo pole with plenty of small side pegs horizontally on a bedroom wall and hang necklaces, bracelets and earrings from the pegs. Cost: $3 to $7. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Pick pegs pointing slightly upward.

### 11. Mini bamboo frame trio

Make three small square frames, 20 cm across, from short bamboo sticks joined at the corners with twine, and hang them in a row with a small print or pressed leaf inside each. Cost: $3 to $8. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. See the [DIY wall decor ideas](/blog/diy-wall-decor-ideas) pillar.

## Woven, Lit and Living Bamboo Art

### 12. Bamboo wind chime wall art

Hang five bamboo tubes of different lengths, 15 to 30 cm, from a small bamboo ring or a horizontal stick, so they tap together gently in a draught. Cost: $4 to $9. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 13. Bamboo fairy-light panel

Build a frame from four bamboo poles, stretch twine across it in a grid, and weave a 2-metre battery LED fairy light string through it. Cost: $7 to $13. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Use battery LEDs only, never mains lights near dry bamboo.

### 14. Bamboo wall plant holder

Lash a small bamboo frame around a hanging planter or a terracotta pot, then mount it on the wall with two brackets so a trailing plant spills down over the bamboo. Cost: $5 to $12. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Line the pot with a plastic saucer so water never touches the bamboo. For spacing, see the [above sofa wall decor](/blog/above-sofa-wall-decor) guide.

## Which Bamboo to Pick: Poles, Sticks, Strips and Mat

Bamboo comes in four useful forms, and each one suits different projects. Poles are whole hollow canes, 2 to 5 cm thick: use them for ladders, slat panels and anything structural. Sticks are thin poles under 2 cm: best for stars, frames and sunburst rays. Split strips are poles cut lengthways into flat ribbons for weaving. Woven mat is ready-made bamboo sheeting sold by the roll: use it as a backdrop or cut it into panels.

| Bamboo form | Best for | Thickness | Price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Poles | Ladders, panels, hangings | 2 to 5 cm | $2 to $4 each | Strongest form |
| Sticks | Stars, frames, rays | Under 2 cm | $3 to $6 a bundle | Easy to cut |
| Split strips | Weaving, hexagons | 5 to 10 mm wide | $4 to $8 a pack | Soak before bending |
| Woven mat | Backdrops, shelf backing | 1 to 2 mm sheet | $6 to $12 a roll | Cut with scissors |

Buy poles from a garden centre and sticks and strips from a craft store. Check every pole for cracks, mould spots and tiny holes, which mean insects. Fresh green bamboo is heavy and wet: it shrinks and splits as it dries, so buy cured, tan-coloured bamboo that feels dry and light. If a pole feels damp or smells musty, leave it on the shelf.

## Cutting and Splitting Bamboo Safely

Bamboo splits along its length if you force it, so the cutting method matters more than the tool. Use a fine-tooth saw with at least 18 teeth per inch: coarse teeth grab the fibres and split the cane. Always cut just above a node, the solid ring where the pole is strongest, and never cut through a node itself. Support both sides of the cut so the pole cannot drop and crack, then saw slowly with light pressure and let the teeth do the work.

To split a pole into strips, score a line down its length, then tap a thin chisel along the score with a mallet. Split from one node to the next, never across a node. Wear gloves and safety glasses: split bamboo edges are razor sharp. Sand every cut end with 120-grit sandpaper, then finish with 220-grit. Work outside or over a sheet: bamboo dust gets everywhere.

## Sealing Bamboo Against Insects and Humidity

Raw bamboo attracts powderpost beetles and absorbs bathroom humidity, so seal every piece before it goes on the wall. Start with a bug check: look for tiny round holes and fine sawdust, and reject any pole that has them. Heat-treat small pieces in an oven at 100 C for 30 minutes, or leave larger poles in strong sun for two days. Then brush on two thin coats of clear matt varnish or tung oil. Varnish costs about $6 a can and one can seals a dozen projects.

In humid rooms, choose varnish over oil: oil feeds mould in damp air, while varnish forms a hard barrier. Never hang unsealed bamboo in a bathroom: steam swells the fibres and the piece warps within weeks. Re-seal pieces once a year in humid homes, and check the back of each piece every six months for mould or beetle dust. ## Sizing Your Bamboo Wall Decor

Size the piece to the wall, not to the bamboo you happen to have. Above a sofa, the piece should span about two thirds of the sofa width: a 180 cm sofa takes a 120 cm pole hanging. In a hallway, keep the piece under 15 cm deep from the wall and choose a flat piece like the frame trio. Lay the finished piece on the floor first and photograph it from the doorway. Hang it so its centre sits at eye level, about 145 cm from the floor, or 15 to 20 cm above furniture.

## Hanging Bamboo Wall Decor by Weight

Bamboo is light for its size, but a slat panel or a shelf still adds up: a large pole hanging can weigh 2 to 3 kg, while a small star weighs under 300 g. Weigh the finished piece before choosing hardware, and always hang from two points on pieces over 1 kg. Use a picture wire across the back, not a single loop of twine, which stretches and lets the piece tilt.

| Finished weight | Hardware | Fixings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 500 g | Picture nail or adhesive hook | 1 |
| 500 g to 1.5 kg | Two picture hooks, wire back | 2 |
| 1.5 kg to 3 kg | Two 3 kg hooks, wire back | 2 |
| Over 3 kg | Two screws into wall plugs or a stud | 2 |

Renters should favour picture hooks over nails. Never hang a piece over 1 kg from a single adhesive hook, or any bamboo piece from a bare nail in drywall. Check the fixings every six months.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Cutting with a coarse saw is the biggest mistake: it splits the cane along its length and ruins the pole. Skipping the sealing step is the second: beetles and humidity attack unsealed bamboo within months. Cutting through a node is the third: the pole cracks at the joint. Hanging a heavy panel from one hook is the fourth: the piece tilts, then falls. Using green, uncured bamboo is the fifth: it shrinks as it dries and loosens every lashing. Mixing too many bamboo tones in one piece is the sixth: pale sticks and dark poles clash. And using mains fairy lights near dry bamboo matters most: battery LEDs give the same glow with none of the risk.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY bamboo wall decor cost?

Most projects cost $2 to $15: a star or frame $2 to $6, a ladder $3 to $8, and a large slat panel $12 to $22.

### What type of bamboo is best for wall decor?

Cured, tan-coloured bamboo that feels dry and light: poles for structure, sticks for stars and frames, split strips for weaving, woven mat for backdrops.

### How do you cut bamboo without splitting it?

Use a fine-tooth saw with at least 18 teeth per inch, cut just above a node, support both sides, and saw slowly.

### Does bamboo wall decor need sealing?

Yes: brush on two thin coats of clear matt varnish or tung oil before hanging. Sealing stops powderpost beetles and keeps humidity from warping the piece.

### How do you hang bamboo wall decor?

Under 500 g takes one picture nail or adhesive hook; 500 g to 1.5 kg takes two picture hooks with a wire back; over 1.5 kg takes two screws into wall plugs or a stud.

### Can I make bamboo wall decor without any tools?

Yes: a star, ladder or photo clip wall needs only bamboo, twine and a saw, and split strips can be bought ready-cut.
