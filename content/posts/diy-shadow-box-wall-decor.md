A shadow box is a frame with depth — a shallow, glass-fronted box that turns flat walls into display cabinets for things a normal frame cannot hold. Ticket stubs, dried flowers, a baby's first shoes, a row of shells: all of it sits behind glass, dust-free and lit like a tiny museum. These 15 DIY shadow box wall decor ideas cover what to fill them with, how deep to build them and how to hang them, with costs for every version.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Match the depth to the contents: 2.5 to 5 cm for paper and fabric, 8 to 15 cm for shoes, trophies and bottles.
> - A ready-made box costs £8 to £25; a deep picture frame plus balsa spacers builds one for about £6.
> - Line the back with linen, velvet or cork — the backing does half the styling work.
> - Fix contents with pins and putty first, glue last, so you can rearrange.
> - A filled box over 2 kg needs a wall anchor or a stud, never a nail.

## Shadow Box Basics: Depth, Size and Backing

Depth is the one decision that decides everything else. Too shallow and the glass crushes the contents; too deep and small items rattle in a cave. Work from the bulkiest thing you plan to display and add 1 cm of air in front of it.

| Contents | Box depth needed | Typical box size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tickets, postcards, pressed flowers | 2.5–4 cm | 20 × 25 cm |
| Photos with fabric, lace, jewellery | 4–6 cm | 25 × 25 cm |
| Shells, corks, medals, small toys | 6–10 cm | 30 × 30 cm |
| Baby shoes, trophies, bottles | 10–15 cm | 30 × 40 cm or larger |

Standard sizes — 20 × 25 cm, 30 × 30 cm, 40 × 50 cm — are the cheapest because the glass is cut in bulk. A ready-made box in those sizes costs £8 to £25 depending on the frame. Odd sizes mean building your own, which is a pleasant hour's work (see the build section below).

Backing does more styling than the frame. Linen in a warm cream makes dried flowers and shells glow; black velvet makes medals, jewellery and ticket stubs pop; cork suits corks and travel pins because you can pin straight into it. Cover a sheet of foam board with the fabric, pulled tight and taped at the back — never glue the face, which wrinkles.

Glass versus acrylic: glass is clearer and cheaper but heavy in big sizes. Acrylic is half the weight and safer over a bed or sofa, and UV-filtering acrylic protects contents from fading — worth the extra £3 for anything paper or fabric. Most of the ideas below are part of the wider [DIY wall decor guide](/blog/diy-wall-decor-guide/), but the shadow box is the only one that protects what it displays.

## Memory and Keepsake Shadow Boxes

### 1. Travel memory box

Back the box with a map of the trip, then layer the evidence over it: two or three ticket stubs, a coin or two, a photo and one small souvenir, angled, not gridded. A 30 × 30 cm box at 5 cm deep holds a whole holiday. Cost: £10 to £18 for the box. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Leave one corner empty — a crowded box reads as clutter, a spaced one as a story.

### 2. Wedding keepsake box

The invitation goes centre-back on foam risers so it floats 1 cm off the backing. Around it: a few dried flowers from the bouquet, a place card, a ribbon and one photo in the corner. Use a cream linen backing and the shallowest box that fits — 4 cm is plenty. Cost: £12 to £20. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. Dried flowers must be fully dry for a week before the glass goes on, or they will mould behind it.

### 3. Baby's first-year box

One pair of first shoes, the hospital wristband, a first photo and a small footprint print fill a 10 cm deep box without crowding it. Back it with the receiving blanket's fabric or a pale linen. Cost: £12 to £22. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Everything here is irreplaceable, so fix with museum putty and pins only — no glue — and hang it where it will never sit in direct sun.

### 4. Concert and ticket-stub box

Rows of ticket stubs on matte black backing, tilted at one consistent angle, look like a record sleeve. Print tickets count too — reprint faded thermal stubs on matte photo paper before they blank. A shallow 3 cm box is enough, and a 40 × 50 cm frame holds about twenty stubs. Cost: £8 to £15. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Add one photograph of the crowd or stage to break the grid.

### 5. Medals and sports memorabilia box

Medals hang from a ribbon bar pinned across the top third; below them sit a team photo and, for ball sports, the ball itself on a small acrylic stand in a 12 to 15 cm deep box. Velvet backing in navy or burgundy is the classic choice. Cost: £18 to £30 for the deep box. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. Weigh it before hanging — a ball and three medals pass 2 kg easily, which means an anchor, not a nail.

## Nature and Art Shadow Boxes

### 6. Dried flower and botanical box

Lavender, eucalyptus, dried roses and grasses arranged loose on cream linen — the look on this page's cover. Pin stems from behind the backing so no fixings show, and keep the arrangement to one side with empty space opposite. Cost: £10 to £16. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. The [pressed flower wall art](/blog/pressed-flower-wall-art/) guide covers pressing your own; pressed pieces sit flatter in a shallow box, while whole dried stems need 5 cm or more.

### 7. Shell and sand-dollar beach box

Sand dollars and pale shells on unbleached linen, biggest pieces at the centre, small ones radiating out. A drop of hot glue on the back of each shell holds it; sand dollars are fragile, so bed them on a dab of museum putty instead. A 30 × 40 cm box suits a holiday's worth of finds. Cost: £10 to £15. Time: 40 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Rinse shells in fresh water and dry for two days first — sealed-in salt smells forever.

### 8. Paper-cut light box

Five to eight layers of white card, each cut with a simpler silhouette than the one in front — hills, then trees, then a moon — spaced 8 mm apart on foam strips with an LED strip around the inside edge. Lit, the layers glow like a stage set. It needs a box 8 to 10 cm deep and a craft knife, or a cutting machine. Cost: £12 to £18. Time: two to three hours. Difficulty: intermediate. Use a battery LED strip with a timer: no cable, and it switches itself off.

![Backlit paper-cut light box with layered pine trees and a crescent moon in a dark wood frame on a wooden table at dusk](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-shadow-box-wall-decor/in-article-1)

### 9. Paper flower box

Rolled cardstock roses in a tight grid — one colour family, three shades — fill a shallow box with pure pattern. A 25 × 25 cm box takes about thirty roses, each a 10 cm circle of card spiral-cut and rolled. Cost: £6 to £10, mostly card. Time: two hours, mostly rolling. Difficulty: easy. It is the cheapest box in this guide, and the one that photographs best.

### 10. Paper butterfly specimen box

Cut or buy paper butterflies and mount each on a 1 cm foam riser at a slight angle, wings lifted, in rows like a Victorian specimen case — all the beauty, no real insects. Print a small typed label under each for the full museum effect. Cost: £8 to £12. Time: one hour. Difficulty: easy. Limit it to three colours or it stops looking like a collection and starts looking like a party.

## Collections and Gallery Shadow Boxes

### 11. Wine cork collection box

A box with a slot or hinged top lets corks drop in over months until the box fills itself. Buy one made for the job (£15 to £25) with the opening at the top and a solid back, and write the date on special corks before they go in. Time: five minutes to hang. Difficulty: easy. Full, it weighs 2 to 3 kg — hang it on an anchor from day one, not when it gets heavy.

### 12. Scrabble tile message box

Tiles glued to the backing spell a name, a date or a short line — a family name across the middle, first names crossing it crossword-style. A shallow 3 cm box, matte grey or cork backing, and tiles at about £4 a bag second-hand. Cost: £8 to £12 total. Time: 40 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Lay the whole design out on the table and photograph it before any glue comes out.

### 13. Vintage key and hardware box

Old keys, a brass hinge, a skeleton lock and a coil of picture wire on dark stained wood or hessian — the steampunk corner of the shadow box world; the box on this page's cover has a key at its centre. Screw small items through the backing rather than gluing; metal works loose from glue as it warms. Cost: £10 to £18, mostly the finds. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 14. Mini shelf box

Fit two or three tiny ledges — balsa strips 2 cm deep — inside a 10 cm box and it becomes a dollhouse shelf: miniature books, a thimble vase, tiny plants. Everything glued or puttied, nothing valuable, because the charm is the smallness. Cost: £8 to £14. Time: one to two hours. Difficulty: intermediate. A ruler and a steady hand matter more than budget here; one crooked ledge shows at a glance.

### 15. Shadow box gallery wall

Three to five boxes of different sizes, frames in one finish, hung as a group with 5 cm gaps, read as one artwork with real depth — the strongest statement in this guide. Tape the layout on the wall first and live with it for a day. Cost: £30 to £60 depending on boxes. Time: half a day. Difficulty: intermediate. The [gallery wall layouts](/blog/gallery-wall-layouts/) guide has the spacing templates, and a [picture ledge](/blog/picture-ledge-wall-decor/) under the group catches the eye line and lets you restyle the ledge instead of the wall.

## How to Build a Shadow Box From a Deep Frame

Start with any frame at least 4 cm deep — charity-shop frames with deep mouldings are ideal at £2 to £5. If the frame is shallow, build the depth: glue balsa or pine strips, painted to match, around the inside edge to make a spacer frame of the depth you need.

Cut foam board to the inside size for the back. Cover it with fabric pulled tight and taped behind, then arrange everything on it flat, on the table. Pin fabric and paper items with dressmaker's pins pushed in at an angle; stand objects on foam risers; hold three-dimensional pieces with museum putty. Photograph the layout before fixing anything permanently, and only glue when you would not move a single piece.

Drop the arranged backing into the frame, add the spacer if you built one, then the glass, then the back board. Seal the back edges with brown tape to keep dust out — dust inside a sealed box is forever.

Hanging follows weight. Under 1 kg, one nail or a large adhesive hook. From 1 to 3 kg, a screw into a wall plug or a plasterboard anchor. Over 3 kg, a stud or two anchors sharing the load. Hang the centre of a single box at 145 to 155 cm from the floor — eye level for art — and keep boxes off the wall directly above radiators, where heat warps backing fabric.

## Keeping Contents From Fading and Slipping

Sunlight is the enemy: a south-facing wall will fade a ticket stub to blank in one summer. Choose UV-filtering acrylic for paper and fabric, and hang memory boxes on the wall that gets light last, not first. Acid-free foam board and backing matter for anything you would grieve — ordinary cardboard yellows and stains what touches it within a few years.

Organic contents — dried flowers, shells, corks — go in bone dry, with a small silica-gel sachet taped behind the backing to absorb the damp of the first weeks. If anything inside can shift, it will, the first time a door slams: putty under every loose item, pins through every fabric one, and test the finished box face-down and shaken gently before it goes on the wall.

## FAQ

### How deep should a shadow box be?

As deep as your bulkiest item plus 1 cm of air. Tickets and pressed flowers need only 2.5 to 4 cm; shells and medals 6 to 10 cm; shoes and trophies 10 to 15 cm.

### How do I hang a heavy shadow box?

Weigh it first. Under 1 kg hangs on a nail; 1 to 3 kg needs a wall plug or plasterboard anchor; over 3 kg needs a stud or two anchors. A full cork box or a ball display passes 2 kg easily.

### What glue is best inside a shadow box?

Use fixings you can undo first: pins for fabric and paper, museum putty for objects, foam risers under photos. Hot glue suits shells and corks. Glue metal last — screws hold it better.

### How do I stop the contents fading?

UV-filtering acrylic, acid-free backing, and a wall out of direct sun. Paper and dried flowers fade fastest; give irreplaceable items all three protections.

### Can renters hang shadow boxes?

Yes. A box under 2 kg hangs on adhesive hooks rated for the weight, and small boxes can lean on a picture ledge instead of hanging at all.
