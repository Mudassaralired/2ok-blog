DIY Christmas table runner ideas start with one strip of fabric, and that strip does more for the table than almost any other decoration. A runner sets the colour scheme, protects the wood from hot dishes, and gives candles and greenery a base. The 15 builds below are runner-only: each covers fabric, yardage, finishing and real cost. For the rest of the table, see our [Christmas table decor](/blog/christmas-table-decor) guide.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - All 15 runners cost $5 to $22; most cost under $15.
> - Five builds need no sewing at all, and two sewn builds include full cut lists.
> - Measure the table first: a runner should hang 15 to 30 cm past each end.
> - Burlap is cheapest and easiest; velvet looks richest but needs care.
> - Roll runners for storage, never fold them, or the creases stay.

## No-Sew Christmas Table Runners

### 1. Five-minute drop-cloth runner

Cut a painter's drop cloth into a strip 35 cm wide and as long as your table plus 60 cm. The raw edges fray slightly, which suits the rustic look. Cost: $5 to $8. Time: 5 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 2. Burlap runner with iron-on hem tape

Cut burlap 40 cm wide to your table length plus 60 cm, fold each long edge under 2 cm, and press iron-on hem tape along the fold. No needle or machine, and the tape holds all season. Fringe the short ends by pulling threads out. Cost: $7 to $10. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

### 3. Kraft paper runner with stencilled trees

Roll out kraft paper 35 cm wide, tape it flat, and stencil Christmas trees down its length with green paint and a foam brush. Let it dry for an hour. Guests can write on it, and it goes straight into recycling after dinner. Cost: $6 to $9. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 4. No-sew felt runner with cut snowflakes

Cut thick white felt 35 cm wide, fold it in half lengthways, and cut half-snowflakes along the fold so they open into full ones. Lay it over a red or green tablecloth. Felt does not fray. Cost: $8 to $11. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 5. Faux-fur strip runner

Cut white faux fur 30 cm wide and glue red grosgrain ribbon down each long edge with a hot-glue gun. The fur hides the glue line. Cost: $12 to $16. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy.

## Beginner Sewn Christmas Table Runners

### 6. Classic plaid flannel runner with mitred corners

Cut plaid flannel 40 cm wide by your table length plus 60 cm, and plain cotton the same size for lining. Sew right sides together with a 1 cm seam, leaving a 15 cm gap, then turn and press. Mitre the corners by folding each into a triangle before stitching. Topstitch 5 mm from the edge. Cost: $10 to $14. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 7. Reversible red-and-green cotton runner

Cut one red and one green cotton piece, each 38 cm wide by your table length plus 60 cm, plus fusible interfacing the same size. Iron the interfacing to the red piece, sew right sides together with a 1 cm seam, leave a gap, turn and topstitch. Cost: $9 to $13. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

![Hands ironing a red Christmas table runner with holly embroidery on a wooden table, with a measuring tape, thread spools and scissors nearby](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-christmas-table-runner-ideas/in-article-1)

### 8. Velvet runner with gold binding

Cut emerald or burgundy velvet 36 cm wide by your table length plus 50 cm, plus cotton lining the same size. Sew together, turn and press with a cool iron through a cloth. Finish the long edges with gold bias binding. Cost: $15 to $20. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 9. Linen runner with embroidered holly

Cut natural linen 38 cm wide by your table length plus 60 cm and hem all four edges with a 1 cm double-fold hem. Embroider a holly sprig near each end in green backstitch and red French knots. Cost: $12 to $16. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 10. Patchwork strip runner from fat quarters

Cut six fat quarters into strips 10 cm wide and as long as your runner, alternating reds, greens and creams. Sew them along their long edges with a 6 mm seam, press the seams open, and back with cotton. Cost: $14 to $18. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

## Quilted and Showpiece Christmas Table Runners

### 11. Quilted star-block runner

Cut twelve 15 cm squares in red, green and cream, sew them into four star blocks, then join the blocks in a row with sashing strips. Layer over batting and backing, quilt along the seams, and bind. It is the most work here, and the most impressive. Cost: $20 to $22. Time: 5 hours. Difficulty: hard.

### 12. Quilted log-cabin runner

Cut strips 5 cm wide in alternating red and cream, and sew them around a small gold centre square, building each block outwards like a log cabin. Join four blocks in a row, add batting and backing, quilt in the ditch and bind. Cost: $18 to $22. Time: 5 hours. Difficulty: hard.

### 13. Sequin-and-lace runner

Cut black or deep-green velvet 35 cm wide, then lay gold sequin lace 20 cm wide down the centre and stitch it along both edges. The lace catches candlelight, so keep the rest of the table simple. Cost: $18 to $21. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 14. Applique Christmas tree runner

Cut a cream linen base 38 cm wide, then cut three triangles in green felt and applique them in a row as Christmas trees, stitching around each with a blanket stitch. Add a gold star and trunk to each tree, and finish with red binding. Cost: $16 to $19. Time: 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 15. Layered burlap-and-velvet runner with tassels

Cut a burlap base 40 cm wide and a velvet strip 25 cm wide, both to your table length plus 60 cm. Centre the velvet on the burlap and stitch down the middle only, so the edges move freely. Tie a tassel to each corner from red and gold thread. Together the two textures look far more expensive than either alone. Cost: $14 to $18. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. ## Choosing the Right Fabric for a Christmas Table Runner

The fabric decides the look, the cost and how the runner survives dinner. Burlap is cheapest and most forgiving: it frays nicely and suits a rustic table. Velvet looks richest but shows every crumb and crushes under a hot plate, so use placemats. Linen drapes beautifully and washes well, but creases, so iron it before guests arrive. Cotton is the safe all-rounder: cheap, washable and easy to sew. Faux fur is dramatic but sheds and cannot be ironed. Kraft paper is the only disposable option, perfect for a big dinner where spills are certain.

| Fabric | Look | Cost per yard | Sewing ease | Washable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Burlap | Rustic, textured | $3 to $5 | Easy | Hand wash |
| Velvet | Rich, formal | $8 to $12 | Moderate | Dry clean |
| Linen | Natural, elegant | $7 to $10 | Easy | Machine wash |
| Cotton | Versatile, crisp | $4 to $7 | Easy | Machine wash |
| Faux fur | Snowy, dramatic | $10 to $14 | Moderate | Spot clean |
| Kraft paper | Casual, crafty | $2 to $3 | No sewing | Disposable |

## How Much Fabric: Sizing and Yardage by Table

A runner should be about one third of the table width, hanging 15 to 30 cm past each end. Shorter looks like a placemat; longer drags. Measure the table length, add 30 to 60 cm for the overhang, then add 5 cm for hems. The table below gives the finished runner size and fabric to buy, assuming standard 110 cm wide fabric.

| Table | Seats | Table length | Finished runner | Fabric to buy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coffee table | 2 to 4 | 90 to 120 cm | 30 by 150 cm | 1.5 yards |
| 4-seat dining | 4 | 120 to 140 cm | 35 by 180 cm | 2 yards |
| 6-seat dining | 6 | 150 to 180 cm | 38 by 220 cm | 2.5 yards |
| 8-seat dining | 8 | 200 to 240 cm | 40 by 280 cm | 3 yards |
| Mantel or buffet | — | 120 to 180 cm | 30 by 180 cm | 2 yards |

For a round table, let the runner hang evenly at both ends and centre it across the middle; it works best on tables 120 cm across or wider. For a sideboard, measure it the same way and use the mantel row.

## Finishing the Ends of a Runner

The ends are what guests see first, so finish them on purpose. Fringe suits burlap and linen: pull out the weft threads for 3 cm at each end, then knot the loose threads in pairs so the fringe does not unravel. Tassels suit velvet and layered runners: wrap thread around a small card 30 times, tie one end, cut the other, and stitch the tassel to each corner. Pointed ends suit sewn runners: fold each end into a triangle, stitch along the fold, and turn it out for a sharp V. Binding suits quilted runners: wrap bias binding around the raw edge and stitch it down.

## Washing and Storing Your Runner

Cotton and linen runners go in the machine on a cold, gentle cycle and air-dry flat; never tumble-dry linen, because it shrinks. Velvet goes to the dry cleaner, and faux fur gets spot-cleaned only. Burlap can be hand-washed in cold water, but it sheds fibres, so wash it alone. Paper runners are single-use. Before storing, make sure the runner is fully dry, or it will smell musty by next December. Roll it around a cardboard tube instead of folding it: folds become creases, and velvet creases never come out. Store it in a cotton bag, away from damp and sun, labelled with the table it fits; see the [Christmas living room decor](/blog/christmas-living-room-decor) guide for storage ideas.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Cutting the runner too short is the biggest mistake: measure the table, add 30 to 60 cm for the overhang, and only then cut. Skipping the pre-wash is the second: cotton shrinks on its first wash, and a runner that fitted in November will be too short by January. Ironing velvet directly is the third: the pile crushes and leaves a shiny mark, so iron through a cloth. Using a real candle on a fabric runner is the fourth: fabric burns, so set candles on a tray. Mixing three or more patterns in one runner is the fifth: pick one pattern and one solid. Forgetting the lining is the sixth: an unlined runner slides on a polished table, while cotton lining grips.

## FAQ

### How long should a Christmas table runner be?

Add 30 to 60 cm to the table length, so it hangs 15 to 30 cm past each end. A 150 cm table takes a 180 to 210 cm runner, and an 8-seat table takes about 280 cm.

### How wide should a Christmas table runner be?

About one third of the table width, usually 30 to 45 cm. A narrow table takes 30 cm, a 6-seat table 38 cm, and an 8-seat table 40 cm.

### How much fabric do I need for a Christmas table runner?

A 4-seat table needs 2 yards, a 6-seat table 2.5 yards, and an 8-seat table 3 yards, assuming 110 cm wide fabric.

### Can I make a Christmas table runner without sewing?

Yes: five builds here need no sewing, including the five-minute drop-cloth runner, the burlap runner with iron-on hem tape, and the kraft paper runner.

### What is the best fabric for a Christmas table runner?

Cotton is the best all-rounder: cheap, washable and easy to sew. Burlap suits a rustic table, velvet a formal one, linen a natural look.

### How do I keep a table runner from sliding?

Line the back with cotton, which grips a polished table, or add non-slip shelf liner under each end.
