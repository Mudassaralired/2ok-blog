## Why Dried Flowers Beat Fresh

Fresh flowers are beautiful for a week, then they wilt and need replacing. Dried flowers freeze a flower at its best and hold it there — no water, no vase changes, no weekly £10 bouquet. That is why they have become a staple of boho, farmhouse and cottage-style homes.

They are forgiving to make: if a stem snaps, trim it shorter and use it in a smaller piece. And dried flowers are light — a full pampas arrangement weighs under 500 grams — so they hang on a single adhesive hook and never drip water onto paint or furniture.

## Best Flowers to Dry

Not every flower dries well. The winners keep their shape and colour; the losers shrink, brown or shatter at a touch. Start with the reliable five below, and save the tricky ones for small accents.

| Flower | Colour after drying | Fragility | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pampas grass | Creamy beige, stays soft | Low | Statement walls, tall vases |
| Lavender | Purple holds well | Low | Bundles, hoops, small posies |
| Eucalyptus | Sage green, fades slowly | Low | Wreaths, garlands, bundles |
| Strawflower | Bright pink, yellow, orange | Medium | Hoops, shadow boxes |
| Lunaria (honesty) | Silvery-white discs | Medium | Shadow boxes, branch mounts |
| Roses | Deepens, then browns | Medium | Small bundles |
| Hydrangea | Fades to dusty tones | High — shatters | Shadow boxes only |

Cut flowers at their peak, just before they fully open — tight buds never open once dried, and fully open blooms drop petals. Cut in the morning, after the dew has dried, and strip the lower leaves so nothing rots. Avoid lilies, tulips and daffodils — their thick petals turn brown — and skip wilting blooms.

## How to Dry Flowers at Home

**Air-drying** is free and suits lavender, eucalyptus, pampas, roses and most grasses. Tie stems into small bundles of five to eight and hang them upside down in a dark, dry, well-ventilated room — a spare bedroom or an airing cupboard. Darkness matters: sunlight bleaches colour while flowers dry. Leave them for 2 to 3 weeks, until the stems snap rather than bend.

**Silica gel** is fast and colour-saving, and suits strawflowers, roses and hydrangeas. Pour a 3 cm layer into an airtight tub, lay the flower heads face-up on top, then spoon more gel over them until buried. Seal the tub for 2 to 7 days, then lift the flowers out and brush off the gel. Cost: about £8 a bag.

**Pressing** flattens flowers completely, so it suits small, flat blooms — daisies, violas, ferns and single petals — used as accents inside a shadow box or on a hoop. Lay the flower between two sheets of baking paper inside a heavy book, add another book on top, and leave it for 2 to 4 weeks. ## 16 DIY Dried Flower Wall Decor Ideas

### 1. Classic hanging bundle

Gather five to seven stems of lavender, eucalyptus and one tall grass, trim them even, tie with twine 5 cm below the heads, and hang upside down from a nail. Cost: about £5. Time: 10 minutes.

### 2. Dried flower embroidery hoop

Stretch linen inside a 30 cm embroidery hoop, then glue small dried stems — strawflowers, lavender and a fern frond — across the lower half, leaving the top empty. Cost: about £8. Time: 45 minutes.

### 3. Pampas statement wall

Fix five to seven tall pampas stems into a wide vase or wall bracket, fanning the plumes across a 90 cm stretch of wall, with dried palm leaves behind for shape. Cost: £20 to £25. Time: 30 minutes.

### 4. Dried flower wall grid

Fix a 40 cm square of wire mesh or a wooden trellis panel to the wall with two hooks, then tuck small bundles and single stems through the grid. Cost: about £15. Time: one hour.

### 5. Mini posy trio

Three tiny posies — one lavender stem, one strawflower and one eucalyptus sprig each — tied with twine and hung from three nails 15 cm apart. Cost: about £6. Time: 20 minutes.

### 6. Dried flower drying rack

Mount a small wooden ladder or a branch across two hooks, and hang six to eight small bundles from it with twine at staggered heights. Cost: about £12. Time: one hour.

### 7. Boho macrame and dried flower hanging

Knot a simple macrame panel — or buy a plain one for about £10 — and weave dried stems through the lower knots. Lavender and pampas suit the loose, textured look best. Cost: £12 to £18. Time: 30 minutes with a bought panel, two hours if you knot it. It pairs well with a [basket wall decor](/blog/basket-wall-decor/) display on the same wall.

### 8. Dried flower shadow box

Arrange lunaria discs, strawflowers and a fern frond inside a deep box frame (5 cm deep, 30 cm square), gluing each stem flat, then close the glass front. Cost: about £18. Time: one hour. The only safe home for hydrangeas, which shatter if left exposed.

### 9. Branch and driftwood mount

Hang a sturdy 60 cm branch horizontally from two nails, then tie five or six small bundles along it with twine, alternating lavender, eucalyptus and a grass plume. Cost: about £8, or free if you find the branch. Time: 45 minutes.

### 10. Dried flower hoop wreath

Wire small bundles around a 40 cm metal or grapevine ring until it is covered, mixing pampas, lavender, strawflower and eucalyptus for texture and colour. Cost: about £15. Time: two hours.

### 11. Pampas cloud ceiling piece

Wire three or four pampas stems to a small ceiling hook or wall bracket, fanned into a soft cloud above a reading chair or a bed. Cost: about £20. Time: one hour.

### 12. Dried flower garland

Wire eucalyptus and lavender along a 2-metre length of twine, draping it across a mantel, doorway or headboard, with strawflowers every 30 cm for colour. Cost: about £15. Time: two hours.

### 13. Framed dried botanical trio

Put a single stem — one lavender, one lunaria, one strawflower — in each of three small frames, and hang them in a row with 10 cm gaps. Cost: about £15. Time: one hour.

### 14. Dried flower wall vase

Fix a wall-mounted test-tube vase or small ceramic pocket vase with one screw or a strong adhesive strip, and fill it with a single pampas plume or three lavender stems. Cost: about £10. Time: 15 minutes.

### 15. Rustic ladder display

Lean a small wooden ladder against the wall and hang bundles from each rung with twine, longest at the bottom and shortest at the top. Cost: about £15 with a bought ladder, free with a spare one. Time: 30 minutes.

### 16. Dried flower monogram or initial

Bend florist wire into a letter — your initial, or a child's — and wire small dried stems along it until it is covered. Cost: about £10. Time: two hours. It makes a personal gift and a lovely nursery piece. For more nursery ideas, see our [DIY wall planter ideas](/blog/diy-wall-planter-ideas/) guide, which uses the same hanging fixings.

## How to Hang Dried Flower Art

Dried flowers are light, which makes them ideal for damage-free hanging. Weigh the finished piece on kitchen scales first — weight decides the fixing, not size.

- **Under 500 grams** (bundles, posies, hoops, wall vases): one adhesive hook or a single nail.
- **500 grams to 1 kg** (wreaths, shadow boxes, branch mounts): two adhesive strips or hooks, 15 cm apart, so the piece cannot twist.
- **1 to 2 kg** (pampas walls, ladder displays, large grids): two picture hooks into wall plugs, or two adhesive strips rated for 2 kg each.
- **Over 2 kg**: two screws into plugs or a stud. Dried pieces rarely reach this weight.

Hang hoops and bundles from the twine loop, never from a single stem — stems snap under load. On fresh paint, wait four weeks before sticking anything down. When moving out, pull strips straight down, never outwards, so the paint stays put.

## Styling Dried Florals by Room and Season

In a living room, go big: a pampas statement wall or a full hoop wreath over the sofa, in cream, beige and sage. In a bedroom, keep it soft — a shadow box or a mini posy trio in lavender and blush, hung where you see it from the bed but never directly above your head, in case a stem drops. In a hallway, use narrow pieces such as the posy trio, because a wide piece gets brushed in passing.

Seasonal swaps keep the same pieces fresh: spring means pale strawflowers and lunaria; summer adds bright strawflower and dried grasses; autumn brings warm pampas, wheat and dried orange slices; winter suits eucalyptus and deep-red strawflowers. ## How to Make Dried Flowers Last

Dried flowers fade for three reasons: sunlight, damp and dust. Control all three and a piece lasts 1 to 3 years; ignore them and it fades in months. Keep every piece out of direct sun — a north-facing wall is ideal, a south-facing window wall the worst. Keep pieces away from radiators, kettles and showers, because heat and steam make stems brittle or limp. Dust monthly with a soft paintbrush or a hairdryer on its coolest, lowest setting, held 30 cm away.

Seal the finished piece with a light mist of unscented hairspray from 30 cm away — one coat, never a soaking spray, which darkens petals. If a stem sheds, trim it back rather than pulling it out, which disturbs the whole arrangement. When a piece fades past saving, compost the flowers and reuse the hardware.

## FAQ

### How long do dried flowers last on a wall?

One to three years if kept out of direct sun and damp. Pampas, lavender and eucalyptus last longest; hydrangeas and roses fade fastest, within a year.

### What are the best flowers to dry for wall decor?

Pampas grass, lavender, eucalyptus, strawflower and lunaria. They hold their colour and shape, and they are sturdy enough to wire, glue or hang without shattering.

### How do you dry flowers so they keep their colour?

Dry them in the dark — sunlight bleaches colour while flowers dry. Air-dry in a dark, ventilated room for 2 to 3 weeks, or bury flower heads in silica gel for 2 to 7 days.

### Can I hang dried flower wall decor without drilling?

Yes. Almost every piece here weighs under 2 kg, so adhesive hooks and strips hold it. Use two fixings 15 cm apart for anything over 500 grams, and pull strips straight down when you remove them.

### Why are my dried flowers shedding and falling apart?

Usually sun, damp or handling. Pampas sheds naturally — a light hairspray mist helps. If stems crumble, they were dried in sunlight or stored somewhere damp.
