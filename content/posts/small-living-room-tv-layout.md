## The 3 numbers that rule every layout

Before the recipes, the physics. Every layout below obeys these three:

1. **Viewing distance:** for a 4K TV, sit 1.2–1.6× the screen diagonal away. A 55" TV (140 cm diagonal) wants 170–225 cm. Closer than 1.2× and you're turning your head; farther than 1.6× and you're squinting. (This comes from THX/SMPTE viewing-angle standards — it's not opinion.)
2. **Screen height:** center of the screen ~100–110 cm from the floor — seated eye level on a standard sofa. Every 10 cm above that is a little more neck strain per movie night.
3. **Walkways:** 75–90 cm of clear path around furniture. Below 75 cm you're sidling; the room feels cramped no matter how nice it looks.

## Recipe 1: The Straight Shot

**Room:** roughly square, 3×3 m to 3.5×4 m. **TV:** 43–55".

The classic: sofa on one wall, TV on the opposite wall, everything facing forward. Boring? Maybe. But it's boring because it works — the sightlines are perfect and every seat is a good seat.

**The numbers:**
- Sofa (180–200 cm wide) centered on its wall
- TV wall-mounted or on a 120–150 cm console opposite
- Viewing distance: 200–230 cm for a 55" (measure sofa front edge to screen)
- Coffee table 40–45 cm deep, 35–40 cm gap to the sofa
- Rug 160×230 cm minimum, front legs of sofa on it

**Furniture list:** sofa, TV console or mount, coffee table, rug, one floor lamp beside the sofa (never behind the TV — backlight kills contrast).

**Mistake to avoid:** pushing the sofa against the wall to "save space" and ending up 3 meters from a 43" TV. If the distance exceeds 1.6× your diagonal, move the sofa forward — floating it 20 cm off the wall feels wrong for a day, then invisible forever. Our [small living room guide](/blog/small-living-room-ideas/) covers the floating trick in detail.

## Recipe 2: The Corner TV

**Room:** awkward — door on one wall, window on another, no clean opposite walls. **TV:** 43–50".

Put the TV in the corner on a full-motion swivel mount. Angled out for movie night, pushed flat when not in use. The corner everyone told you "wastes space" becomes the thing that saves the layout.

**The numbers:**
- Corner mount, TV angled 20–30° toward the main seat
- Primary seat (armchair or loveseat end) 180–210 cm from screen
- Keep 75 cm clear in front of the corner — that's your walkway swinging past
- Screen center still ~105 cm high; corner mounts often end up too high, so measure

**Furniture list:** loveseat or sofa on the longest wall, one angled armchair, small round side table (not a big coffee table — corners need the floor space).

**Mistake to avoid:** the fixed corner shelf. A TV jammed into a corner on a static stand, angled permanently, with cables dangling — that's the "dorm room" look. The full-motion mount ($30–$50) is non-negotiable here; it's what makes the corner intentional instead of desperate.

## Recipe 3: TV + Fireplace

**Room:** has a fireplace hogging the best wall. **TV:** 50–55".

The internet is at war over this. Here's the honest version: TV above the fireplace works in a small room IF the mantel is low (under 120 cm) and you use a tilt mount. If the mantel is high, the TV ends up at 150 cm+ and you're craning for two hours — that's a genuine ergonomic failure, not snobbery.

**The numbers:**
- Screen center max 120–125 cm from floor (tilt mount angled down 5–10°)
- Seating 220–250 cm back — you need the extra distance to compensate for the height
- Mantel depth matters: 25 cm+ deep mantel deflects heat; check the wall temperature after the fireplace runs an hour — if you can't hold your hand there comfortably, the TV can't live there either

**Alternative if the mantel is too high:** put the TV on the adjacent wall (Recipe 1 rotated 90°) and let the fireplace be its own moment. Two focal points is fine — the "one focal point" rule is for formal rooms, not small apartments.

**Mistake to avoid:** mounting above a WORKING wood fireplace without checking heat. Electronics and heat are enemies; a fried $600 TV erases every dollar you saved on the layout.

## Recipe 4: The Narrow Room

**Room:** long and thin — 2.5×4.5 m or similar. The classic apartment rectangle. **TV:** 43–50".

TV on one SHORT wall, sofa facing it from the other end. This feels wrong ("so far!") but the math works: 4 meters of room gives you ~280 cm viewing distance after furniture depth — actually ideal for a 50–55".

**The numbers:**
- TV on short wall, centered
- Sofa on opposite short wall — yes, against the wall here; the room is too narrow to float it
- Walkway runs ALONG one long wall, never between sofa and TV — 75 cm minimum, rug-free (rugs in walkways bunch and trip)
- Narrow console (30–35 cm deep) under the TV; standard 45 cm consoles eat the walkway

**Furniture list:** sofa, narrow TV console, slim rug (120×180 cm) under the coffee zone only, wall-mounted shelves instead of a bookcase.

**Mistake to avoid:** the "bowling alley" — lining all furniture along both long walls so the middle is an empty corridor. Pull ONE piece (an armchair, a plant cluster) slightly into the room's middle third to break the tunnel. It feels counterintuitive in a narrow room; it works anyway.

## Renters: the no-drill versions

Every recipe above assumes you can mount a TV. If you can't drill:

- **Straight Shot:** low TV console (40–50 cm tall) instead of a mount — puts the screen center right at ~105 cm naturally. This is actually the easiest recipe to do drill-free.
- **Corner TV:** a corner TV stand ($40–$70) instead of the swivel mount. Less elegant, fully reversible.
- **TV + Fireplace:** lean a large mirror on the mantel INSTEAD of mounting the TV above it; put the TV on the adjacent wall per the alternative. Zero holes, better ergonomics.
- **Cables:** adhesive raceways painted wall-color ($8), or a fabric cord cover. The difference between "renter setup" and "designed setup" is 100% cable management.

## The takeaway

Small-room TV layout is arithmetic, not talent. Measure your viewing distance against your screen size, get the screen center to ~105 cm, keep 75 cm walkways, and pick the recipe matching your room shape. Do that and the room works — regardless of budget, style, or how awkward the floor plan is.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where should I put my TV in a small living room?**
On the longest clear wall opposite your main seat (Recipe 1). If no clean opposite wall exists, use a corner with a swivel mount (Recipe 2). Measure first: viewing distance should be 1.2–1.6× your screen diagonal.

**How far should I sit from a 55-inch TV in a small room?**
170–225 cm (1.2–1.6× the 140 cm diagonal) for 4K. In a small room, err toward the closer end — 180–200 cm feels immersive without head-turning.

**How high should I mount my TV?**
Screen center ~100–110 cm from the floor — seated eye level. Above a fireplace, max 120–125 cm with a tilt mount; higher than that causes real neck strain over a movie.

**Is it OK to put the TV in the corner?**
Yes — with a full-motion swivel mount ($30–$50). A static corner placement looks improvised; the articulating mount makes it intentional and solves glare by angling the screen.

**Can I put my TV opposite a window?**
Avoid it if you can — direct reflections kill contrast. If the layout forces it, use blackout or layered blinds and consider bias lighting (a $15 LED strip behind the TV) which reduces perceived glare and eye strain.
