A Christmas tree skirt hides the stand, catches fallen needles and gives presents a soft place to land — and a handmade one costs a fraction of a shop-bought skirt while looking far more personal. These 15 ideas cover every skill level, from a no-sew fleece circle to a patchwork quilt skirt worth keeping for decades.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Most tree skirts cost £4 to £18 and take one to three hours.
> - A skirt should measure about half the width of the tree's lowest branches.
> - No-sew fleece and faux fur are the fastest routes for a first skirt.
> - Keep every skirt at least 1 metre from candles and open fires, and use LED lights only.
> - In a breathable cotton bag, a handmade skirt lasts ten Christmases or more.

## No-Sew Christmas Tree Skirts

No-sew skirts are the quickest route: cut, trim, lay it down. Fleece and faux fur do not fray, so raw edges look deliberate.

### 1. Fluffy faux-fur circle skirt

A circle of white or cream faux fur, edged with wide satin ribbon glued around the rim, looks like fresh snow under the tree. Cut one slit from edge to centre so it slips around the trunk. Cost: £12 to £18. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 2. No-sew fleece circle skirt

Two layers of fleece, cut as one big circle with a small centre hole for the trunk, need no hemming — fleece does not fray. Choose deep red or cream, and add a pom-pom trim with fabric glue. Cost: £8 to £12. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 3. Ruffled drop-cloth skirt

Painter's drop cloth, cheap cotton canvas from hardware shops, makes an elegant skirt once washed and given a ruffled edge. Gather a long strip with a running stitch or hot glue, and glue it around the rim. Cost: £6 to £9. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 4. Felt scalloped-edge skirt

Cut a circle from two metres of thick wool-blend felt, then cut a scalloped edge using a plate as a template, and glue a felt star near the front edge. Cost: £7 to £10. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Use 3 mm felt or thicker; thin craft felt curls within a week.

### 5. Pom-pom trim fleece skirt

A plain fleece circle becomes a feature when chunky yarn pom-poms are glued around its edge — white on red, cream on green, or a mix. Make them with a cardboard doughnut template. Cost: £6 to £9. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

## Sewn Christmas Tree Skirts

Sewn skirts take longer but last the longest. A basic circle skirt is one of the easiest sewing projects there is.

### 6. Patchwork quilt tree skirt

Sew leftover quilting cotton squares — 15 cm squares in reds, greens and creams — into a large square, cut the circle from it, back it with plain cotton and bind the edge. Cost: £10 to £15. Time: 4 to 5 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 7. Velvet tree skirt with piping

Deep green or burgundy velvet with a contrasting gold piping cord in the outer seam looks far more expensive than it is. Velvet slips under the presser foot, so pin generously and sew slowly. Cost: £14 to £20. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. 

### 8. Burlap and lace farmhouse skirt

Natural burlap cut into a circle, edged with a wide band of cream cotton lace, is the classic farmhouse skirt. Hem the burlap first or it sheds fibres everywhere, then topstitch the lace over the hem. Cost: £8 to £12. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 9. Plaid flannel skirt with ties

Red and black buffalo plaid flannel, cut into a circle and finished with fabric ties at the back slit, is the cosiest skirt here. Flannel frays, so finish the edge with bias binding or a double-fold hem. Cost: £9 to £13. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. Pre-wash the flannel — it shrinks noticeably.

## Knitted, Crochet and Upcycled Tree Skirts

These take the longest, but they are the ones people keep. A knitted skirt is a winter-evenings project; upcycled skirts cost almost nothing.

### 10. Chunky knitted tree skirt

Knit a flat circle in chunky yarn on large needles, working increases every few rounds, or knit six wedge panels and sew them into a circle. Cream yarn with a cable pattern looks best. Cost: £12 to £18. Time: 8 to 10 hours over several evenings. Difficulty: moderate.

### 11. Crochet granny-square tree skirt

Crochet granny squares in cream, red and green, join them into a large square, then crochet a border round the outside, or leave it square for a modern tree. Use a 4.5 mm hook and double-knit yarn. Cost: £8 to £12. Time: 10 to 12 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 12. Old sweater tree skirt

Felt three or four old wool jumpers in a hot wash, cut them into panels and sew them into a circle, keeping the ribbed hems as the outer edge. Mixed cable knits in cream and grey look Scandi. Cost: £2 to £4. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Only real wool felts, so check the label first.

### 13. Quilt or blanket tree skirt

An old quilt, a thin blanket or a large tablecloth, cut into a circle with a slit and a bound edge, becomes a skirt in under an hour — and a quilted one already has padding that makes presents sit nicely. Cost: £3 to £6. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Bind the raw edge with bias tape.

## Tree Collars: The Alternative to a Skirt

A tree collar is a rigid ring — wood, metal or woven basket — that stands around the tree base instead of lying flat. Collars suit homes where a skirt would be walked on, chewed or dragged about.

### 14. Wooden slat tree collar

Four hinged panels of thin pine slats, joined with small hinges and finished with clear matt varnish, fold flat for storage and stand around the tree base. Each panel is about 30 cm tall and 50 cm wide. Cost: £10 to £16. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Sand every edge smooth.

### 15. Galvanised metal or woven basket collar

A galvanised metal ring, or a large seagrass basket with the bottom cut out, drops over the stand in seconds. Metal suits modern rooms; the basket suits farmhouse trees. Cost: £12 to £25. Time: 10 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Measure the stand's widest point before buying — most stands are 25 to 30 cm tall. ## What Size Tree Skirt Do I Need?

The rule is simple: the skirt's diameter should be about half the width of the tree's lowest branches. Too small and the stand shows; too big and it gets walked on. Measure the widest spread of the lowest branches, halve it, and cut to that diameter.

| Tree height | Lowest branch spread | Skirt diameter | Fabric needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 ft (150 cm) | 90–100 cm | 45–50 cm | 1 m |
| 6 ft (180 cm) | 110–120 cm | 55–60 cm | 1.5 m |
| 7 ft (210 cm) | 130–140 cm | 65–70 cm | 1.5 m |
| 9 ft (275 cm) | 160–180 cm | 80–90 cm | 2 m |

For a slim tree, drop one size down; for a very full tree, go one size up. Cut the centre hole small, about 10 cm across, and widen it only if the trunk needs it. On bare boards, stick non-slip rug underlay underneath so the skirt does not slide.

## How to Make a No-Sew Fleece Tree Skirt: Step by Step

Start here: one afternoon, no sewing machine, and a skirt that looks bought. You need 1.5 m of fleece, a pin, string, tailor's chalk, sharp fabric scissors, fabric glue and ribbon for ties. For the finished look, see the [Christmas tree decorating ideas](/blog/christmas-tree-decorating-ideas) guide.

Fold the fleece in half, then in half again, so you have four layers with one folded corner at the centre. Push a pin through all four layers at that corner. Tie one end of the string to the pin and the other to the chalk, with the string at half your skirt diameter — 60 cm for a 120 cm skirt.

Hold the pin firmly and sweep the chalk around in a smooth arc, keeping the string taut. Cut through all four layers with long, steady strokes — short snips leave a jagged edge. Open it out for a full circle. For the centre hole, tie the string to 5 cm and sweep a small circle around the pin. Cut it out, then cut one straight slit from the outer edge to the centre hole.

Glue a pom-pom trim or ribbon binding around the rim, working in 30 cm sections so the glue does not set first. Glue a 30 cm ribbon to each side of the slit as ties, then leave it flat for an hour to cure. Slide it under the tree and tie the ribbons at the back. ## Fire and Heat Safety: Skirts, Candles and Lights

A tree skirt is fabric on the floor under a tree full of lights, so treat fire safety as part of the making. Fleece, faux fur, burlap and cotton are all flammable, and a skirt sits where a dropped candle would land. Never place lit candles on or beside the skirt, and keep candlesticks at least 1 metre from the tree.

Use LED fairy lights only, never old incandescent strings, which run hot enough to scorch fabric. Check every string for frayed wires or cracked bulbs before it goes on the tree, and switch them off when you leave the house or go to bed. Keep the tree — skirt included — at least 1 metre from fireplaces, radiators and portable heaters. Keep a real tree's stand topped up with water — it dries out through December.

## Christmas Tree Skirts with Kids and Pets

Children and pets treat a tree skirt as a blanket, a bed and a toy, so choose accordingly. For toddlers, fleece or felt is safest: no small parts, nothing to pull off. Skip pom-pom trims, buttons and beads until children are old enough not to chew them — a loose pom-pom is a choking risk. Sew trims on rather than gluing them, and keep the ties at the back of the trunk.

Cats will knead a soft skirt, sleep on it and drag it across the room; a heavy quilted or velvet skirt stays put better than a light fleece one. Dogs may chew the edge, so a washable cotton or fleece skirt is the practical choice — it can go in the machine on a cool wash in January. If a pet keeps pulling the skirt out from under the tree, switch to a rigid collar instead: a wooden or metal collar cannot be dragged away. ## Takedown and Storage: Keeping Your Skirt for Next Christmas

Take the skirt down with the tree in early January, before dust and damp settle in. Shake it outside first — a season under a real tree leaves it full of needles — then wash it if the fabric allows: fleece, cotton and flannel machine-wash cool, while faux fur, velvet and knitted skirts need hand-washing. Dry everything fully before storing; a damp skirt smells musty by next December.

Fold the skirt loosely and store it in a breathable cotton bag or pillowcase, not a sealed plastic box, which traps moisture. Fold knitted skirts, never hang them, or they stretch out of shape. Add a lavender sachet to keep moths from wool and velvet, label the bag with the skirt's size, and store it flat on top of the other Christmas boxes.

## FAQ

### What size Christmas tree skirt do I need for a 6 ft tree?

A 6 ft tree with a 110 to 120 cm branch spread needs a skirt about 55 to 60 cm across — roughly half the spread. Slim trees take one size down.

### Can I make a Christmas tree skirt without sewing?

Yes — fleece and faux fur are best because they do not fray. Cut a circle with the string-compass method, cut a slit to the centre, and glue on a trim. See the tutorial above.

### Are tree skirts safe with real candles nearby?

Keep lit candles at least 1 metre from the tree and never place them on the skirt — fleece, faux fur, burlap and cotton are all flammable. Use LED lights and switch them off when you leave.

### How do I wash a handmade Christmas tree skirt?

Fleece, cotton and flannel machine-wash cool; faux fur, velvet and knitted skirts need hand-washing. Dry fully, then store in a breathable cotton bag.

### Is a tree collar better than a tree skirt?

A collar is better with pets or toddlers who drag a skirt around, or for a structured, modern look. A skirt suits a soft, traditional finish. Both hide the stand, so choose by who lives in the house.
