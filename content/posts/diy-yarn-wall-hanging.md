A ball of yarn, a wooden dowel and an afternoon warms up a blank wall. Yarn wall hangings are soft, forgiving and cheap, and unlike paint they come down without a trace. These 15 DIY yarn wall hanging ideas need no loom, no knitting, no crochet — just wrapping, tying, braiding and cutting.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every project here is no-loom: tassels, pom-poms, fringe, braiding and dip-dye only.
> - Most projects cost £3 to £15 and use one to three balls of yarn.
> - Chunky yarn works up fastest; cotton yarn gives the cleanest fringe.
> - Measure your yarn first — a 60 cm hanging can swallow 150 metres.
> - Weigh the finished piece, then use hardware rated for twice that weight.

## Tassels and Fringe: The Easiest DIY Yarn Wall Hangings

### 1. Classic tassel fringe hanging on a wooden dowel

Wrap yarn around a 15 cm cardboard piece, tie it off and cut the loop. Make twelve to sixteen tassels in two or three colours and tie them along a 60 cm dowel. Cost: £5 to £8. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Needs about 90 metres — one 100 g ball.

### 2. Dip-dye ombre fringe hanging

Make a plain cream fringe hanging first, then dip the bottom third of the tassels in diluted fabric dye for ten minutes for a soft ombre fade. Cost: £6 to £10. Time: 2 to 3 hours, plus drying. Difficulty: easy.

![Hands wrapping cream and terracotta yarn around cardboard to make tassels, with yarn balls, scissors and a tape measure on a wooden table](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-yarn-wall-hanging/in-article-1)

### 3. Beaded tassel hanging

Thread a large wooden bead onto each tassel before tying it to the dowel, in a gentle V shape. Cost: £7 to £11. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Twenty 20 mm beads cost about £3. The [DIY terracotta pot wall decor](/blog/diy-terracotta-pot-wall-decor) guide uses the same palette.

### 4. Giant single statement tassel

Scale one tassel up to 60 cm long and 15 cm wide, wrapped from a full ball of chunky yarn around a book, then hang it from a ring. Cost: £4 to £7. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. At 200 to 300 g, one adhesive hook holds it.

### 5. Layered tassel garland

Tie twenty 8 cm tassels onto yarn at 10 cm intervals, then drape the garland across a mirror or bedhead. Cost: £3 to £6. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Alternate colours for a bunting effect, or use one colour for a calm look. The [boho wall decor ideas](/blog/boho-wall-decor-ideas) guide shows garlands layered with soft textures.

## Pom-Poms, Braids and Rainbows: Texture-Rich Yarn Wall Art

### 6. Pom-pom garland wall hanging

Wind yarn around a fork or pom-pom maker, tie it in the middle, cut the loops and fluff the ball into shape. Make fifteen pom-poms, thread them onto yarn and hang the garland in two swags. Cost: £4 to £8. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Fifteen 7 cm pom-poms need about 180 metres.

### 7. Pom-pom embroidery hoop wreath

Wrap a 30 cm wooden embroidery hoop in yarn until no wood shows, then tie eight pom-poms around the bottom. Cost: £5 to £9. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Hang it from a ribbon loop; at under 400 g, one adhesive hook is enough.

### 8. Chunky three-strand braided hanging

Cut nine lengths, each three times the finished length, knot them at the top, then braid in three groups of three and knot the ends. Cost: £6 to £10. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. A 90 cm braid needs about 270 metres, so buy three balls.

![A yarn rainbow arch, tassel wall hanging and pom-pom hoop styled on a sage green bedroom wall above a bed with cream bedding](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-yarn-wall-hanging/in-article-2)

### 9. Yarn rainbow arch

Cut rope lengths in graduating sizes, wrap each in a different colour, then bend them into arches, stitch together and trim the ends. Cost: £8 to £13. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. A 40 cm rainbow uses about 120 metres across five colours.

### 10. Braided rope and tassel combo hanging

Braid two thick yarn ropes, hang them from a dowel in a V, then fill the gap with short tassels in a contrasting colour. Cost: £8 to £12. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. The [macrame wall hanging](/blog/macrame-wall-hanging) guide pairs braided yarn with knotted cord.

## Branch, Dowel and Hoop Hangings: Bigger Statement Pieces

### 11. Foraged branch hanging with mixed yarn lengths

Find a straight fallen branch, about 80 cm long, scrub it clean and let it dry, then tie yarn strands along it — long tassels at the ends, short fringe in the middle. Cost: £3 to £6. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. The branch is free, so yarn is the only cost.

### 12. Yarn-wrapped hoop wall art

Wrap two embroidery hoops, 25 cm and 40 cm, in contrasting colours, then hang the smaller inside the larger and add three tassels. Cost: £6 to £10. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. The [DIY fabric wall art](/blog/diy-fabric-wall-art) guide shows how wrapped hoops sit beside framed fabric pieces.

### 13. Rainbow arch trio for a nursery

Make three small rainbow arches in the same colours — 30 cm, 20 cm and 15 cm — and hang them above a cot or changing table. Cost: £10 to £15. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Use soft cotton rather than wool, since nursery pieces get touched and washed often.

### 14. Full-width fringe curtain for a doorway

Tie 60 to 80 strands, each 180 cm long, along a tension rod inside a door frame, then trim the bottoms into a curve. Cost: £12 to £18. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. A doorway curtain uses 250 to 300 metres, so buy four balls of the same dye lot.

### 15. Yarn tassel mobile for a reading nook

Tie six tassels of different lengths to a small wooden ring, then hang the ring from the ceiling so it floats above a chair or a reading corner. Cost: £5 to £9. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Keep the mobile under 500 g so one adhesive ceiling hook holds it. The [DIY wall decor guide](/blog/diy-wall-decor-guide) shows how a hanging piece anchors a small corner.

## Yarn Types Compared: Which Yarn Suits Which Project

The yarn you choose decides how the piece looks and lasts. Chunky wool works up fastest and gives the fullest tassels. Cotton is smooth, cuts cleanly and takes dye beautifully. Acrylic is cheapest and comes in every colour, but it looks shiny and melts near heat.

| Yarn | Cost per 100 g | Feel | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chunky wool | £4–£6 | Soft, full | Tassels, pom-poms, braids | Sheds; hand-wash only |
| Cotton DK | £3–£5 | Smooth, clean edge | Dip-dye, rainbows, fringe | Less fluffy than wool |
| Acrylic chunky | £2–£4 | Light, slightly shiny | Budget garlands, kids' rooms | Looks cheap |

If you only buy one yarn, buy chunky cotton or a cotton-acrylic blend: cheap, clean-cutting and good for almost every project here. Avoid very fine yarn — a 4-ply tassel needs four times the wrapping and still looks thin.

## Yarn-Length Maths: How Much Yarn Each Project Needs

Running out of yarn halfway through is the most common frustration. A tassel uses roughly twice its finished length per wrap. Fringe strands need twice the finished length plus 10 cm. A 7 cm pom-pom uses about 12 metres. Braids need three times the finished length per strand.

| Project | Finished size | Yarn needed | Balls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tassel fringe hanging | 60 cm wide | 90 m | 1 |
| Pom-pom garland | 15 pom-poms | 180 m | 2 |
| Braided hanging | 90 cm long | 270 m | 3 |
| Doorway fringe curtain | 80 cm wide | 300 m | 4 |

Always buy one extra ball, all from the same dye lot — the number printed on the label. Two balls of the "same" terracotta from different dye lots can look different. If you run short, finish the outer tassels in a contrasting colour.

## Choosing Colours: Palettes That Work in Real Rooms

Colour is what makes a yarn hanging look designed rather than accidental. Start with the room, not the yarn shelf. In a neutral room, terracotta, sage and cream add warmth without fighting anything. In a white bedroom, dusty pink, mustard and cream feel soft. Use three colours maximum: a main colour for 60 percent, a supporting colour for 30 percent, one accent for the last 10 percent.

Test the palette first. Lay the three balls on the bed or sofa the piece will hang above and look from the doorway. Cream is the safest main colour for a first project — it suits every wall. Avoid neon yarn for large pieces; bright colours work in small doses.

## Sizing and Layout: Getting Yarn Wall Decor to Sit Right

Yarn is soft and light, so it reads smaller than wood or metal — go bigger than you think. A single hanging should cover roughly two-thirds of the furniture below it: above a 180 cm sofa, aim for about 120 cm of yarn, which usually means a wide dowel or two hangings. Hang the centre at about 145 cm — average eye level.

Fringe length matters as much as width. Short fringe of 15 to 20 cm looks tidy and modern; longer fringe looks boho but catches on furniture. Leave at least 15 cm between the bottom of the fringe and the top of a sofa or bedhead, or the tassels will brush the cushions.

## Hanging Hardware by Weight: What Holds What

Yarn hangings are light, but a wide curtain weighs more than it looks. Weigh the finished piece on kitchen scales first, then pick a fixing rated for twice that weight. Under 500 g — a tassel, a pom-pom hoop, a small rainbow — a large adhesive hook holds it. From 500 g to 2 kg, use one or two picture hooks. From 2 to 4 kg, use screw hooks or wall plugs, with two fixings, not one.

| Finished weight | Examples | Best fixing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 500 g | Single tassel, pom-pom hoop, small rainbow | Large adhesive hook |
| 500 g to 2 kg | Tassel fringe, braided hanging, branch piece | 1–2 picture hooks |
| 2 kg to 4 kg | Doorway curtain, wide piece | 2 screw hooks or wall plugs |

Two fixings split the load and keep the piece level — one hook lets a wide dowel tilt. On plasterboard, use toggle bolts for anything over 2 kg. Never hang yarn from a drawing pin; the pin bends and the piece falls.

## Renter-Friendly Yarn Wall Hangings: No Holes, No Damage

Renters can make and hang almost every idea here. Adhesive hooks are the default: stick them to a clean, dry painted wall, wait a full hour before loading, and remove them by pulling the tab slowly down the wall, never outwards. For heavier pieces, lean them instead — a large tassel hanging leans against a wall behind a sofa.

A tension rod is the renter's best friend for a doorway curtain: it wedges inside the frame with no screws. A picture rail works the same way — hooks slide along the rail and the piece hangs on a cord. Avoid tape and blu-tack on painted walls; both leave residue and pull paint. ## Mistakes to Avoid and Caring for Yarn Wall Decor

The biggest mistake is cutting the fringe before hanging. Yarn stretches under its own weight, so a fringe trimmed on the table hangs 2 to 3 cm longer on the wall — hang it first, let it settle, then trim straight. The second mistake is wrapping tassels too loosely; loose wraps slide apart and the tassel goes flat, so wrap firmly and tie the neck twice. The third is mixing yarn weights in one piece — chunky tassels beside fine fringe look uneven, so keep one weight per hanging.

Dust yarn pieces monthly with a dry cloth, and never vacuum them — the suction pulls fibres out. If a tassel goes frizzy, hold a steamer 15 cm away for a few seconds, then comb it straight. Keep yarn away from direct sun, which fades terracotta and mustard within a year, and from candles and radiators.

## FAQ

### How much yarn do I need for a wall hanging?

A 60 cm tassel fringe hanging needs about 90 metres — one ball. A pom-pom garland needs roughly 180 metres, and a doorway curtain needs 250 to 300 metres.

### Can I make a yarn wall hanging without a loom?

Yes. Every project here is no-loom: tassels wrap around cardboard, pom-poms wind on a fork, braids are made by hand, rainbows wrap around rope.

### What is the cheapest yarn wall hanging to make?

A layered tassel garland or a foraged branch hanging, both £3 to £6. The branch is free and the garland needs one ball of yarn.

### How do I hang a yarn wall hanging without damaging the wall?

Use large adhesive hooks for anything under 500 g. For heavier pieces, use a tension rod, lean the dowel against the wall, or hang from a picture rail.

### How do I stop yarn tassels from going frizzy?

Wrap firmly, tie the neck twice and trim with sharp scissors. If a tassel still frizzes, hold a steamer 15 cm away for a few seconds and comb it straight.
