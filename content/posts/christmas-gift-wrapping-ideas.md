## Classic Kraft and Natural Toppers

### 1. Kraft paper with dried orange and cinnamon

Wrap in matte kraft, tie once each way with jute twine, and slide one dried orange slice and a cinnamon stick under the knot. Cost: £1 to £3 per gift. Time: 10 minutes. The most-saved Christmas wrapping look on Pinterest, and it costs almost nothing.

### 2. Kraft paper with a fresh pine sprig

Wrap in heavy kraft, run two turns of jute twine around the box, and tuck a short pine sprig under the knot. Cost: £1 to £2 per gift. Time: 5 minutes. Cut the sprig short, about 8 cm, so it sits flat. The [Christmas garland ideas](/blog/christmas-garland-ideas) guide uses the same greenery across the room.

### 3. Kraft paper with a rosemary sprig and twine

Wrap in kraft, tie with garden twine, and tuck a fresh rosemary sprig under the knot. Cost: £1 to £2 per gift. Time: 5 minutes. Rosemary's woody stem does not wilt, and one plant covers a dozen gifts.

### 4. Layered kraft with a cream paper band

Wrap in kraft, then wrap a band of cream paper about a third of the box height around the middle. Tie with twine and slide one dried orange slice and a short cedar sprig under the knot. Cost: £2 to £4 per gift. Time: 15 minutes.

## Velvet, Wax Seals and Luxe Looks

### 5. Cream paper with wide emerald velvet ribbon

Wrap in heavy cream paper, run wide emerald velvet ribbon around it once each way, and tie a generous bow with long tails. Cost: £4 to £7 per gift. Time: 10 minutes. Velvet holds its shape and makes plain paper look boutique-bought.

### 6. Burgundy velvet ribbon with a wax seal

Wrap in cream paper, tie narrow burgundy velvet ribbon in a flat knot, melt wax onto the knot, and press a brass stamp for 20 seconds. Cost: £5 to £9 per gift. Time: 15 minutes. Practise on scrap paper first.

### 7. Black paper with a gold cord and wax seal

Wrap in matte black paper, tie a thin gold cord around it, and finish with one drop of burgundy wax. Cost: £4 to £8 per gift. Time: 10 minutes. A plain black box looks like a mistake until the seal catches the light. It suits the [Christmas mantel decor](/blog/christmas-mantel-decor) guide.

## Fabric Wraps and Furoshiki

### 8. Linen furoshiki wrap

Use a linen or cotton square about three times the width of your box. Place the box diagonally in the centre, knot opposite corners, then knot the remaining pair on top. Cost: £3 to £8 per gift. Time: 5 minutes. No tape, no scissors; the cloth becomes part of the gift.

![Christmas gifts wrapped in cream linen fabric furoshiki style with soft knotted tops, arranged on a wooden sideboard beside eucalyptus and a lit candle](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/christmas-gift-wrapping-ideas/in-article-2)

### 9. Rumpled linen loose wrap

Wash cream or soft grey linen and dry it on high so it wrinkles, then wrap the gift loosely, gathering the fabric on top. Tie with thin velvet ribbon and tuck one dried eucalyptus leaf under the knot. Cost: £3 to £6 per gift. Time: 5 minutes. Do not iron the linen: the softness is the point. It suits a tree styled with the [Christmas tree decorating ideas](/blog/christmas-tree-decorating-ideas) in soft neutrals.

### 10. Tea towel wrap with rosemary

Lay a cotton tea towel flat, place the gift in the centre, fold the sides in, bring the ends up, and tie with jute twine. Tuck a fresh rosemary sprig under the knot. Cost: £2 to £5 per gift. Time: 5 minutes. A tea towel wraps a cookbook or a bottle of oil, and becomes useful the next day.

## Playful and Personal Wraps

### 11. Vintage map paper with a kraft tag

Wrap in a vintage map, a matte reprint, or a large atlas page, keeping the folds clean. Finish with thin navy grosgrain ribbon and a blank kraft tag. Cost: £2 to £5 per gift. Time: 10 minutes. A map of a place that matters makes the paper part of the story.

### 12. Sheet music wrap with a black velvet ribbon

Overlap sheet music pages to cover the box, taping underneath only, and finish with thin black velvet ribbon and a blank kraft tag. Cost: £2 to £4 per gift. Time: 10 minutes. For a musician, a gift in their favourite score feels personal in a way patterned paper never can.

### 13. Potato block-printed kraft paper

Halve a potato, press a star cookie cutter through it, and trim the excess. Brush on thin water-based red paint and stamp onto kraft in a loose pattern. Let it dry fully, then wrap and finish with raffia. Cost: £2 to £4 per gift. Time: 20 minutes plus drying. Children love helping, and the handmade wobble is the charm. ## Budget and No-Waste Wraps

### 14. Brown grocery bag wrap with a stamped snowflake

Cut open a brown grocery bag and turn it wrong side out. Stamp a snowflake with a wine cork dipped in white paint, tie with red-and-white twine, and add a pine cone. Cost: £0 to £1 per gift. Time: 10 minutes. Bag paper is thicker than wrapping paper, so corners fold crisply and never tear.

### 15. Newspaper wrap with red twine and a wooden star

Wrap in black-and-white newsprint, tie with red twine, and add a small painted wooden star. Cost: £0 to £1 per gift. Time: 5 minutes. The comics page is fun for children; plain newsprint with a red ribbon looks smart for adults. Foil and glitter papers cannot be recycled.

## How to Measure and Cut Wrapping Paper

Bad measuring wastes more paper than bad wrapping. Stand the box on the paper, roll it over once each way, mark where the paper meets itself, and add about 5 cm of overlap. For the ends, the paper must extend past each end by just over half the box height. Leave too little and the end gapes; leave too much and folds turn lumpy.

Fold hospital corners like a bed sheet: side flaps in first, then top flap down and bottom flap up, tucking the point under. Hide double-sided tape inside the flaps — invisible tape is the biggest upgrade to any wrap.

## Step-by-Step: Dried-Orange Gift Topper Set

Dried orange slices top half the looks in this guide, and one baking session makes enough for the whole pile. You need 3 navel oranges, a sharp knife, a baking tray, baking paper, and a cooling rack. The set costs about £3 and makes around 15 slices.

Step 1: Heat the oven to 110°C (90°C fan). Slice the oranges about 3 mm thick and pat each slice dry. Thin slices dry flat; thick slices curl and stay damp.

Step 2: Lay the slices in a single layer on a lined tray. Bake for 2 hours, turning halfway, until dry and papery with no soft spots.

Step 3: Cool completely on a rack, then store in a lidded jar; they keep for weeks. Thread twine through a slice before knotting it on, or slide it under the knot with a cinnamon stick. One orange makes about 5 slices — 3 oranges cover 15 gifts at roughly 20p per topper.

## Step-by-Step: Fabric-Wrap Furoshiki Set

A furoshiki set is three cloth squares — about 50 cm, 70 cm and 90 cm — and it wraps every box shape without a roll of paper. The set costs about £10 to £15 if you buy the fabric, or nothing if you use scarves and tea towels you already own.

Step 1: Wash and iron the cloths smooth, or leave them rumpled. A square three times the box width wraps a standard box; twice the width wraps a book or bottle.

Step 2: Place the gift diagonally in the centre. Fold the two nearest corners over it, then bring the two far corners up and tie them in a double knot. Fluff the knots into soft ears and tuck greenery or a dried orange slice under the knot.

Step 3: For a bottle, stand it in the centre, gather all four corners above the neck, and tie them with a ribbon: the bottle becomes a drawstring bag. Reuse the same three cloths every year — furoshiki gets softer with every use.

## Wrapping Odd Shapes and Gift Cards

Odd shapes defeat most wrapping paper, so change the container. A bottle goes in a furoshiki knot or a cellophane twist tied at both ends like a cracker. A soft toy or jumper goes in a linen drawstring sack with a wood tag. A round tin or candle goes in fabric gathered on top, or a paper bag folded over twice with two holes for ribbon.

Gift cards are the trickiest small gift, because a bare card feels like an afterthought. Searches for gift card presentation ideas are up 620% on Pinterest this year. Slip the card into a small kraft envelope, seal it with wax, and tie it onto a wrapped box. Or tuck it in a fabric pouch or a jam jar of sweets. The card stays the gift, but the wrapping gives the recipient something to open. ## Mistakes to Avoid and Storing Leftovers

The biggest mistake is too much paper: an over-long sheet makes thick, lumpy end folds that spring open. Measure first. The second is shiny tape outside: it catches the light in every photograph, while double-sided tape hidden in the flaps costs the same and looks ten times better. The third is over-decorating: three toppers look cluttered, while one good topper looks chosen. Pick one paper, one ribbon, one topper — and stop there.

The fourth is damp greenery: wet leaves stain kraft paper, so pat sprigs dry and wrap the day before, not a week ahead. The fifth is forgetting the tag: a pile of matching presents is only charming if everyone can find theirs, so write the name on before you tie it on. Store leftovers well: roll paper around a tube, coil ribbon around card in a shoebox, keep dried orange slices in a sealed jar, and fold fabric wraps flat in a drawer.

## FAQ

### How much does Christmas gift wrapping cost per gift?

Most looks cost £1 to £9 per gift: grocery bag and newspaper wraps £0 to £1, kraft with greenery £1 to £3, burgundy velvet with a wax seal £5 to £9. Fifteen gifts cost about £25 to £40 if you mix three looks.

### What is the cheapest way to make gifts look expensive?

Start with kraft or cream paper and add one luxe detail: wide velvet ribbon, a wax seal, greenery, or a dried orange slice. One good detail beats busy decoration.

### How do I wrap a gift without tape?

Use furoshiki: place the gift diagonally on a square cloth, knot opposite corners, then knot the remaining pair on top. No tape or scissors needed.

### How do I make dried orange slices for gift toppers?

Slice navel oranges 3 mm thick, pat dry, and bake in a single layer at 110°C for 2 hours, turning halfway. Three oranges make about 15 slices — roughly 20p per topper.
