Blank walls make a home feel unfinished, but art is expensive and committing to holes in the wall is scary. DIY wall decor solves both: it's cheap, it's personal, and beginners can genuinely pull these off in a weekend.

## 1. The taped gallery wall

Use washi tape or painter's tape to frame prints directly on the wall — no frames, no nails. Arrange in a loose grid, step back, adjust. It looks modern and intentional, and you can redo it endlessly. Perfect for renters.

## 2. Fabric wall hanging

A beautiful piece of fabric, a wooden dowel, and some string. That's a wall hanging. Look for interesting textiles — a scarf you love, leftover linen, even a patterned bedsheet cut down. It adds softness and texture that framed prints can't.

## 3. Floating photo ledges

One narrow picture ledge (or a simple painted plank on brackets) lets you layer framed photos and small art without committing to nail positions. Swap pieces seasonally. The layering looks curated and costs very little.

## 4. Dried flower hoop art

An embroidery hoop, some dried flowers or grasses, and fishing line. Arrange the stems inside the hoop, tie them off, hang. It takes twenty minutes and looks like something from a boutique. Eucalyptus, lavender, and wheat all work beautifully.

## 5. Painted arch accent

Paint a large arch shape directly on the wall in a warm tone (terracotta, sage, clay). It creates a "zone" — behind a reading chair, over a console table — with zero furniture. All you need is painter's tape, a sample pot of paint, and a steady hand for the curve.

## 6. The plate wall

Thrifted plates in coordinating colors, hung with plate hangers or adhesive disc hangers (no drilling). Mix sizes, keep the palette tight — whites and blues, or warm earth tones. It's charming, personal, and costs almost nothing at thrift prices.

## 7. Oversized single print

Sometimes the easiest DIY is a print job: have a favorite photo or abstract design printed large at a copy shop, mount it on foam board, and lean it on a shelf or mantel. One big piece has more impact than six small ones and there's nothing to hang.

## Beginner tips that matter

- **Odd numbers** look better than even — group in 3s and 5s.
- **Eye level** for the center of art is roughly 145cm from the floor.
- **Mock it up first** with paper templates taped to the wall before any nails.
- **Cohesion beats matching** — pieces don't need to match, they need a common thread (color, frame style, or mood).

Start with one wall, one project. Done is better than perfect — and you can always rearrange next weekend.
