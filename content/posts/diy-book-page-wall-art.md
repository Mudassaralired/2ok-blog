## Folded and Sculpted Book Page Art

### 1. Layered book-page peony panel

Fold and curl aged book pages into peony-like blooms, then glue about nine onto a painted plywood backing. Cost: £6 to £12. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. A 60 × 60 cm panel weighs under 1 kg, so two adhesive strips hold it.

### 2. Folded-book wall sculpture

Take a thick damaged paperback, fold every page into a fan or heart shape using a simple measured pattern, then mount it on a small wooden shelf bracket. Cost: £4 to £8. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. The folded book weighs about 600 g, so one screw hook holds it.

![Hands folding aged book pages into paper flowers on a wooden craft table, with scissors, a glue gun and finished paper peonies](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-book-page-wall-art/in-article-1)

### 3. Rolled-page sunburst mirror

Roll about sixty book pages into tight tubes and glue them in a radiating circle around a round mirror, varying the lengths slightly so it looks handmade. Cost: £12 to £22. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. The mirror weighs about 1.5 kg, so use one screw hook into a wall plug.

### 4. Book-page rosette medallions

Fold book-page strips concertina-style, join the ends into a circle and pinch the centre flat, then hang three or five medallions in a row. Cost: £3 to £6. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Each medallion weighs under 100 g, so one adhesive dot holds it. The [DIY paper wall decor](/blog/diy-paper-wall-decor) guide shows the same concertina fold on a larger scale.

## Framed and Flat Book Page Art

### 5. Framed botanical book-page print

Choose illustrated pages from an old botany or field guide, mount one or two in a wooden frame with a cream mount, and hang them as a pair. Cost: £5 to £10. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. A framed A4 page weighs about 500 g, so one adhesive hook holds it.

### 6. Book-page typography quote art

Cut a favourite word or short line from a book page, mount it on contrasting card and frame it, letting the printed text around the cut-outs show through. Cost: £4 to £8. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. A framed piece weighs under 700 g, so adhesive strips hold it. The [DIY wooden sign wall decor](/blog/diy-wooden-sign-wall-decor) guide covers cutting large letters cleanly.

### 7. Vintage book-page collage panel

Layer torn book pages, old maps and sheet music on a canvas board, seal it, then add one bold painted shape — a heart, a bird or a tree — on top. Cost: £7 to £13. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. A 50 × 70 cm panel weighs about 800 g, so two adhesive strips hold it. The [DIY fabric wall art](/blog/diy-fabric-wall-art) guide covers stretching a backing drum-tight for a flat finish.

## Book Page Flowers and Butterflies

### 8. Book-page butterfly cluster

Cut butterflies in three sizes, fold the wings up so they cast a small shadow, then arrange twenty or so rising up the wall in a loose diagonal. Cost: £4 to £8. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. The cluster weighs under 200 g, so small adhesive dots hold it.

### 9. Layered book-page peony bouquet

Roll and curl book-page petals into three large peonies, glue them onto a round board, and tuck small rolled-page leaves between the blooms. Cost: £8 to £14. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. One bouquet weighs about 500 g, so one adhesive hook holds it. The [DIY dried flower wall decor](/blog/diy-dried-flower-wall-decor) guide shows how a floral cluster anchors a hallway wall.

## Garlands, Mobiles and Statement Walls

### 11. Book-page bunting garland

Cut book pages into triangles, fold the top edge over twine and glue it down, then drape the garland across a mantel, doorway or headboard. Cost: £3 to £6. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. A 2 metre garland weighs under 300 g, so two small adhesive hooks hold it.

### 12. Hanging book-page mobile

Cut stars, moons and butterflies from book pages, hang them at different heights from a wooden hoop or painted branch with clear thread, and suspend the mobile near a wall. Cost: £5 to £9. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. The mobile weighs about 400 g, so one ceiling hook holds it. ### 13. Full book-page feature wall

Paste whole book pages onto one wall in overlapping rows like wallpaper, seal with two coats of clear matt varnish, and keep the furniture in front plain so the wall does the talking. Cost: £10 to £18. Time: 4 to 5 hours. Difficulty: moderate. You will need about 120 pages for a 3 metre wall, so buy a box of damaged books from a charity shop.

Cut a large heart from a backing board, cover it with small folded book-page rosettes, and hang it above a bed or sofa. Cost: £4 to £7. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Layer folded book-page flowers and butterflies inside a deep shadow box, weave a short battery fairy-light string behind the top layer, and hang it where the glow washes the wall. Cost: £9 to £16. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. A 40 × 50 cm shadow box weighs about 1.2 kg, so use one screw hook.

## Choosing Your Pages

The first rule is simple: never cut up a good book. Use books that are already damaged, incomplete or falling apart, and buy them for pennies at charity shops, car boot sales and library sales — a box of unsellable paperbacks often costs £2 to £5. Old dictionaries and out-of-date textbooks are ideal because nobody misses them and their thin paper folds beautifully.

Check the paper first. Thin, slightly yellowed pages from the 1960s to the 1990s fold best and hold a curl; glossy pages from modern coffee-table books resist glue and crack when folded, so avoid them for flowers. Save illustrated pages for framed pieces, and use plain text pages for tubes, garlands and collages.

## Cutting, Folding and Gluing Techniques

Cut book pages with a sharp craft knife and a metal ruler on a cutting mat — scissors crush thin paper and leave a wavy edge. For repeated shapes like petals, cut a card template first and trace around it, then cut inside the line so no pencil marks show.

Fold against a ruler for a crisp crease, and score thick pages lightly before folding or they will crack. Hot glue is the fastest join for tubes and petals — a pea-sized dot holds a petal, while a big blob soaks through thin paper and shows as a hard lump. PVA glue dries slower but stays flexible, which suits flat collages and page walls. ## Sealing and Care

Unsealed book pages yellow fast, so seal every finished piece. A clear matt spray varnish is easiest — two light coats from about 30 cm away, letting the first dry fully, protects the surface without adding shine. For flat collages and page walls, brush on water-based matt varnish instead; it bonds the layers together.

Keep paper art out of bathrooms and steamy kitchens — damp air makes pages curl, go limp and grow mould. Hang pieces out of direct sun where you can. Dust weekly with a soft brush, and never wipe with a damp cloth: water spots thin paper permanently.

## Sizing and Layout

Paper art is visually light, so size it generously: a piece that is too small looks lost. A single piece should cover roughly two-thirds of the furniture below it — above a 180 cm sofa, aim for about 120 cm of art. Hang the centre at about 145 cm, average eye level, and keep 15 cm clear above a sofa or headboard.

For a butterfly cluster or garland, plan the layout on the floor first, then transfer it to the wall one piece at a time, starting from the centre. For a gallery, keep gaps of 5 to 8 cm. A full page wall works best on one wall only. The [DIY wall decor guide](/blog/diy-wall-decor-guide) shows how to lay out a repeating pattern.

## Hanging Hardware by Weight

Weigh the finished piece first, then pick a fixing rated for at least twice that weight. Almost every book page piece here weighs under 1 kg, and a large adhesive strip or hook holds it. From 1 to 2 kg — a sunburst mirror or a shadow box — use one screw hook into a wall plug. Over 2 kg, use two screw hooks.

Two fixings split the load and keep the piece level. On plasterboard, use adhesive strips or toggle bolts rather than a single screw, which pulls out. Paper pieces need no drilling if they are light enough, but the wall must be clean, dry and smooth or the strips will fail.

| Weight | Examples | Best fixing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 1 kg | Butterflies, rosettes, garlands, framed pages | Large adhesive strip or hook |
| 1–2 kg | Sunburst mirror, shadow box, peony panel | 1 screw hook into a wall plug |
| Over 2 kg | Large framed collage | 2 screw hooks into wall plugs |

## Renter-Friendly Methods

Book page art is one of the most renter-friendly crafts, because almost every piece is light enough for adhesive fixings. Use removable strips on a clean, dry wall, wait an hour before loading, and remove them by pulling the tab slowly down the wall. Test one strip in a hidden spot first, and never stick paper to freshly painted walls: paint needs four weeks to cure.

A picture rail is even better where one exists — hooks slide along it and the piece hangs on cord. A tension rod across a doorway holds a garland with no fixings, and a large framed piece can simply lean on a sideboard. For a page wall in a rental, paste the pages onto large boards instead of the wall, then lean the boards. ## Mistakes to Avoid

The biggest mistake is cutting up a book that is still worth reading — check charity-shop shelves first, because a damaged copy costs pennies. The second is using too much hot glue: a big blob soaks through thin paper and shows as a hard lump, so use pea-sized dots and let them set.

The third is skipping the seal. An unsealed butterfly cluster in a sunny room fades and yellows within months — two light coats of matt spray varnish take ten minutes and double the life of the piece. The fourth is hanging paper in a damp room: pages curl and can grow mould. The fifth is overfilling the wall — pick one hero piece and let the rest support it.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY book page wall art cost?

Most projects cost £3 to £22: a bunting garland £3 to £6, a butterfly cluster £4 to £8, and a sunburst mirror £12 to £22.

### Is it OK to cut up old books for wall art?

Yes, as long as the book is damaged, incomplete or otherwise unreadable — charity shops and library sales sell boxes of unsellable books for £2 to £5. Never cut up a good or valuable book; use a damaged copy instead.

### How do I stop book page art from yellowing?

Seal every finished piece with two light coats of clear matt spray varnish, hang it out of direct sunlight, and keep it away from damp rooms. Sealing slows yellowing dramatically.

### How do I hang book page wall art without damaging the wall?

Most pieces weigh under 1 kg, so removable adhesive strips or hooks hold them and peel off cleanly. For heavier pieces over 1 kg, like a sunburst mirror, use one screw hook into a wall plug.

### Can I hang book page art in a bathroom?

No — damp air makes paper curl, go limp and grow mould. Keep book page art in bedrooms, living rooms and hallways instead.

### Where can I find old books for book page crafts?

Charity shops, car boot sales and library sales all sell damaged or incomplete books for pennies. Old dictionaries and textbooks are the best sources, because their pages are large and nobody misses them.
