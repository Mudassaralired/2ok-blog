Every project below is wall-only, with cost, time and difficulty. The [DIY wall decor guide](/blog/diy-wall-decor-guide) shows how to plan a wall before you start.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every project costs $5 to $38, and most take under three hours.

> - DVDs split into two layers, so one DVD gives twice as many mosaic pieces as one CD.
> - Most CD pieces weigh under 2 kg, so one picture hook holds them.
> - Seal mosaic work, or shards loosen and fall within months.

## Mosaic and Shard Wall Art

### 1. Mosaic wall panel from cut CD shards

Glue CD shards in a tight pattern across a 60 x 40 cm plywood board, then grout the gaps with white tile grout. Cost: $8 to $18, 3 to 4 hours, moderate, about 2.5 kg, two 3 kg picture hooks.

### 2. Mosaic mirror

Glue CD shards around a 30 cm round mirror on a plywood ring, keeping shard edges flush with the mirror edge. Cost: $10 to $20, 2 to 3 hours, moderate, about 1.5 kg, one 3 kg picture hook. The [mirror wall decor ideas](/blog/mirror-wall-decor-ideas) guide shows where a mosaic mirror earns its keep.

### 3. CD shard picture frame

Glue small CD shards around a plain wooden frame, shiny side up, then seal it so no sharp edge stays exposed. Cost: $5 to $12, 2 hours, easy, about 500 g, one picture hook.

## Mirrors, Clocks and Mandalas

### 4. Iridescent sunburst mirror frame

Glue whole CDs and long, pointed CD shards in two rings around a 30 cm round mirror, alternating long and short rays. Cost: $12 to $22, 2 to 3 hours, moderate, about 2 kg, two 3 kg picture hooks.

![A CD sunburst mirror with iridescent disc rays around a round mirror, on a deep navy wall](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-cd-wall-art/in-article-2)

### 5. CD wall clock

Glue twelve number stickers or painted dots around one whole CD, fit a clock movement kit through the center hole, and add the hands. Cost: $8 to $15, 1 hour, easy, about 200 g, one picture hook.

### 6. CD mandala

Paint a whole CD black, then draw a mandala in white and metallic pens from the center outward, working in rings. Cost: $6 to $12, 2 hours, moderate, about 100 g, one small picture hook.

## Painted, Patterned and Floral Discs

### 7. Scratched-paint CD art (black paint and sgraffito)

Paint a whole CD solid black, let it dry, then scratch a design into the paint with a toothpick so the rainbow surface shows through. Cost: $5 to $10, 1 to 2 hours, easy, about 100 g, one small picture hook.

### 8. CD flower wall plaque

Cut CD shards into petal shapes, glue six petals in a circle on a plywood disc, and add a bead or small mirror in the center. Cost: $8 to $16, 2 hours, moderate, about 400 g, one picture hook.

### 9. CD string-art panel

Hammer small nails in a pattern around a plywood board, glue a whole CD in the center, then wind colored string between the nails. Cost: $10 to $18, 2 to 3 hours, moderate, about 1 kg, one 3 kg picture hook.

### 10. CD vinyl-style record wall

Paint whole CDs matte black, add a colored paper label to each center, and hang nine discs in a three-by-three grid. Cost: $9 to $16, 2 hours, easy, about 900 g, one hook per disc. The [boho wall decor ideas](/blog/boho-wall-decor-ideas) guide shows how a dark grid anchors a music corner.

## Hanging, Clustered and Light-Play Displays

### 11. CD dreamcatcher

Glue CD shards around a 25 cm embroidery hoop, weave string across it in a web pattern, and add three strands of beads and feathers below. Cost: $9 to $17, 2 to 3 hours, moderate, about 400 g, one picture hook.

### 12. CD photo collage cluster

Glue small printed photos onto the centers of six whole CDs, then hang the discs in a loose cluster with 5 cm gaps. Cost: $7 to $14, 1 to 2 hours, easy, about 600 g, one picture hook per disc. The [above sofa wall decor](/blog/above-sofa-wall-decor) guide shows the spacing that keeps a cluster from crowding the sofa.

### 13. Hanging CD mobile

Thread fishing line through the center holes of five whole CDs, knot each disc at a different height on a wooden dowel, and hang the dowel from two cords. Cost: $8 to $15, 1 to 2 hours, easy, about 500 g, one 3 kg picture hook.

### 14. CD light-reflector wall installation near a lamp

Glue whole CDs and large shards in a scattered pattern beside a table lamp, shiny side out, so the light throws rainbow flecks across the room. Cost: $10 to $20, 2 hours, easy, almost no weight, adhesive hooks.

### 15. CD garland wall hanging

Thread whole CDs onto jute twine through their center holes, spacing them 10 cm apart, and drape two or three strands across a wall. Cost: $6 to $12, 1 hour, easy, about 300 g per strand, one hook per end. Like a [basket wall decor](/blog/basket-wall-decor) display, a garland works best at slightly different heights.

## How to Cut CDs Safely

Soak each disc in hot water for five minutes: warm plastic cuts more cleanly than cold plastic. Cut with strong kitchen snips or tile nippers, never with paper scissors, which bend and slip. Always wear work gloves and eye protection, because shards fly off the cut edge without warning. Cut over a tray, so loose shards land in one place. Never snap a CD by hand: it shatters into needle-sharp splinters.

## CD vs DVD: What Changes

A CD is one thick disc of polycarbonate, about 1.2 mm thick, with a single shiny layer. It cuts cleanly after a hot-water soak, and the pieces are stiff enough to glue flat. A DVD is two thin layers, each about 0.6 mm thick, that split apart when cut. That split is a gift for mosaics: one DVD gives twice as many pieces as one CD, and the pieces are thinner and lighter. But the thin layers bend, curl and peel, so a DVD makes a poor clock face or record-wall disc. Use CDs wherever a disc stays whole, and DVDs for thin, light mosaic shards.

## Best Glues for CD Wall Art

E6000 is the strongest choice for mosaics: it bonds CD plastic to wood and glass, stays slightly flexible, and holds about 2 kg per bond once cured. Its downside is the 24 to 72 hour cure time and strong fumes, so use it in a ventilated room and let panels cure flat overnight. Hot glue is the fastest: it sets in seconds, which suits garlands, mobiles and clusters, but it softens in warm rooms and peels off smooth CD plastic under weight, so never trust it for a heavy panel. Tile adhesive is the best choice for large grouted panels: spread it on the board, press the shards in, and it holds even heavy pieces, though it needs 24 hours to cure.

## Backing Boards Compared

Plywood is the best all-rounder: strong, light, and glue grips it well. MDF is smoother and cheaper, but heavier, and it swells if it gets damp. Foam board is the lightest option and suits garlands and clusters, but glue peels off its paper surface under weight. Canvas board looks ready-made, yet its fabric flexes and cracks grout lines. Cardboard is free and fine for practice, but it warps and sags within weeks.

## Hanging CD Wall Art by Weight

CD pieces look heavier than they are, so most hang on one hook. Hang the center of any piece at about 145 cm. Under 1 kg covers the clock, the mandala, the scratched-paint art, the flower plaque, the dreamcatcher, the photo cluster discs and the garland strands: one 1 kg picture hook, or a small adhesive hook, holds them. From 1 to 2 kg covers the mosaic mirror, the sunburst frame and the string-art panel: use one 3 kg picture hook, driven into a stud or a wall plug, never a bare nail in drywall. Over 2 kg covers the mosaic wall panel, about 2.5 kg once grouted: use two 3 kg hooks spaced 20 to 30 cm apart, with a wire across the back, plus a 5 kg wall anchor in drywall.

## Sealing and Finishing So Shards Stay Put

A mosaic that looks finished on day one can shed shards by month three if it was never sealed. For grouted panels, brush clear grout sealer over the whole surface once the grout has cured for 48 hours. For un-grouted work, brush on two thin coats of clear acrylic varnish, letting the first coat dry before the second. Check every piece before hanging: add a second dab of glue to any shard that moves when pressed. Dust finished pieces monthly with a soft brush, never a damp cloth. Keep CD art out of steamy bathrooms and away from radiators.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Cutting CDs cold and bare-handed leaves razor-sharp splinters on the floor. Trusting hot glue for a heavy mosaic panel sheds shards within weeks in a warm room. Skipping the sealer lets grout crumble and unsealed shards lift off the board. Hanging a grouted panel on a single bare nail in drywall works loose and drops the piece. Using DVDs for a painted whole-disc project curls and peels the thin split layers. Placing CD art in steam or above a radiator warps the plastic.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY CD wall art cost?

Most projects cost $5 to $38: a scratched-paint disc $5 to $10, a mosaic mirror $10 to $20, a mosaic panel $8 to $18.

### How do I cut CDs safely for wall art?

Soak each disc in hot water for five minutes, cut with strong snips or tile nippers over a tray, and wear gloves and eye protection. Never snap a CD by hand: it shatters into needle-sharp splinters.

### Can I use DVDs instead of CDs for wall art?

Yes, for mosaics: a DVD splits into two layers, so one DVD gives twice as many pieces as one CD. Use CDs for whole-disc projects like clocks, because DVD layers bend and peel.

### What is the best glue for CD mosaic wall art?

E6000 is the strongest: it bonds CD plastic to wood and glass and holds about 2 kg per bond once cured in 24 to 72 hours. Hot glue is faster but softens in warm rooms.

### How do I hang heavy CD wall art?

Use two 3 kg picture hooks, spaced 20 to 30 cm apart, with a wire across the back, plus a 5 kg wall anchor for pieces over 3 kg in drywall.
