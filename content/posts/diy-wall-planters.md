DIY wall planters put living green on a wall that would otherwise sit empty. They take up no floor space, soften a bare wall, and turn a plain room into a garden. Every project below is a complete build with a real cost, a time, a difficulty rating, and a plant that suits the container.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every project here costs $7 to $45, and most take under three hours.
> - A watered planter weighs two to three times its empty weight: hang by the wet weight, never the dry one.
> - Every planter needs drainage or a drip plan, or the wall behind it will stain.
> - Trailing plants like pothos forgive beginners; herbs need the brightest spot.
> - Renters can hang light planters with adhesive hooks and tension rods — no drilling needed.

## Simple Single-Pot Planters

### 1. Ceramic wall pocket

Hang a flat-backed ceramic wall pocket on one hook and plant a trailing pothos in it. Cost: $7 to $15. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Plant: golden pothos, which trails fast and forgives missed waterings. Line it with plastic so water never touches the wall. Start with the [DIY wall decor guide](/blog/diy-wall-decor-guide).

### 2. Terracotta pot on a wall ring

Fix a metal wall ring to the wall, drop a terracotta pot into it, and let the plant spill over the rim. Cost: $8 to $14. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Plant: string of pearls, whose beaded stems drape from a high ring.

### 3. Mason jar herb planter

Screw a metal hose clamp around a mason jar, fix the clamp to a small wooden board, and mount the board on the wall. Cost: $7 to $12. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Plant: basil, which grows fast in a bright kitchen window. The [DIY mason jar wall decor](/blog/diy-mason-jar-wall-decor) guide shows more jar mounts.

### 4. Macrame hanging planter

Knot a simple macrame hanger from cotton cord, slip a pot inside, and hang it from a ceiling hook or wall bracket. Cost: $10 to $18. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Plant: spider plant, whose arching leaves and baby plantlets suit a hanging basket.

## Wooden and Pallet Planters

### 5. Pallet herb planter

Fix three small wooden boxes to a small pallet and fill them with herbs. Cost: $12 to $25. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Plants: basil, rosemary and mint, one per box. A planted pallet weighs 6 to 8 kg wet, so anchor it into studs.

### 6. Wooden box planter

Build a shallow pine box, stain it, and mount it on two brackets. Cost: $15 to $28. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Plant: heartleaf philodendron, which trails over the front edge and softens the straight lines.

![Hands potting a small pothos plant into a white ceramic wall-mounted planter](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-wall-planters/in-article-1)

### 7. Ladder shelf planter

Lean a short wooden ladder against the wall, fix the rungs level, and set small pots on each rung. Cost: $18 to $35. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. Plants: succulents and small cacti, which stay light and need little water.

### 8. Wooden crate wall planter

Mount a shallow wooden crate on the wall like an open shelf, fill it with soil in a liner, and plant it as a mini garden bed. Cost: $10 to $20. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Plant: Boston fern, which loves the humidity of a lined crate and fills it fast. A planted crate weighs 4 to 5 kg wet: two screws into studs or two 5 kg anchors.

## Upcycled and Budget Planters

### 9. Tin can herb trio

Clean three tin cans, paint them, drill drainage holes, and hang them in a row on a wooden strip with metal rings. Cost: $7 to $10. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Plants: parsley, chives and mint, one herb per can.

### 10. Plastic bottle vertical planter

Cut large plastic bottles in half lengthways, hang them in a column on a wire or wooden frame, and plant each one. Cost: $7 to $12. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Plant: pothos cuttings, which root fast and trail down the column.

### 11. Shoe organiser pocket garden

Hang a canvas shoe organiser on the wall, fill each pocket with soil, and plant herbs and succulents pocket by pocket. Cost: $9 to $16. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Plants: thyme, oregano and small succulents, which cope with shallow pockets.

### 12. Rain gutter planter

Cap a length of vinyl rain gutter, drill drainage holes, and mount it on brackets. Cost: $12 to $22. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Plants: lettuce and leafy herbs, which grow well in a shallow gutter. A 1 m gutter weighs 5 to 6 kg wet, so use two brackets into studs. Hang it at the height in the [above sofa wall decor](/blog/above-sofa-wall-decor) guide if it goes in the living room.

## Statement Living Walls

### 13. Felt pocket living wall

Fill a felt pocket panel with soil and plants, and water from the top so moisture trickles down. Cost: $25 to $45. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Plants: pothos, philodendron and ferns, mixed for texture. One full panel weighs 10 to 12 kg wet: anchor into at least two studs.

### 14. Grid panel plant wall

Hang small pots from a wall-mounted grid panel with S-hooks, moving them as plants grow. Cost: $20 to $40. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Plants: air plants on the top hooks and trailing pothos lower down. The grid makes rearranging easy: swap a struggling plant for a fresh one in seconds.

### 15. Moss frame planter

Fill a deep picture frame with preserved moss, tuck in a few air plants, and hang it like art. Cost: $15 to $30. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Plants: air plants and preserved moss, which need no soil at all. Mist air plants weekly and soak them for 20 minutes every two weeks. The soft green texture sits beautifully beside the [boho wall decor ideas](/blog/boho-wall-decor-ideas).

## Best Plants for Wall Planters by Light Level

A wall planter cannot be moved easily, so match the plant to the light the wall gets. Bright light means within 1 m of a sunny window; medium means a bright room without direct sun; low means more than 3 m from the nearest window.

| Plant | Light | Water | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Golden pothos | Low to bright | Weekly | Trailing pockets, bottles, grids |
| Heartleaf philodendron | Low to medium | Weekly | Wooden boxes, felt walls |
| Spider plant | Medium to bright | Weekly | Macrame hangers |
| Basil | Bright | Twice a week | Mason jars, kitchen pallets |

Buy small plants and let them grow into the planter. A sun-loving herb on a dark wall will stretch, pale and die within weeks, so check the tag before you buy.

## Drainage and Watering Without Ruining the Wall

Drainage is the part most guides skip, and it is the part that saves your wall. A damp patch behind a planter grows mould and stains paint. Every planter needs one of three plans: a hole with a saucer, a waterproof liner, or a fast-draining soil mix.

Drill at least two drainage holes in every rigid container. Put a saucer or drip tray under any planter over furniture or flooring. Line wooden planters with a plastic liner, and leave a 1 cm air gap behind the planter so splashes dry.

Water slowly: a wall planter holds little soil, so it floods fast. Water until a few drops leave the drainage hole, then empty the saucer after 15 minutes. Check the wall behind a new planter for the first month. In winter, cut watering in half: plants grow slowly in low light and rot in wet soil.

## Hanging Wall Planters by Weight

A watered planter is much heavier than an empty one, and that is where most wall planters fail. Wet potting mix weighs about 1 kg per litre, so a planter holding 3 litres of soil gains 3 kg the moment you water it. Always hang by the wet weight, using hardware rated for at least double that weight.

| Planter, watered | Wet weight | Hardware | Fixing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single ceramic pocket | 1 to 2 kg | 3 kg hook | Stud or wall plug |
| Terracotta pot in a ring | 2 to 3 kg | 3 kg hook | Stud or wall plug |
| Wooden box, crate | 4 to 5 kg | Two 5 kg anchors | Two studs or anchors |
| Felt pocket living wall | 10 to 12 kg | Four heavy anchors | Two studs minimum |

Find the studs first and mark them before you drill. Drywall alone holds almost nothing: a single screw in bare drywall pulls out under 2 kg. Use wall anchors in drywall and screws into studs for anything over 4 kg. Test every mount before loading it with plants, and check the fixings every six months.

## Renter-Safe Wall Planters

Renters can have wall planters too, with no holes and no lost deposit. Stick to light planters under 2 kg and use removable hardware. Large adhesive hooks hold 1 to 3 kg on clean, smooth walls: wipe the spot with rubbing alcohol first, press the hook for 30 seconds, and wait an hour before hanging anything.

Tension rods turn a window frame into a planter rail: hang small pots from S-hooks and take the whole thing down in a minute. A leaning ladder planter needs no fixing if the top is secured with a removable adhesive strip. Avoid heavy planters in a rental: pallets, felt walls and gutters need studs and screws.

## Care and Maintenance

Water on the same day each week, and check the soil with a finger first: if the top 2 cm is dry, water; if it is damp, wait. Rotate each planter a quarter turn every two weeks so plants grow evenly instead of leaning one way.

Feed plants once a month in spring and summer with half-strength liquid fertiliser, and skip feeding in winter. Prune trailing plants when they get leggy: snip just above a leaf node, and root the cuttings in water. Yellow leaves usually mean overwatering; brown crispy edges mean underwatering or dry air.

Buy pots and timber from discount stores and salvage yards: pallets are often free from garden centres. A thriving plant makes a $7 planter look expensive, while a dying plant makes a $40 planter look cheap.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping drainage is the biggest mistake: a planter with no drainage hole drowns the roots and stains the wall. Hanging by the dry weight is the second: a planter that holds fine empty can rip out after the first watering. Garden soil adds weight and holds too much water.

A sun-loving herb on a dark wall stretches, pales and dies within weeks. Watering on a schedule instead of checking the soil rots roots in winter and dries them in summer. A heavy planter on a single bare nail in drywall is a fall waiting to happen. Forgetting the saucer means drips on the floor below. Mint spreads fast, so always give it its own container.

## FAQ

### How much does a DIY wall planter cost?

Most single-pot planters cost $7 to $15, wooden boxes and crates $13 to $28, and a full felt pocket living wall $25 to $45. One $8 pothos gives you cuttings for several planters.

### Which plants are best for wall planters?

Pothos, philodendron and spider plants are the safest: they tolerate low light, forgive missed waterings, and trail nicely. Herbs suit bright kitchen walls; succulents and air plants suit sunny, dry spots.

### How do I stop a wall planter leaking onto the wall?

Drill drainage holes, put a saucer under the planter, line wooden planters with a plastic liner, and leave a 1 cm air gap behind it. Water slowly, empty the saucer after 15 minutes, and check the wall behind it for the first month.

### How heavy is a watered wall planter?

Wet potting mix weighs about 1 kg per litre: a small planter weighs 2 to 3 kg watered, a wooden box 4 to 5 kg, and a full felt living wall 10 to 12 kg. Always hang by the wet weight, using hardware rated for at least double that weight.

### Can renters hang wall planters without drilling?

Yes: use adhesive hooks rated for 1 to 3 kg on clean smooth walls, tension rods with S-hooks, or a leaning ladder planter secured with a removable strip. Stick to light planters under 2 kg, and avoid heavy pallets and felt walls.
