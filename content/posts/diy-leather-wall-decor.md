Leather is heavy for its size, so most pieces weigh 1 to 3 kg and need proper fixings, but it never frays, it ages beautifully and it suits every room. A single hide or a bag of scraps covers several projects. For the wider starting point, see our [DIY wall decor ideas](/blog/diy-wall-decor-ideas) pillar.

## Classic Leather Wall Art

### 1. Fringe leather wall hanging

Cut a rectangle of tan leather about 60 by 80 cm, fold the top over a wooden dowel, and cut the bottom into fringe strips 20 cm long. Cost: $8 to $16. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 2. Leather pennant wall flag

Cut a long triangle of dark brown leather, about 25 cm wide and 70 cm long, and stitch a wooden dowel through a folded pocket at the top. Cost: $3 to $7. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy.

### 3. Tooled leather wall panel

Stamp a repeated pattern into a square of vegetable-tanned leather about 45 cm across, then dye it cognac and mount it on a wooden backing board. Cost: $10 to $18. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 4. Leather strap wall hooks

Mount three short leather straps, each about 30 cm long, vertically on the wall with brass screws, and loop each through a metal ring to make a hook. Cost: $5 to $11. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

## Statement Leather Pieces

### 5. Large patchwork leather wall hanging

Stitch squares of mixed leather scraps, each about 15 cm across, into a panel about 90 by 110 cm, then hang it from a dowel with two leather loops. Cost: $12 to $24. Time: 4 to 5 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 6. Leather and wood wall shelf straps

Hang a wooden shelf board about 60 cm long from two wide leather straps fixed above it, so the shelf floats on the straps. Cost: $9 to $17. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: moderate.

### 7. Scalloped leather wall hanging

Cut three overlapping rows of leather scallops, each about 60 cm wide, and stitch them onto a backing cloth hung from a dowel. Cost: $7 to $14. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. It pairs well with the [DIY rattan wall decor](/blog/diy-rattan-wall-decor) guide.

### 8. Braided leather wall wreath

Braid three long strips of soft leather into a thick rope, join the ends into a circle about 45 cm across, and hang it from a leather loop. Cost: $6 to $12. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

## Small-Space and Bedroom Leather Art

### 9. Leather photo clip wall

Fix a leather strap about 80 cm long horizontally on the wall, then clip photos to it with small brass clips. Cost: $3 to $8. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Use two screws into wall plugs.

### 10. Leather jewellery wall hanger

Mount a leather panel about 30 by 45 cm on a bedroom wall with small brass hooks, and hang necklaces, bracelets and earrings from them. Cost: $4 to $9. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Pick hooks that point slightly upward.

### 11. Mini leather frame trio

Make three small square frames, 20 cm across, from folded leather strips joined at the corners, and hang them in a row with a small print inside each. Cost: $4 to $9. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. See the [DIY fabric wall art](/blog/diy-fabric-wall-art) guide for soft pairings.

## Textured, Lit and Living Leather Art

### 12. Leather tassel wall hanging

Cut twenty leather tassels, each about 15 cm long, tie them to a wooden dowel, and trim the ends into a gentle V shape. Cost: $5 to $10. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy.

### 13. Leather fairy-light panel

Build a frame from leather-covered wooden battens, stretch twine across it in a grid, and weave a 2-metre battery LED fairy light string through. Cost: $8 to $14. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Use battery LEDs only, never mains lights near dry leather.

### 14. Leather wall plant holder

Stitch a leather sling around a small terracotta pot, then mount it with two brackets so a trailing plant spills down over the leather. Cost: $6 to $13. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Line the pot with a plastic saucer so water never touches the leather. For spacing, see the [above sofa wall decor](/blog/above-sofa-wall-decor) guide.

## Which Leather to Pick: Genuine, Faux, Bonded, Suede and Scraps

Leather comes in five useful forms. Genuine full-grain leather is the thickest and strongest: use it for tooling and dyeing. Faux leather, usually polyurethane on a fabric backing, is cheap and easy to cut and glue, but it cannot be tooled or dyed. Bonded leather is leather fibres pressed with glue: it looks real at first but cracks and peels within a year, so avoid it for pieces you want to keep. Suede is soft and matte: best for tassels, braids and soft panels. Scraps are mixed offcuts sold by weight: the cheapest way to build a patchwork panel.

| Leather type | Best for | Thickness | Price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Genuine full-grain | Tooling, dyeing, straps | 2 to 3 mm | $12 to $25 a hide | Ages beautifully |
| Faux leather | Cutting, gluing, fringe | 1 to 2 mm | $5 to $10 a metre | Cannot be tooled |
| Bonded leather | Short-term pieces only | 1 to 2 mm | $4 to $8 a metre | Peels within a year |
| Suede | Tassels, braids, panels | 1 to 2 mm | $8 to $15 a metre | Marks easily, keep dry |
| Leather scraps | Patchwork, small pieces | Mixed | $3 to $8 a bag | Best value for beginners |

Buy hides and faux leather from a craft store or an online leather supplier, and scraps from a leather workshop. Check every hide for thin spots, scars and holes before you buy: a scar in the middle of a panel is hard to hide. Vegetable-tanned leather is the only type that takes tooling and dye well, so ask for it by name. For a lighter woven texture, see our [DIY rattan wall decor](/blog/diy-rattan-wall-decor) guide.

## Cutting and Tooling Leather

Leather dulls blades fast, so the cutting method matters more than the tool. Use a sharp craft knife with a fresh blade and a steel ruler: fabric scissors leave a ragged line. Always cut on a self-healing mat, in several light passes rather than one hard push, which slips and scars the leather. For curves and scallops, trace a template first and cut just inside the line.

To tool a pattern, dampen vegetable-tanned leather lightly with a sponge until it darkens, then press metal stamps into it with a mallet. Work on a hard, flat surface and strike each stamp once, firmly. Let the leather dry fully before dyeing: wet leather takes dye unevenly. Punch stitch holes with an awl or a hole punch first, never force a needle through thick leather. For a similar hand-cut routine on wood, see our [DIY wood wall art](/blog/diy-wood-wall-art) guide.

## Dyeing, Sealing and Caring for Leather

Raw vegetable-tanned leather is pale, so dye gives it the warm cognac and chestnut tones that make leather wall decor glow. Apply leather dye with a wool dauber in thin, even coats, and let each coat dry for at least 30 minutes. Two coats give a rich colour; three give a deep one. Always test the dye on a scrap first: the colour darkens as it dries. Faux leather cannot be dyed, so buy it in the colour you want.

Seal every piece before it goes on the wall. Brush on a thin coat of leather balm or a clear acrylic leather finish, which costs about $7 a bottle and seals a dozen projects. Sealing stops dust and sunlight from drying the surface and fading the colour. In a sunny room, re-seal pieces once a year. Keep leather out of bathrooms: steam stiffens it and leaves water marks. Dust with a dry cloth and never use household cleaners, which strip the finish.

## Sizing Your Leather Wall Decor

Size the piece to the wall, not to the hide you happen to have. Above a sofa, the piece should span about two thirds of the sofa width: a 180 cm sofa takes a 120 cm hanging. In a hallway, keep the piece under 10 cm deep and choose a flat piece like the frame trio. Hang it so its centre sits at eye level, about 145 cm from the floor.

## Hanging Leather Wall Decor by Weight

Leather is heavy for its size: a large patchwork panel can weigh 3 to 4 kg, while a small pennant weighs under 300 g. Weigh the finished piece before choosing hardware, and always hang from two points on pieces over 1 kg. Use a picture wire across the back, not a single loop of leather, which stretches and lets the piece tilt.

| Finished weight | Hardware | Fixings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 500 g | Picture nail or adhesive hook | 1 |
| 500 g to 1.5 kg | Two picture hooks, wire back | 2 |
| 1.5 kg to 3 kg | Two screws into wall plugs, wire back | 2 |
| Over 3 kg | Two screws into a stud or masonry plugs | 2 |

Renters should favour picture hooks over nails. Never hang a piece over 1 kg from a single adhesive hook, or any leather piece from a bare nail in drywall.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Cutting with fabric scissors is the biggest mistake: they crush the edge and leave a ragged line. Using bonded leather is the second: it peels within a year and ruins a piece you spent hours on. Skipping the sealing step is the third: dust and sunlight dry the surface and fade the colour. Hanging a heavy panel from one hook is the fourth: the piece tilts, then falls. Dyeing without testing on a scrap is the fifth: the colour darkens as it dries. Stitching without punching holes first is the sixth: the needle bends or tears the leather.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY leather wall decor cost?

Most cost $3 to $15: a pennant or photo clip wall $3 to $8, a fringe hanging $6 to $11, and a large patchwork panel $12 to $24.

### What type of leather is best for wall decor?

Genuine vegetable-tanned leather for tooling and dyeing, faux leather for cutting and gluing, suede for tassels and braids, and scraps for patchwork.

### Can I use faux leather for wall decor?

Yes: faux leather cuts and glues easily and costs $5 to $10 a metre, but it cannot be tooled or dyed.

### How do you cut leather without ragged edges?

Use a sharp craft knife with a fresh blade and a steel ruler, cut on a self-healing mat, and make several light passes.

### Does leather wall decor need sealing?

Yes: brush on a thin coat of leather balm or a clear acrylic leather finish before hanging. Sealing stops dust and sunlight from drying the surface.

### How do you hang leather wall decor?

Under 500 g takes one picture nail or adhesive hook; 500 g to 1.5 kg takes two picture hooks with a wire back; over 1.5 kg takes two screws into wall plugs.
