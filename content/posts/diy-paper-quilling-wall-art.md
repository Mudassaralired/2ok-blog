DIY paper quilling wall art turns narrow strips of colored paper into raised, sculptural pictures. You roll, pinch and glue simple coils into flowers, mandalas and trees. Every project below is wall-only, with a real cost, time and difficulty rating.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Every project here costs $4 to $28, and most take under four hours of rolling.
> - Use 120 gsm paper in 5 mm strips for wall art: lighter paper sags, heavier paper cracks.
> - Quilling is dimensional, so only a shadow box or a deep frame protects it.
> - Most finished pieces weigh under 400 g, so one picture hook holds them.
> - Seal every piece against humidity and sunlight, or the colors fade within a year.

## Floral Quilling Wall Art

### 1. Quilled floral heart

Fill a large heart outline with layered quilled flowers in terracotta, blush and cream, using teardrop petals and tight-coil centers, then add sage marquise leaves to fill the gaps. Cost: $6 to $12. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: easy. It weighs about 250 g, so one picture hook holds it.

### 2. Wildflower meadow panel

Scatter small quilled daisies, lavender spikes and grasses across a 30 × 40 cm cream board, leaving space between the stems so it reads as a landscape, not a pile of flowers. Cost: $8 to $14. Time: 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. It weighs about 300 g, so one picture hook holds it. Glue the tallest stems last.

### 3. Sunflower statement piece

Build one oversized sunflower from 3 cm marquise petals in two shades of gold, a tight-coil brown center, and two broad green leaves. Cost: $7 to $12. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. It weighs about 200 g, so one picture hook holds it.

### 4. Lavender sprig trio

Make three narrow vertical panels, each with one lavender sprig of stacked purple teardrops on a thin green stem, hung in a row with 5 cm gaps. Cost: $5 to $9. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. The trio weighs about 200 g in total, so one picture hook per panel holds them. ## Mandalas and Geometric Quilling

### 5. Terracotta and cream mandala

Build a symmetrical mandala from rings of teardrops, marquise coils and scrolls in terracotta, cream and sage, working outward from a tight-coil center. Cost: $10 to $18. Time: 5 to 6 hours. Difficulty: moderate. It weighs about 350 g, so one picture hook holds it.

### 6. Geometric hexagon panel

Arrange quilled hexagons in a honeycomb grid, alternating solid cream coils with terracotta and sage outlines, and leave two cells empty. Cost: $8 to $13. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. It weighs about 300 g, so one picture hook holds it. Draw the grid first: a wonky grid spoils this design.

### 7. Rainbow ombre circle

Fill a 40 cm circle with rows of tight coils shifting gradually from deep red through gold to teal, so it reads as a gradient from across the room. Cost: $9 to $15. Time: 4 to 5 hours. Difficulty: moderate. It weighs about 350 g, so one picture hook holds it. Buy pre-cut ombre packs: hand-dyed strips fade unevenly and break the gradient.

Layer curved quilling strips in deep blue, teal and white into three rolling waves, with tight white coils as foam at each crest. Cost: $10 to $16. Time: 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. It weighs about 300 g, so one picture hook holds it.

## The Quilling Shape Chart

Every project above uses the same small set of shapes. Roll a strip around a slotted quilling tool, let it relax to the size you want, glue the end, then pinch it into shape. A tight coil stays rolled hard and suits flower centers and berries. A loose coil relaxes into a ring and suits filler and backgrounds. Pinch one end of a loose coil for a teardrop, the petal in almost every flower here. Pinch both ends for a marquise, the leaf in the tree of life and the peacock feathers.

| Shape | How to make it | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tight coil | Roll hard, glue at once | Flower centers, berries, buds |
| Loose coil | Roll, relax, then glue | Filler, backgrounds, gradient rows |
| Teardrop | Pinch one end of a loose coil | Petals, sunflower rays |
| Marquise | Pinch both ends of a loose coil | Leaves, feathers, tree foliage |
| Heart scroll | Fold strip, roll both ends inward | Mandala rings, wreath fillers |
| S scroll | Fold strip, roll both ends outward | Borders, lettering, flourishes |
| Fringed flower | Cut fringe into a wide strip, roll up | Fluffy blooms, lavender spikes |


## Choosing Paper Weight and Strip Width

Paper weight decides whether your wall art lasts or sags. Standard 120 gsm paper is right for wall art: it holds a crisp coil, pinches cleanly and keeps its shape on a vertical wall. Lighter 80 gsm paper is fine for cards, but its coils relax and droop once hung. Heavier paper at 160 gsm and above looks bold but cracks when pinched into teardrops.

Strip width decides the look. Narrow 3 mm strips make delicate, jewelry-like pieces with fine detail, and they suit small frames and monograms. Standard 5 mm strips are the best all-round choice: bold enough to read from across the room and sold in the widest color range. Wide 10 mm strips make chunky coils for statement pieces like the peacock, but they use twice as much paper. Start with 5 mm strips in 120 gsm paper. The [DIY paper wall decor](/blog/diy-paper-wall-decor) guide covers more paper crafts.

Buy pre-cut strips: a pack of 100 in mixed colors costs $3 to $5, and every strip is the same width, which keeps your coils even. Acid-free paper costs about $1 more per pack and is worth it: ordinary paper yellows within a year on a sunny wall.

## Framing, Hanging and Preserving Quilling

Quilling is dimensional, so an ordinary frame will crush it. The coils stand 1 to 2 cm proud of the board, and flat glass presses straight onto them, flattening petals and snapping scrolls. Use a shadow box frame with at least 3 cm of depth between board and glass: many frames labeled "deep" are only 2 cm. The [DIY shadow box wall decor](/blog/diy-shadow-box-wall-decor) guide shows how to build one yourself for about $12.

Glue the piece to a rigid backing board, not paper: 3 mm foam board or thin plywood stops warping. Use white PVA glue with a toothpick, a tiny dot per coil, and let each section dry for an hour before moving the board. Never use a hot glue gun: the glue strings across the coils and the heat warps the paper.

Quilling is light: most pieces here weigh 150 to 400 g, so a single picture hook rated for 1 kg holds them. Hang the hook at 145 cm, average eye level. For the tree of life and the peacock, use a wire hanger on the back of the frame, never a single sawtooth tab.

Paper is vulnerable to humidity and sunlight, and an unsealed piece fades and softens within a year. Spray the finished piece with two light coats of clear matte acrylic sealer before framing it. Keep quilling out of direct sun and steamy rooms unless it sits behind glass in a sealed shadow box. Dust it with a soft brush, never a damp cloth. ## Mistakes to Avoid

The biggest mistake is framing quilling in an ordinary flat frame, which crushes the coils. The second is using 80 gsm paper, which sags on a vertical wall within weeks. The third is too much glue: a visible blob of PVA yellows and shines, so use a toothpick and a tiny dot per coil. The fourth is skipping the sealer, which lets humidity soften the coils and sunlight fade the colors. The fifth is starting with the hardest project: the tree of life and the peacock need hundreds of coils, so build the lavender trio first. The sixth is hanging quilling in a bathroom without a sealed frame. The [gallery wall layouts](/blog/gallery-wall-layouts) guide shows how to space mixed sizes evenly.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY paper quilling wall art cost?

Most projects cost $4 to $28: a mini wreath $4 to $8, a floral heart $6 to $12, and a tree of life $12 to $20, plus a shadow box frame. A beginner kit costs $8 to $15.

### What paper weight is best for quilling wall art?

Use 120 gsm paper in 5 mm strips. Lighter 80 gsm paper sags on a wall, and heavier 160 gsm paper cracks when pinched. Buy acid-free paper so colors do not yellow.

### Can I frame quilling in a normal picture frame?

No, an ordinary flat frame crushes the raised coils. Use a shadow box with at least 3 cm of depth, or build a deep frame yourself for about $12.

### How do I stop quilling wall art from fading or going limp?

Spray it with two light coats of clear matte acrylic sealer before framing, keep it out of direct sunlight, and never hang unsealed quilling in a steamy bathroom. Dust with a soft brush, never a damp cloth.

### Is paper quilling hard for a beginner?

No, it uses only seven basic shapes, and most beginners finish a first piece in two to three hours. Start with the lavender trio or the mini wreath, then try the mandala.
