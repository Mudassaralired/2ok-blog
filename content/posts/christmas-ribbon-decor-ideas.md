## Ribbon Decor for the Christmas Tree

### 1. Velvet ribbon tree cascade

Drape wide red velvet ribbon in long loops down the tree, tucking each loop in every 30 cm. Cost: $12 to $25. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Use 2.5-inch wired velvet so the loops hold their shape.

### 2. Ribbon swags on the tree

Run ribbon around the tree in loose swags, dipping between branches, with a bow at each swag. Cost: $8 to $18. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. One 10-yard spool covers a 6-foot tree. See the [Christmas tree decorating ideas](/blog/christmas-tree-decorating-ideas) guide for ornaments.

### 3. Ribbon bows in the branches

Make 10 to 12 small bows from 1.5-inch wired ribbon and tuck them into the tree, one per branch tip, so they read as oversized ornaments. Cost: $6 to $14. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Mix red and gold for a classic look.

### 4. Ribbon tree topper bow

Replace the star or angel with one large wired-ribbon bow, 30 to 40 cm across, with long tails trailing down the top branches. Cost: $5 to $12. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. See the DIY bow method below.

## Ribbon Decor for Wreaths and Garland

### 5. Big bow on a plain wreath

Wire one oversized bow from 4-inch wired ribbon to the bottom of a plain green wreath. Cost: $5 to $12. Time: 20 minutes. Difficulty: easy. It turns a $10 wreath into a $40 one. The [DIY Christmas wreath ideas](/blog/diy-christmas-wreath-ideas) guide shows how to build the wreath base itself.

### 6. Ribbon loops in a wreath

Cut ribbon into 25 cm strips, fold each into a loop and pin it into the wreath with floral wire, spacing the loops evenly. Cost: $6 to $15. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: moderate. Wired ribbon keeps the loops standing proud.

### 7. Plaid ribbon bows along a garland

Space three to five plaid bows evenly along a mantel or doorway garland, wiring each through the greenery so it sits on top. Cost: $8 to $18. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. See the [Christmas garland ideas](/blog/christmas-garland-ideas) guide for garland bases.

### 8. Ribbon tails on a garland

Cut long ribbon tails, 60 to 90 cm, and tie them to the garland at intervals so they hang down and move when someone walks past. Cost: $4 to $10. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. V-cut the ends so they will not fray.

## Ribbon Decor for Stairs and the Mantel

### 9. Plaid bows on the staircase banister

Wrap pine garland around the banister, then wire a large plaid bow at every newel post and each turn. Cost: $10 to $25. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. See the [Christmas staircase decor](/blog/christmas-staircase-decor) guide for the full look.

### 10. Ribbon-wrapped banister swags

Weave wide ribbon through the spindles in a loose spiral, finishing with a bow at the bottom newel post. Cost: $6 to $15. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Wired ribbon stays put; soft ribbon needs a pin every few spindles.

### 11. Ribbon bows on the mantel garland

Wire three bows across the mantel garland, one in the centre and one near each end, then tuck short tails between the greenery. Cost: $7 to $16. Time: 45 minutes. Difficulty: easy. See the [Christmas mantel decor](/blog/christmas-mantel-decor) guide for layering with stockings and candles.

### 12. Ribbon-wrapped stocking cuffs

Wrap velvet ribbon around the cuff of each stocking and tie a small bow at the front. Cost: $3 to $8. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. One spool covers a whole row of stockings. Match the tree ribbon for a pulled-together room.

## Ribbon Decor for the Table and Gifts

### 13. Ribbon napkin rings

Wrap a 20 cm strip around each rolled napkin, tie a small bow and tuck in a sprig of rosemary or pine. Cost: $3 to $8. Time: 20 minutes. Difficulty: easy. See the [Christmas table decor](/blog/christmas-table-decor) guide for the full setting.

### 14. Wide ribbon table runner

Lay a 4-inch ribbon down the centre of the table, ends hanging over each edge, then set candles and greenery along it. Cost: $5 to $12. Time: 15 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Velvet reads richest; burlap suits a rustic table.

### 15. Ribbon-wrapped gifts

Wrap gifts in plain kraft paper, then tie wide wired ribbon and a full bow instead of printed paper. Cost: $4 to $10. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. See the [Christmas gift wrapping ideas](/blog/christmas-gift-wrapping-ideas) guide for bow techniques.

## How Much Ribbon Do You Need

Guessing yardage is the most common ribbon mistake. A cascade needs roughly three times the tree height, because each loop dips and doubles back. A garland needs about one and a half times its length. A wreath needs far less than people expect — one 10-yard spool makes the bow and the loops.

| Project | Ribbon width | Yards needed | Metres needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6-foot tree cascade | 2.5 inches | 20 to 25 | 18 to 23 |
| 7-foot tree cascade | 2.5 inches | 20 to 30 | 18 to 27 |
| 9-foot tree cascade | 4 inches | 30 to 40 | 27 to 37 |
| 6-foot garland, bows only | 2.5 inches | 5 to 8 | 5 to 7 |
| 6-foot garland, woven ribbon | 2.5 inches | 10 to 12 | 9 to 11 |
| Standard wreath, bow + loops | 2.5 inches | 6 to 10 | 5 to 9 |
| Staircase, 12 steps | 4 inches | 12 to 15 | 11 to 14 |

Ribbon is sold in 10-yard spools, so round up to the next whole spool. A 6-foot tree cascade takes two to three spools; a full house takes five to seven. Buy all spools from the same dye lot: two spools of the same red can differ under lights.

## Ribbon Types Compared

Ribbon type matters more than colour. Velvet looks expensive and photographs beautifully, but it creases if folded and costs the most per yard. Wired ribbon has a thin wire in each edge, so it holds loops and bows without flopping — the best all-rounder. Satin is smooth, shiny and cheap, but it slips off branches and needs pins. Burlap is stiff and rustic, ideal for farmhouse looks, though it sheds fibres. Plaid reads instantly as Christmas and hides creases, but busy patterns clash with patterned ornaments.

| Ribbon type | Best for | Holds shape | Price per 10-yard spool | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Velvet | Tree cascades, bows | Good | $8 to $15 | Richest look |
| Wired | Trees, wreaths, garlands | Excellent | $6 to $12 | Best all-rounder |
| Satin | Gifts, napkins | Poor | $3 to $6 | Slips |
| Burlap | Rustic mantels, stairs | Good | $5 to $9 | Sheds fibres |
| Plaid | Bows, garlands | Good | $6 to $12 | Hides creases |

If you buy only one ribbon, buy wired velvet in 2.5 inches: it covers tree, wreath and garland, and looks far more expensive than it is. Keep satin for gifts and napkins, where its shine shows. Avoid sheer organza outdoors: it fades in sun and tears in wind.

## DIY: A Big Wired-Ribbon Bow

A shop-bought bow this size costs $15 to $25; the same bow costs $5 to $8 in ribbon and takes 20 minutes. Start with 2 yards of 4-inch wired ribbon. Fold it into a 20 cm loop, pinch it in your left hand, then make three more loops the same size, alternating sides. Keep pinching the centre; if you let go, the loops collapse. Cut two 45 cm tails and lay them behind the loops. Wrap 15 cm of floral wire tightly around the pinched centre and twist it at the back. Fan the loops out, shape the wire edges until the bow looks full, then V-cut the tails. Wire the bow on through the twisted wire at the back.

## DIY: Cascading Ribbon Down the Tree

The cascade is the most dramatic ribbon look, and it is easier than it looks. Start at the top, the ribbon end tucked behind the top branches and pinned with a floral pin. Let it fall about 45 cm, tuck it into the branches, then let it billow out into a loop before tucking it back in. Repeat down the tree, making each loop slightly larger than the one above, so the cascade widens toward the bottom. Use three to five cascades spaced evenly around the tree, loops on the outside where the light catches them. Check the spacing as you go: uneven spacing looks messy. At the bottom, cut the ribbon and tuck the end under a branch. Add the ornaments after the ribbon, so nothing hides the loops.

## Storing and Reusing Ribbon After Christmas

Good ribbon lasts five or more seasons if you store it right. Roll each length back onto its spool, or around cardboard if the spool is gone, and tape the end. Never fold ribbon: folds become permanent creases, and velvet creases never come out. Store spools upright in a box, standing on end, so the ribbon does not flatten. Keep the box in a dry cupboard, away from damp and sun — damp makes burlap smell musty and sun fades red ribbon to pink. Label each spool with colour and width, so next December you find the right one fast. Bows store best stuffed with tissue paper in a shoebox, loops up, nothing stacked on top. Creased ribbon recovers with a steamer held 15 cm away, or a low iron through a cloth — never iron velvet directly.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Using non-wired ribbon on a tree is the biggest mistake: soft ribbon flops and looks limp in a day. Buying too little ribbon is the second: a half-dressed tree looks worse than no ribbon at all, so measure first. Mixing three or more ribbon patterns in one room is the third: pick one pattern and one solid. Hanging ribbon near a real candle is the fourth: ribbon is fabric and it burns, so use LED candles instead. Wiring bows too tightly is the fifth: a crushed bow looks flat, so fan the loops out after wiring. Forgetting to V-cut the tails is the sixth: straight-cut tails fray and look unfinished. And storing ribbon folded is the seventh: the creases never come out.

## FAQ

### How much ribbon do I need for a Christmas tree?

A 6-foot tree needs 20 to 25 yards for a full cascade, a 7-foot tree 20 to 30 yards, and a 9-foot tree 30 to 40 yards. A 6-foot tree takes two to three 10-yard spools.

### What is the best ribbon for Christmas decor?

Wired ribbon is best overall because its wire edges hold loops and bows in shape. Wired velvet in 2.5 inches is the single best buy: it suits the tree, wreath and garland.

### How do you keep ribbon from falling off a Christmas tree?

Tuck it into the branches every 30 cm and pin each tuck with a floral pin. Wired ribbon grips better than soft ribbon. Add ornaments after the ribbon.

### Can I reuse Christmas ribbon next year?

Yes, if you store it rolled on its spool, upright in a dry box, away from sun. Never fold ribbon: folds become permanent creases. Well-stored ribbon lasts five or more seasons.

### Is ribbon safe near Christmas lights?

Yes, with LED lights, which stay cool. Keep ribbon away from real candles and mains fairy lights that run warm. Never drape ribbon over a hot bulb.
