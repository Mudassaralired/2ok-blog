DIY map wall art turns a blank wall into a story about where you have been and where you still want to go. A framed vintage map costs less than a takeaway dinner, and one afternoon turns it into the most personal piece in the room. Every project below is wall-only, with a real cost, time and difficulty rating. Start with the [DIY wall decor guide](/blog/diy-wall-decor-guide) if you are planning the whole wall.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - All 15 projects cost $5 to $60; most take under three hours.
> - Free printable maps keep the cheapest builds at $5; vintage maps look richer but cost more.
> - Cork and foam boards take pins without wall damage, so travel maps stay changeable.
> - Seal paper maps with two thin coats of matte sealer, or they curl and fade.
> - A large map should span about two thirds of the sofa or bed below it.

## Classic Framed Map Art

### 1. Framed vintage world map

Print a vintage world map at 60 by 90 cm and frame it in a simple wooden frame. Cost: $5 to $15. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. The aged colours do the decorating, so keep the frame plain.

### 2. Framed city street map

Pick the city where you met or grew up, print its street map large, and frame it with a wide white mount. Cost: $8 to $18. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. It reads as abstract art from afar and as a memory up close, so it suits a hallway.

### 3. Map in a floating frame

Sandwich a printed map between two sheets of glass in a floating frame so the wall colour shows through the edges. Cost: $12 to $22. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Trim the map 2 cm smaller than the glass, and a cheap print looks gallery-made.

### 4. Antique map print with tea-stained edges

Print a map on cream paper, brush the edges with strong black tea, and dry it flat before framing. Cost: $6 to $14. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. The stain warms the paper and hides its bright white, and it pairs well with the [DIY wall decor ideas](/blog/diy-wall-decor-ideas) hub.

## Pin and Travel Tracking Maps

### 5. Cork pin map with travel flags

Glue a cork world map onto a 60 by 90 cm board, then mark each trip with a flag pin. Cost: $15 to $28. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. One flag colour marks places visited, another the wish list, so the map becomes a plan as well as a record.

### 6. Push-pin world map with colour-coded pins

Stretch a printed world map over a cork board, frame it, and pin every place you have stayed. Cost: $10 to $20. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Red for home bases, blue for holidays and gold for honeymoon spots read clearly at a glance.

### 7. Scratch-off travel map in a frame

Buy a gold-foil scratch-off world map, frame it behind glass, and scratch off each country you visit. Cost: $18 to $30. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: easy. Dust the foil first: grit under it scratches the bright map underneath.

### 8. String-and-pin route map

Pin the start and end of a road trip on a framed map, then wind coloured string between the pins to trace it. Cost: $12 to $20. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: moderate. One string per trip keeps it readable; five trips tangle, so label each route instead.

## Wooden and Statement Map Art

### 9. Layered wooden world map

Buy or cut a laser-cut wooden world map in three tones of plywood and mount the continents in layers on spacers. Cost: $35 to $60. Time: 3 to 4 hours. Difficulty: moderate. The layered edges cast soft shadows that change through the day, making this the most striking piece here.

### 10. Wood-burned map plaque

Trace a continent outline onto a 40 by 60 cm pine board and burn the lines with a wood-burning pen. Cost: $15 to $25. Time: 3 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Practise on scrap wood first: a wobbly line is charming, but a burnt blob is hard to fix.

![Close-up of a layered wooden world map with continents in different wood tones on a pale sage-green wall](https://res.cloudinary.com/zhzxqahv/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1200/2ok-blog/diy-map-wall-art/in-article-2)

### 11. Map decoupage on a wooden panel

Tear a paper map into pieces and glue them over a wooden panel with decoupage medium. Cost: $10 to $18. Time: 2 to 3 hours. Difficulty: easy. Smooth each piece from the centre outward to push out air bubbles: the method matches the [DIY decoupage wall art](/blog/diy-decoupage-wall-art) guide.

### 12. Giant map mural from printed tiles

Print one large map across nine A3 sheets, trim the borders, and paste the tiles edge to edge like wallpaper. Cost: $20 to $35. Time: 4 to 5 hours. Difficulty: moderate. Mark a pencil grid with a spirit level first: a 2 mm drift at the top becomes a 2 cm gap below.

## Small Spaces and Kids Map Art

### 13. Mini map gallery in a hallway

Frame three small maps, 20 by 30 cm each, in matching black frames: home town, favourite city and one dream destination. Cost: $12 to $20. Time: 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. Hang them in a row 5 cm apart at eye level, and a narrow hallway gains a gallery that fits its width; the [gallery wall layouts](/blog/gallery-wall-layouts) guide keeps the spacing even.

### 14. Kids' scratch-and-sticker map

Mount a colourful world map at a child's eye level and let them add a sticker for every place they learn about. Cost: $8 to $15. Time: 1 hour. Difficulty: easy. Laminate the map first so stickers peel off cleanly, and use round stickers in one size so the map stays tidy.

### 15. Map art above a desk

Frame a 40 by 60 cm map of your home region above a desk, with a slim shelf below for a plant. Cost: $10 to $16. Time: 1 to 2 hours. Difficulty: easy. A local map above a workspace feels personal without distracting, and needs no wall beyond the desk width. For the height, follow the [above sofa wall decor](/blog/above-sofa-wall-decor) guide.

## Where to Find Maps for Wall Art

The map decides the budget and the look. Free printable maps cost nothing but printing, and vintage-style reproductions cost $5 to $15 for a large sheet. Old atlases from thrift stores cost $2 to $5, though the maps are small and need enlarging. Bought art prints cost $15 to $40 and arrive ready to frame. Check the resolution: a map at 150 dpi looks soft at 60 by 90 cm, while 300 dpi stays crisp. Avoid heavy watermarks and glossy coatings, since glare hides the detail.

| Map source | Cost | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free printable maps | $0 to $3 printing | Budget builds | Low resolution, watermarks |
| Vintage map reproductions | $5 to $15 | Framed statement maps | Colours vary between sellers |
| Old atlases, thrift stores | $2 to $5 | Small framed maps | Small pages, fragile paper |
| Bought art prints | $15 to $40 | Large statement maps | Shipping cost, long delivery |
| Scratch-off travel maps | $15 to $25 | Travel tracking walls | Foil scratches easily |

Buy the frame after the map: printed sizes vary by a centimetre or two, and a frame bought first rarely fits. Print at home on matte photo paper.

## Sizing a Map for Your Wall

Size the map to the furniture below it. Above a sofa, the framed map should span about two thirds of the sofa width: a 180 cm sofa takes a 120 cm map, hung with its centre at about 145 cm from the floor. Above a bed, match the headboard width: a 150 cm headboard takes a 100 cm map. In a hallway, keep maps 30 to 60 cm wide and hang a row of three rather than one large piece. For a statement wall, a map 120 to 150 cm wide anchors the room, but anything wider needs two people. Cut a paper template the size of the frame and tape it to the wall first: it shows the true scale better than a tape measure. The [DIY canvas wall art](/blog/diy-canvas-wall-art) guide uses the same two-thirds rule.

## Hanging Map Wall Art by Weight

Most framed maps are light, but a large wooden map or a glass-fronted frame gets heavy fast. A framed print under 2 kg hangs on one picture hook or two adhesive strips. From 2 to 5 kg, use two picture hooks 30 cm apart with a wire across the back, and find a stud for at least one hook. Over 5 kg, which covers large wooden maps and glass frames above 90 cm wide, use two stud-mounted hooks and a steel wire rated for twice the weight. Never hang a heavy map on a single nail in drywall: it pulls out slowly, and the frame falls when nobody is watching.

| Finished weight | Hardware | Hooks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 2 kg | Picture hook or strips | 1 |
| 2 to 5 kg | Two hooks, wire back | 2 |
| Over 5 kg | Stud hooks, steel wire | 2 |

Renters should favour adhesive strips and foam-backed maps: the piece comes down without a mark. Weigh the finished piece on a bathroom scale before choosing hardware.

## Sealing and Finishing Map Art

Paper maps curl, fade and stain unless sealed. Brush two thin coats of matte decoupage medium or clear acrylic sealer over the map, letting the first coat dry fully before the second. Thin coats dry flat; one thick coat pools, wrinkles the paper and dries cloudy. Seal the back too in a humid room, since moisture enters from behind. A map behind glass still fades in direct sun, so hang it on a shaded wall or use UV-filtering glass. Wooden maps need a different finish: sand the edges lightly, then rub in clear wax or matte varnish, which deepens the wood tones without shine. Never spray sealer indoors without ventilation, and never seal on a damp day: trapped moisture clouds the finish.

## What DIY Map Wall Art Costs

Map wall art stays cheap because the map itself is often free or nearly free. The frame is the real cost: a simple wooden frame for a 60 by 90 cm map costs $8 to $15, a floating frame $12 to $22. Cork board, pins and string add $5 to $10 to a travel map, and a laser-cut wooden map kit is the one big spend at $35 to $60. Printing at home costs $2 to $5, while a print shop charges $5 to $12 for a large sheet.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Printing a low-resolution map is the biggest mistake: a map that looks sharp on a phone turns blurry at 60 by 90 cm, so check the file is 300 dpi before printing. Hanging a map too high is the second: the centre should sit at eye level, about 145 cm from the floor, not near the ceiling. Skipping the sealer lets a paper map curl within weeks, especially in a humid room. Over-pinning turns a travel map into a pincushion, so use one pin per place. Mixing map styles on one wall looks accidental: one vintage map and one modern map clash, while two in the same style look collected. Forgetting to weigh a wooden map before hanging risks a fall, and direct sun fades the colours within a year.

## FAQ

### How much does DIY map wall art cost?

Most projects cost $5 to $30: a framed print $5 to $15, a pin travel map $10 to $20, a scratch-off map $18 to $30. The layered wooden map is the most expensive at $35 to $60.

### Where can I get a map for wall art for free?

Public map archives offer free high-resolution vintage maps to download and print, and old atlases from thrift stores cost $2 to $5. Print at home on matte paper or use a print shop for large sheets, and check the file is 300 dpi first.

### How do you hang a heavy wooden map on the wall?

Use two stud-mounted hooks with a steel wire across the back, rated for twice the map's weight. Weigh the finished piece first, find a stud for at least one hook, and check the level again the next day.

### How do you stop a paper map from curling on the wall?

Brush two thin coats of matte sealer over the front, letting the first coat dry fully before the second. Seal the back too in a humid room, and frame it behind glass if it hangs near a window.

### What size map should I hang above a sofa?

The framed map should span about two thirds of the sofa width: a 180 cm sofa takes a 120 cm map, hung with its centre about 145 cm from the floor. Cut a paper template first and tape it to the wall to check the scale.
